# slepkov2010

**Read here 2026-07-26** (PDF supplied). The external audit's report is
confirmed verbatim.

**The precedent, in their own words.** Fig. 3 caption: "The effect of guided
mode nonuniformity on the ac Stark shift. (a) Radial mode profiles for 10 mW
flat-top and Gaussian-core modes. (b) Simulated ac Stark shifts of a Lorentzian
saturated-absorption peak by flat-top and Gaussian-core modes. ... **The
nonuniformity of the core mode is also seen to broaden and to steepen the line
toward higher frequencies.**" Fig. 4: "The experimental shifts are larger than
those expected for a flat-top mode (dashed line) and are **well fit by the
simulated shift of a nonuniform Gaussian-guided mode** (solid line)."

So they keep the intensity distribution, simulate the asymmetric line it
produces, and fit data with it in preference to a uniform-intensity model.
**"Keep the distribution and fit the asymmetric line in a structured field" is
therefore not new, and this must be cited up front.**

**What is delineated, checked against the text rather than assumed.** Their
signal is **one-photon** saturated absorption, so the weight is I rather than
the I^2 of a degenerate two-photon line -- no triangular law, no closed form.
Their treatment is a numerical simulation of the mode profile, not an
analytic weight with a calculable asymmetry. And they use it to *correct* a
measured shift, with the frequency axis intact; they do not use lineshape to
recover a coefficient when the reference is unusable. The surviving claim here
is that conjunction, not the distribution idea.

**Independently useful for the guided-platform question (PD).** Rb inside a
hollow-core photonic bandgap fibre, room-temperature vapour generated by
light-induced atomic desorption, with **large ac Stark shifts, power
broadening and transit-time broadening present even at nanowatt powers**. Their
guide is far tighter than a kagome: cross-sectional area of order 1e-7 cm^2
(mode radius ~1.8 um, against the ~18 um quoted for the CRYST3 fibre), which is
why 18 mW of desorption light reaches 1e8 mW/cm^2. Note the regime difference
before borrowing numbers -- their atoms are a hot desorbed vapour, not a cold
guided ensemble.
