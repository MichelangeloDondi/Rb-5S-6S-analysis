# epple2014

VERIFIED for page 1 (title, authors, affiliations, abstract). REPORTED beyond
that: the body sections quantifying how the observed level shifts depend on
Rydberg state, core diameter and exposure time are unread: precisely the
part most relevant to a light-shift-distribution reading of this paper.

## What page 1 gives, verbatim

"The exceptionally large polarisability of highly excited Rydberg atoms — six
orders of magnitude higher than ground-state atoms — makes them of great
interest in fields such as" quantum optics, quantum computing, quantum
simulation and metrology (the paper's own list, p. 1, each term there carries
its own inline citation markers, omitted here). "If however they are to be
used routinely in
applications, a major requirement is their integration into technically
feasible, miniaturised devices. Here we show that a Rydberg medium based on
room temperature cæsium vapour can be confined in broadband-guiding
kagomé-style hollow-core photonic crystal fibres. Three-photon spectroscopy
performed on a cæsium-filled fibre detects Rydberg states up to a principal
quantum number of n = 40. Besides small energy level shifts we observe narrow
lines confirming the coherence of the Rydberg excitation. Using different
Rydberg states and core diameters we study the influence of confinement
within the fibre core after different exposure times. Understanding these
effects is essential for the successful future development of novel
applications based on integrated room temperature Rydberg systems."

## Use in this record

This is a guided-geometry cousin of the surface-proximity Rydberg physics
this record already holds extensively for the ONF platform (patrick2025,
raj2026, gokhroo2022, dutta2024/2025, ton2026, piotrowski2026): a Rydberg
atom's enormous polarizability responding to its confining geometry, here a
hollow fibre core instead of an evanescent nanofibre field. Its own stated
"small energy level shifts" in fact shrink with exposure time instead of
growing (against the paper's own Figs. 3-4: the shift is present at the shorter,
two-month exposure and disappears by the longer, four-month one, in both
fibres studied, which the paper attributes to the fibre reaching a more
homogeneous, higher-density caesium fill that screens the background
electric field, not to a growing charge). That is still the same phenomenon
class the ONF holdings (patrick2025, raj2026, and the newly-held naber2016
and hattermann2012 below) document for the open-geometry case, a background
or adsorbate-related electric field near a dielectric surface shifting a
nearby Rydberg line, just screened out by higher density here instead of
accumulating over time. Reading them together supports this repository's own
point that a per-run calibration of the surface-charge term and an
atom-based monitor of adsorbate dynamics on the fibre are assets the guided-
atom programme keeps from this work, a cross-geometry phenomenon, not unique
to the nanofibre evanescent-field case.
