# lee2010

Held. National Cheng-Kung University, Tainan (Tsai/Chui group), the same
laboratory as [lee2012](lee2012.md).

## The system

A two-photon Cs 6S → 8S transition at 822.5 nm in a hot vapour cell,
Doppler-free with a retro-reflected beam and cascade-fluorescence
detection (455.5/459.3 nm, 7P → 6S) on a PMT with lock-in referenced to a
1.15 kHz chopper. A Ti:sapphire laser (Coherent 899-29, ~500 kHz RMS
linewidth, Verdi-pumped) is focused through a confocal pair of $f = 150$
mm lenses to a beam diameter of ~68 µm at the interaction region. Laser
intensity and atomic density are scanned as two independent variables,
and the lineshape is decomposed with a Voigt fit into a homogeneous
Lorentzian and an intensity-dependent Gaussian.

The measurement uses two cells: a reference cell at fixed laser intensity
and a main cell whose intensity is varied with neutral-density filters,
with the light shift taken as the difference between the two cell
centres so the absolute frequency axis is not used directly. Both cells
are temperature-stabilised to better than 0.1 °C, with the body at 65 °C
and the cold finger held 10 °C below it, at 55 °C (given as "65 °C (55 °C
at fingers)" in the Fig. 5 caption), the finger setting the vapour
pressure and the body the thermal velocity. The pressure scan in Fig. 6
is made "by changing the cell temperature," which moves the thermal
velocity together with the vapour pressure, and §1 states the widths are
analysed "by varying the laser intensity or the Cs cell temperature (and
hence the vapor pressure and the mean velocity of atoms)": density and
thermal velocity are not varied independently.

## The numbers

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| light shift, Cs 6S–8S (F=4→F=4) | −7.25 ± 0.45 Hz/(mW/mm²) |
| theoretical comparison | −6.58 Hz/(mW/mm²) |
| prior measurement (their ref [17]) | −6.13 ± 1.25 Hz/(mW/mm²) |
| main-cell power range of the light-shift scan (Fig. 3) | 20 → 120 mW (6 → 33 W/mm²) |
| reference-cell power, held fixed | ~48 mW (13 W/mm²) |
| Lorentzian width, power- and pressure-independent | 1.53 ± 0.08 MHz |
| a typical single fit | $W_L = 1.53 \pm 0.01$, $W_G = 1.05 \pm 0.01$ MHz, $\chi^2 = 0.064$ |
| Gaussian width, abstract's own range over both scans | ~1.0 → ~2.0 MHz |
| vs power (Fig. 5, ~13 → ~90 mW) | ~1.0 → ~2.2 MHz |
| vs vapour pressure (Fig. 6, fixed 40 mW) | up to ~2.4 MHz at ~27 mPa |
| transit-time broadening (their estimate) | 0.48 MHz |
| laser bandwidth (assigned to the Lorentzian) | 1 MHz (2 × 500 kHz, two-photon) |
| second-order Doppler / Zeeman | ~160 Hz / ~500 Hz, negligible |

Three scans are distinct. Fig. 3 is the light shift against main-cell
power over 20–120 mW. Fig. 5 is the Voigt widths against power, with
plotted data to ~90 mW and a largest Gaussian of ~2.2 MHz. Fig. 6 is the
widths against vapour pressure at a fixed 40 mW, where the ~2.4 MHz value
appears. The abstract's Gaussian range, ~1.0–2.0 MHz, spans both scans.

The invariance of $W_L$ against both laser power (20–120 mW) and vapour
pressure (0–27 mPa) is read as an upper bound on the Cs 8S natural
linewidth, 1.53 ± 0.08 MHz, against a theoretical 1.5 MHz and prior
measurements of 1.89(14) and 1.2 MHz.

## Validity

The growing Gaussian width is attributed tentatively to the laser: "the
laser beam is not a plane wave. Therefore, the Gaussian width is possibly
obtained from the spatially inhomogeneous of laser intensities." A second
candidate is kept open throughout: the increase "is caused by the
spacial distribution of laser power intensity or the velocity dependant
collision." The mechanism is not established, only named as one of two
candidates.

A check against the paper's own published numbers argues against that
attribution being the dominant cause. From the stated 68 µm beam diameter
and the measured $-7.25(45)$ Hz/(mW/mm²) coefficient, the peak shift at
120 mW is $S_0 \approx 0.48$ MHz and the shift kernel grows by 0.22 MHz
over 20 → 120 mW. Reading the shift directly off Fig. 3, which needs no
beam diameter or intensity convention, gives a centre shift of ~313 kHz
at 120 mW, hence $S_0 \approx 0.47$ MHz and a kernel of 0.26 MHz FWHM.
Over the same range the Gaussian grows by $\sqrt{2.17^2 - 1.0^2} = 1.93$
MHz FWHM in quadrature, 4–9× larger than the light-shift distribution
their own coefficient permits, the range covering retro-reflection and
axis-convention uncertainty. Only the most favourable combination reaches
2.2×. This is consistent with their own remark that "the fluctuation of
laser pointing or non-Gaussian intensity distribution may be responsible
for the uncertainty in the higher power region," and with keeping
velocity-dependent collisions as an alternative. The check assumes the
coefficient refers to peak rather than average intensity, and that 68 µm
is a centre waist for the confocal two-lens geometry.

A direct test would plot $W_G^2$ against $P^2$: the intercept gives the
power-independent floor ($W_{G0}$: residual Doppler, beam-overlap and
pointing terms, not the 1 MHz laser bandwidth assigned to the Lorentzian
nor the 0.48 MHz transit estimate) and the slope carries $\Delta\alpha$.
This tests only the width of the intensity kernel, not its shape: a
skewed kernel fitted as a Gaussian still yields an effective width, so
agreement would not confirm a Gaussian shape and disagreement would not
rule it out. A shape test needs the fit residuals, which are not
published. If the true kernel is skewed rather than Gaussian, a Voigt fit
could push the misspecification into the Lorentzian component, so
whether the quoted 1.53 MHz bound would survive a correctly shaped kernel
is open.

## Use in this record

For Rb 5S–6S at 993 nm, $\Delta\alpha = 1093.0$ a.u. gives, through
$S_0 = \Delta\alpha I / (2\varepsilon_0 c h) $, 5.12 Hz/(mW/mm²) on the
transition axis or 2.56 on the laser axis. Against Lee's measured
7.25(45) Hz/(mW/mm²) for Cs 6S–8S at 822 nm, the ratio is 0.71
(transition axis) or 0.35 (laser axis): different alkalis, different
$nS \to n'S$ transitions, different wavelengths, and the same order of
magnitude, which is what should happen. This is a magnitude check only,
weaker than the four-significant-figure static comparison in
[quirk2024](quirk2024.md). It does not settle the sign, because Lee's
sign is quoted in a differential-cell convention and the axis convention
is itself unresolved.

The same physics Lee models as a symmetric Gaussian is derived elsewhere
in this analysis as a skewed closed form: the signal-weighted
distribution of AC-Stark shifts for a rate proportional to $I^n$ is
$f(s) \propto \vert s\vert ^{n-1}$, triangular for a two-photon transition ($n=2$)
with skew $g_1 = +0.566$. A Voigt fit has no third moment to represent
that asymmetry. The same concern, that a Voigt fit can misassign a skewed
component into the Lorentzian width, motivates comparing two transit-time
kernel forms, a two-sided exponential and a Gaussian, elsewhere in this
analysis.
