# lee2010

**Read 2026-07-30** from the PDF supplied by the experimenter. National Cheng-Kung
University, Tainan, **Taiwan** (Tsai/Chui group) — the same laboratory as
[lee2012](lee2012.md), whose note already referred to this paper without holding
it. That gap is now closed.

## Why this is the most important note in the prior-art section

Every other precedent this repository holds is *adjacent*:
[wieman1987](wieman1987.md) is one-photon in a beam,
[patterson2018](patterson2018.md) is a static surface potential around cold
atoms, [bruvelis2012](bruvelis2012.md) is a molecular beam. **This one is the
same experiment.** A two-photon $nS \to n'S$ alkali line in a hot vapour cell,
Doppler-free with a retro-reflected beam, cascade-fluorescence detection, with
laser intensity and atomic density scanned as the two independent variables — and
the analysis is a lineshape decomposition into a homogeneous part and an
intensity-dependent part. That is Paper 1's experiment, in Cs, sixteen years
earlier.

## What they did

Cs 6S → 8S at 822.5 nm, Ti:sapphire (Coherent 899-29, ~500 kHz RMS linewidth,
Verdi-pumped), detected on the 455.5/459.3 nm cascade fluorescence from 7P → 6S
with a PMT and lock-in at a 1.15 kHz chopper. Confocal pair of $f = 150$ mm
lenses, **beam diameter ~68 µm** at the interaction region, retro-reflected by a
plane mirror.

Two apparatus choices are worth more to this programme than the results:

1. **A two-cell differential measurement.** A *reference* cell at fixed laser
   intensity and a *main* cell whose intensity is varied by neutral-density
   filters; the light shift is the **difference** between the two cell centres.
   The absolute frequency axis therefore never has to be trusted.
2. **Both cells temperature-stabilised to better than 0.1 °C**, body at 65 °C
   with the cold finger held **10 °C below it**, i.e. at 55 °C (stated as
   "65 °C (55 °C at fingers)" in the Fig. 5 caption). The finger sets the
   saturated vapour pressure, the body the thermal velocity.

*A lesson this note claimed on first writing and has had to withdraw.* The
fixed 10 °C offset does **not** let them vary density and velocity
independently, and they do not: the pressure scan of their Fig. 6 is made "by
changing the cell temperature", which moves the thermal velocity with it, and
§1 says the widths are analysed "by varying the laser intensity or the Cs cell
temperature (and hence the vapor pressure and the mean velocity of atoms)".
Their density–velocity degeneracy is **the same one this campaign has**, not a
solved version of it. The offset is there to keep the coldest point in the cell
away from the windows, which is ordinary vapour-cell practice.

What does transfer is the first item. The M20 frequency history and the
centre-channel Stark bounds were retracted precisely because the absolute axis
moved and was re-zeroed with the scope window; a simultaneous fixed-intensity
reference cell removes that failure mode at the source rather than modelling it
afterwards. That one belongs in the apparatus section of any proposal.

## The results

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| light shift, Cs 6S–8S (F=4→F=4) | **−7.25 ± 0.45 Hz/(mW/mm²)** |
| — theoretical comparison | −6.58 Hz/(mW/mm²) |
| — prior measurement (their ref [17]) | −6.13 ± 1.25 Hz/(mW/mm²) |
| — main-cell power range of the light-shift scan (their Fig. 3) | 20 → 120 mW (6 → 33 W/mm²) |
| — reference-cell power, held fixed | ~48 mW (13 W/mm²) |
| Lorentzian width, power- and pressure-independent | **1.53 ± 0.08 MHz** |
| — a typical single fit | $W_L = 1.53 \pm 0.01$, $W_G = 1.05 \pm 0.01$ MHz, $\chi^2 = 0.064$ |
| Gaussian width, abstract's own range over both scans | ~1.0 → ~2.0 MHz |
| — vs **power** (their Fig. 5, data spanning ~13 → ~90 mW) | ~1.0 → ~2.2 MHz |
| — vs **vapour pressure** (their Fig. 6, at fixed 40 mW) | up to ~2.4 MHz at ~27 mPa |
| transit-time broadening (their estimate) | 0.48 MHz |
| laser bandwidth (they assign it to the **Lorentzian**) | 1 MHz (2 × 500 kHz, two-photon) |
| second-order Doppler / Zeeman | ~160 Hz / ~500 Hz, negligible |

Three distinct scans have to be kept apart, and conflating them was this note's
first error: **Fig. 3** is the light shift against main-cell power over
20–120 mW; **Fig. 5** is the Voigt widths against power, whose plotted data stop
near 90 mW and whose largest Gaussian is ~2.2 MHz; **Fig. 6** is the widths
against vapour pressure at a fixed 40 mW, and that is where ~2.4 MHz comes
from. The paper's own abstract quotes the Gaussian range as ~1.0–2.0 MHz across
both.

Their conclusion from the invariance of $W_L$ against both laser power (20–120
mW) and vapour pressure (0–27 mPa) is that 1.53 ± 0.08 MHz is an **upper bound on
the Cs 8S natural linewidth**, against a theoretical 1.5 MHz and prior
measurements of 1.89(14) and 1.2 MHz.

## The magnitude check it hands the Δα dispute

This repository's pinned `DELTA_ALPHA_AU` = **1093.0 a.u.** for Rb 5S–6S at
993 nm gives, through `stark_shift_S0_mhz`'s own relation
$S_0 = \Delta\alpha I / (2\varepsilon_0 c h)$ (CALCULATED here):

- **5.12 Hz/(mW/mm²)** on the transition axis, or **2.56** on the laser axis.

Against Lee's measured **7.25(45)** for Cs 6S–8S at 822 nm, that is a ratio of
0.71 (transition axis) or 0.35 (laser axis). Two different alkalis, two different
$nS \to n'S$ transitions, two different wavelengths — same order of magnitude is
what should happen, and it does. This is a weaker instrument than
[quirk2024](quirk2024.md), which is a four-significant-figure *dc* target for the
sum-over-states machinery, but it is the first *ac* number in the holdings that
is a measured two-photon light shift of the same structural kind. It is a
**magnitude** check only: it does not settle the sign, because their sign is
quoted in a differential-cell convention and the axis convention is itself
unresolved (see the verify flag). Recorded as a sanity check, not as evidence.

## The disagreement, which is the point

They attribute the growing Gaussian width to the light — **tentatively**, and
the hedge is theirs, not an editorial softening here:

> the laser beam is not a plane wave. Therefore, the Gaussian width is *possibly*
> obtained from the spatially inhomogeneous of laser intensities.

They keep a second candidate alive throughout, writing later that the increase
"is caused by the spacial distribution of laser power intensity **or** the
velocity dependant collision". So they did not settle the mechanism; they named
ours as one of two. That is a weaker precedent than "they attribute it to the
intensity distribution" would imply, and correspondingly a larger opening.

Either way it is the same physical cause [THEORY_NOTE](../THEORY_NOTE.md) §2 treats — and
they model it as a **Gaussian**, which is symmetric, $\kappa_3 = 0$. The
programme's derivation says the signal-weighted distribution of AC-Stark shifts
for a rate $\propto I^{n}$ is $f(s) \propto |s|^{n-1}$, which for the two-photon
case $n = 2$ is **triangular**, with intrinsic skew $g_1 = +0.566$. A Voigt fit
cannot represent that: it has no third moment to spend.

So the prior art on the *closest possible system* saw the effect, named its
cause correctly, and fitted it with a shape that is wrong in a specific and
checkable way. Three consequences, and the first two are claims this programme
can defend:

1. **The novelty is the shape, not the phenomenon.** Paper 1 should concede
   loudly that intensity-inhomogeneity broadening of a two-photon alkali line is
   long known — Lee is the citation — and claim only the closed-form
   *distribution* and its cumulants. That is a narrower claim than the current
   framing and a much more defensible one.
2. **Their $W_G$ is a testable prediction, not just a nuisance — but the test is
   not the obvious one.** This note first argued that an AC-Stark $W_G$ "must be
   linear in power", saw that Fig. 5 is not linear through the origin, and
   treated that as informative. **That reasoning is wrong**, and it is recorded
   rather than deleted because it is an easy mistake to repeat. $W_G$ is the
   *total* Gaussian component of a Voigt fit, and independent Gaussian
   contributions add in quadrature, so even if the AC-Stark part were exactly
   linear in power the observed width would go as
   $W_G(P) = \sqrt{W_{G0}^2 + (aP)^2}$ — never linear, never through the origin.
   Non-linearity in Fig. 5 is therefore evidence of nothing.

   The correct test is on $W_G^2$ against $P^2$, which should be a straight line
   whose intercept is the power-independent floor ($W_{G0}$: residual Doppler,
   beam-overlap and pointing terms — **not** the 1 MHz laser bandwidth, which
   they assign to the Lorentzian, nor obviously their 0.48 MHz transit estimate)
   and whose slope carries $\Delta\alpha$. Even that only probes the *width* of
   the intensity kernel, not its shape: a skewed kernel fitted as a Gaussian
   still yields some effective width, so agreement would not vindicate the
   Gaussian ansatz and disagreement would not by itself indict it. Testing the
   *shape* needs their residuals, which are not published.
   **Nothing here has been computed. Recorded as OPEN.**
3. **A caution about our own $W_L$.** Their headline is that the Lorentzian is
   invariant, which they read as the natural width. If the true intensity
   kernel is skewed rather than Gaussian, a Voigt fit will push the
   misspecification somewhere — and "somewhere" is usually the Lorentzian.
   Whether their 1.53 MHz bound survives a correctly shaped kernel is an open
   question this repository is unusually well placed to ask, and the same
   question applies to the `transit_kind` model-form systematic on
   $\beta_{\rm self}$ (M4c/M8), where the repo already differences the
   two-sided-exponential and Gaussian transit forms for exactly this reason.
