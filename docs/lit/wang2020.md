# wang2020

Held. Verified against the PDF, nine pages, on 2026-09-11.

## What it computes

A Monte Carlo of the trajectories of a cold 85Rb ensemble falling from a
free-space trap into a hollow-core photonic crystal fibre, returning the
temperature, the loading efficiency and the geometry of the ensemble inside
the fibre. The modelled fibre has a mode field radius of 22 microns and the
atoms are "trapped a few millimeters above a piece of 4-cm-long hollow-core
photonic crystal" fibre. The abstract states the mechanism verbatim: "the
number of cold atoms in the fiber is mainly determined by the loading process
from free space to waveguide confinement".

## The comparison table, which is the useful part

The paper tabulates its own simulation beside two experiments:

| | this work | second column | third column |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOT temperature | 10 uK | 5 uK | 40 uK |
| dipole beam power | 0.5 W (313 uK deep) | 1 W (14 mK deep) | 25 mW (10 mK deep) |
| dipole wavelength | 821 nm | 797.25 nm | 802 nm |
| fibre length | 4 cm | 10 cm | 3 cm |
| core diameter | 64 um | 45 um | 7 um |
| efficiency | 0.19 per cent (simulation) | 3.2 per cent (experiment) | 3 per cent (experiment) |

## Why this record needed it

`results/platform_twins.csv`'s `hcpcf_cold` row assumes a density and derives
an atom count from it. Nothing in this repository converted a trap's atom
number into a guided one, so that density was a design figure with no route to
a measurement. A loading efficiency of a fifth of a per cent to three per cent
is that route, and it is small: a hundred million atoms in a trap become two
hundred thousand to three million in the fibre before any further loss.

The paper also names the mechanism that costs the most, which is the mismatch
between the free-space and guided trap depths and extensions, and reports that
the loading efficiency falls as the fraction of the fundamental mode falls,
since a mixed mode's interference pattern heats the sample.

## What it does not license

It is a simulation, and its own efficiency is the smallest of the three
numbers in its table. **The two experiments in that table are not
interchangeable, and which one a row may cite depends on the row's geometry.**
The 3.2 per cent is a 45-micron core over ten centimetres, the geometry closest
to this record's warm guided row. The 3 per cent is a 7-micron core over three
centimetres from a 40-microkelvin trap, and that is the one a *cold*, narrow,
centimetre-scale row may lean on. The simulation's own 0.19 per cent is the
pessimistic end of both and is a different fibre again, 64 microns over four
centimetres.
