# zhang2026

Held. Read against the PDF, eight pages, 2026-09-10.

## The result

From the abstract, verbatim: "Results show that replacing a tightly focused
beam with a large collimated one reduced the laser power-induced frequency
shift by two orders of magnitude." And immediately after: "Furthermore,
increasing the vapor temperature enhances collision-induced fre- quency
shifts, adversely affecting long-term stability."

## Why it matters here, and it is not as a competitor

The two-photon clock community's problem is that the light shift is a
systematic. Their answer is geometric: a large collimated beam makes the
intensity nearly uniform over the interrogated volume, so the shift and its
spread both collapse. Two orders of magnitude is the measured size of that.

**This record's programme is the same physics read the other way.** The shift
is the signal, its DISTRIBUTION is the observable, and the levers that suppress
it are the levers that must be avoided. The 2025 archive already sits where
they would not put a clock, at a 64 um waist, and the campaign proposes to go
tighter still.

So this paper is the quantitative statement of what the opposite design costs
and buys, and it is the cleanest external evidence that the two programmes are
solving different problems with one apparatus. It also prices the temperature
axis in their direction: they lose long-term stability to collisions where this
record spends temperature to raise the density and the signal.

## The connection to ayachitula2024

[ayachitula2024](ayachitula2024.md) is this result already applied, on the very
transition this record measures: unfocused beams at a 2.5 mm intensity FWHM,
an AC Stark shift calculated at -0.2 kHz, and the differential entry set to
zero in the error budget. Zhang give the design rule. Ayachitula show what it
buys at 993 nm. Neither is available to a programme that wants the shift.
