# wang2025

**VERIFIED, 2026-08-02**, full text read.

**The system.** Rb 5S(1/2) to 7S(1/2) two-photon excitation with a single
760 nm laser, read out on five simultaneous fluorescence channels (780, 741,
795, 728, and about 420 nm) through a filter array into one PMT. The
branching ratios normalised to 780 nm are 1 : 0.64 : 0.52 : 0.35 : 0.20, so a
single 420 nm channel alone captures only a fraction of the available signal.
The cell is natural-abundance Rb with no buffer gas, scanned from 100 to
170 C for the broadening measurement. Density is computed from temperature
rather than measured.

**The numbers.**

| quantity | value | convention |
|---|---|---|
| self-broadening slope | 0.32 ± 0.01 MHz/mTorr | **not stated** |
| zero-pressure intercept | 1.08 ± 0.02 MHz | not stated |
| 7S natural width, quoted for comparison | ~0.9 MHz | not stated |
| collisional shift | not reported | — |

The paper argues that 5S-7S could serve as the basis for an optical
frequency standard, the same application class as the 5S-6S line (see
[BIG_PICTURE](../BIG_PICTURE.md) §1).

**On 5S-6S.** Cited once, as [nieddu2019](nieddu2019.md), to support a
statement about circular-polarization selection rules cancelling two-photon
amplitudes. The paper reports no 5S-6S linewidth, broadening, shift, or
comparison.

**Use in this record.** At 423 K the same conversion used for
[cao2025](cao2025.md) gives 0.32 MHz/mTorr = 0.014 MHz per 1e12 cm^-3, a
factor 8 above the 5D(3/2) entry from the same group. The missing
convention could move that factor by up to two. The 5S-6S per-peak fitted
central values (0.026 to 0.045 MHz per 1e12 cm^-3) are 1.8 to 3.2 times the
7S entry, and the 95% bound (0.21 to 0.43) is 15 to 31 times above it. The
fitted 5S-6S centrals sit above the 7S value despite 6S being the more
compact state, consistent with an unresolved instrumental floor rather than
resolved collisions. The fitted width rises only 1.47 times across a
52.5-fold density span (the density-lever test).
