# gilles1964

Held, two pages, read in full against the PDF on 2026-09-14. The file is
the book-review section of JACS 86 (20 December 1964). The subject is the review of Nesmeyanov on pp. 5702 to 5703, the fourth on
the page.

## What it covers

A review of A. N. Nesmeyanov, Vapor Pressure of the Chemical Elements,
edited by Robert Gary, Elsevier 1963, 462 pages (p. 5702). The reviewer
is a vaporisation thermodynamicist (University of Kansas).

## The claims, with pages

1. The author is misidentified on the title page: the book is by Andrey N.
   Nesmeyanov of Moscow State University, whose field is vaporisation
   studies, and not by the organometallic chemist Aleksandr N. Nesmeyanov
   (p. 5702).
2. The book gives, per element, a literature survey and a Table II of
   computed coefficients for a four-term vapour-pressure equation, which
   the reviewer describes as of use perhaps to engineers (p. 5702). That
   four-term equation is the form `rb5s6s/density.py` carries with the
   coefficients 15.88253, 4529.635, 0.00058663 and 2.99138, and the form
   [gallagher1973](gallagher1973.md) Table II and
   [siddons2008](siddons2008.md) Appendix A quote.
3. The reviewer's central objection (pp. 5702 to 5703): nowhere does the
   book give a critical discussion of all the work on an element, a
   choice of a value, and an estimate of the reliability of the chosen
   value. In that regard it is a disappointment.
4. On use (p. 5703): the tabulated results should be used as they appear
   only by one who wishes numbers regardless of their reliability, and the
   bibliography contains many errors.
5. Specific errors named: eq. 63 on the book's p. 73 lacking a factor,
   and on its p. 68 a wrong quantity plotted against 1/T (p. 5702).
6. The balance the reviewer strikes (p. 5703): the book can be used in
   three ways, as an excellent source on vapour-pressure methods for a
   newcomer, as a very good bibliography, and, terribly, by taking its
   tables as they stand.

## Validity

A contemporary expert's reading of the source. It is not a measurement
and it does not say the rubidium coefficients are wrong. It says the
source attaches no reliability to them.

## What this record takes from it

The provenance of the record's headline density law. The four-term
coefficients descend from this book's Table II, a compilation whose
reviewer states it carries no reliability estimate. The 24 per cent
`N_SCALE_FRAC_SYST` in `density.py` is therefore the record's own
envelope, drawn from the spread between correlations, and not a figure
the source supplies. The only absolute measurement in the held set that
tests those coefficients is [gallagher1973](gallagher1973.md), six
points at 326 to 339 K, which the book predates and which its author
fit, in Gallagher's words, probably by coincidence. The wiki page on
density cites the book as A. N. Nesmeyanov (Elsevier, 1963), which is
correct on either reading of the initials.

## Conflicts with the record

None numerical. Gallagher's reference 22 spells the author Nesemeyanov. That is a misprint there, not a different author.
