seen by the atoms are unread.

## What page 1 gives, verbatim

"We describe the loading of laser-cooled rubidium atoms into a single-mode
hollow-core photonic-crystal fiber. Inside the fiber, the atoms are confined
by a far-detuned optical trap and probed by a weak resonant beam. We describe
different loading methods and compare their trade-offs in terms of
implementation complexity and atom-loading efficiency. The most efficient
procedure results in loading of ~30,000 rubidium atoms, which creates a
medium with optical depth ~180 inside the fiber. Compared to our earlier
study [1] this represents a six-fold increase in maximum achieved
optical depth in this system."

## Use in this record

This is the earliest of the three HCPCF papers the search found beyond the
Taiwan HCPCF group (item 4), and the one closest to this record's own species
(Rb) and platform family (a dipole-trapped, guided ensemble probed
optically). It is also a precedent that group's own HCPCF thread cites,
though only narrowly: xin2018 (the Taiwan HCPCF group's first HCPCF atom
interferometer, `private/reviews/LAN_GROUP_DIGEST.md` §3 item 5) cites this
paper exactly once, for its ballistic-expansion method of extracting atom
temperature from an OD-versus-release-time curve, not for a shared loading
strategy: xin2018's own Materials and Methods `Atom loading` subsection
describes its dipole-force-from-divergent-beam loading with no citation at
all (checked 2026-09-22 against the held Xin et al. 2018 PDF). It gives this
record an independent measurement, from outside that group's own lineage, of
the guided ensemble's optical depth and trap geometry to compare against when
discussing what the guiding beam's intensity distribution does to an atom
held inside a hollow core, one physical rung below the differential-AC-Stark-
shift-as-a-distribution framing this record's own programme brings.
