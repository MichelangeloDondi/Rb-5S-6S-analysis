VERIFIED against the held preprint (scope in `verify_flags`).

## The experiment

A 10 cm hollow-core photonic crystal fibre of Kagome type, 60 um core and about 42 um mode field diameter, carries two counter-propagating 805 nm lattice beams and the 780 and 480 nm EIT beams, all coupled into its fundamental mode with over 90 per cent efficiency. About 5 x 10^4 atoms from a MOT 5 mm before the tip are moved in by detuning the lattice beams, and probed with 2 us pulses in a sequence repeated up to 1000 times, with the lattice off during each pulse (their Fig. 1). Inside the fibre the atoms live 120 to 180 ms at about 500 uK.

## What it finds

Rydberg EIT through 29S1/2 is seen inside the fibre, with about 40 per cent peak transmission against 60 outside and the same fitted control Rabi frequency, about 2 pi x 9.5 to 9.9 MHz (Fig. 2). On the fibre's effect, verbatim: "To extract the influence of the fiber on the lifetime of the Rydberg state, we compare the decay rates 2π (2.6 ± 0.75) MHz inside and 2π (0.9 ± 0.4) MHz outside the fiber." The relative shift, 2.2 +- 1 MHz, gives with the 29S polarizability, verbatim: "this shift would correspond to an electric field of (2 ± 1.3) V/cm." The authors compare it with adatom fields on dielectric surfaces and add that it stays constant between days, which they take to suggest the adatom coverage has saturated. A two-pulse sequence separates coherent EIT from Rydberg-induced loss: inside the fibre the loss-dominated regime begins after about 300 pulses, earlier than outside, while the EIT decay rates themselves are not changed by the fibre (Figs. 3 and 4). Close to the tip, verbatim: "Only at positions very close to the fiber tip, we see such a strong influence that we have not been able to produce a clear EIT signal, possibly due to large inhomogeneous electric fields due to adatoms as also observed in numerous other experiments [43–49]."

## Use in this record

The thesis's proposals chapter treats the hollow-core fibre beside the nanofibre, and the owner's programme names atoms inside hollow-core fibres as one of the systems a joint analysis should model better. This is the measured baseline for Rydberg atoms at tens of micrometres from a silica wall inside a fibre: a field of order volts per centimetre that is steady from day to day, a dephasing of a few megahertz, and a failure near the entrance where the walls' field is inhomogeneous. The distance from the wall, about 30 um at the core's centre, is two orders larger than a nanofibre's evanescent region, so it bounds nothing for the nanofibre directly.
