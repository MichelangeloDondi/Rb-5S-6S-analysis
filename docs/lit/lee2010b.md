## What it says, in its own terms

The paper isolates polarization and pressure effects in the caesium 6S-8S two-photon transition at 822.5 nm, using a single-frequency Ti:sapphire ring-cavity laser split between a reference cell (fixed intensity) and a main cell (varied intensity), with quarter-wave plates giving either linear or circular polarization in the main cell. Two-photon absorption is read out through 7P-6S cascade fluorescence at 455.5/459.3 nm on a photomultiplier tube, lock-in referenced to an optical chopper.

Its central methodological argument is that a Lorentzian-only fit cannot separate the natural linewidth from other broadening mechanisms. Under a weak-field approximation the natural linewidth is expected to be Lorentzian, and the standard theory of power broadening of a homogeneously broadened line predicts a half-width growing as the unsaturated half-width times the square root of one plus the saturation parameter S0. Fitting the circularly polarized data to that theory alone gives a small S0 of about 0.02, consistent with the weak-field regime, but the same fit applied to the linearly polarized data gives S0 of about 0.95, which the authors argue cannot be reconciled with a fixed excited-state lifetime, so some other broadening mechanism must be present. They resolve this by fitting a Voigt profile instead (a Lorentzian convolved with a Gaussian), which recovers a Lorentzian width pinned at the theoretical natural linewidth regardless of polarization, laser power or vapour pressure, and assigns the excess broadening to a separate Gaussian component.

That Gaussian component behaves differently for the two polarizations. Pumped circularly, the total linewidth and its Gaussian part stay close to constant (about 2 MHz total, about 1.1 MHz Gaussian) as laser power increases. Pumped linearly, the Gaussian broadens visibly with both laser power and vapour pressure. The paper attributes the power dependence to "the spatial inhomogeneity of the laser intensities and the misalignment" (p. 5), and attributes the vapour-pressure dependence to collision broadening, concluding that circular polarization gives a narrower line, a smaller light shift, and less first-order Doppler background than linear polarization, all read off the same apparatus and the same fit family.

The light shift and pressure shift are both measured on a two-cell differential design, where the reported frequency is the difference between the main- and reference-cell line centres rather than an absolute frequency. The light shift for each polarization is compared against an unspecified theoretical calculation and against each other. The pressure shift is obtained by tuning the main-cell temperature (and hence its vapour pressure) while holding the reference cell fixed.

## What it is worth here

This is a same-technique precedent for the vapour-cell 5S-6S programme: Doppler-free two-photon absorption of an alkali nS-n'S transition in a hot cell, a Voigt decomposition used to separate a natural-linewidth Lorentzian from a power/pressure-dependent Gaussian, and a two-cell differential design for the light shift and pressure shift. Its light-shift and pressure-shift coefficients, and its explicit pressure-broadening slope (52 kHz mPa^-1, attributed to collisions), are a directly comparable external anchor for the same class of measurement on Rb 5S-6S, in the same spirit as the sibling paper lee2010 already serves. Its own account of the growing Gaussian component (spatial laser-intensity inhomogeneity, with collision broadening kept open as an alternative) is the same kind of ambiguity this record's own AC-Stark-shift-distribution work addresses with a closed-form, non-Gaussian lineshape rather than a phenomenological Voigt fit, so the paper is as useful as a contrast case as it is as a numerical anchor.
