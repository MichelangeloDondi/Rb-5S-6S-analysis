## The system and the method

Sodium vapor, the 3S to 4D two-photon transition (Fig. 1's own line is
specifically 3S(F=2) -> 4D_5/2), driven by two independently tunable cw dye
lasers at ~589 nm (nu_2, tuned near the 3P_3/2 or 3P_1/2 intermediate state)
and ~569 nm (nu_1), propagating through the vapor in **opposite directions**,
the Doppler-free two-photon geometry of the authors' own preceding Letter
(footnote 1: J. E. Bjorkholm and P. F. Liao, Phys. Rev. Lett. **33**, 128
(1974)), itself citing the original technique of Vasilenko, Chebotaev and
Shishaev, Pis'ma Zh. Eksp. Teor. Fiz. **12**, 161 (1970) [JETP Lett. **12**,
113 (1970)] (footnote 2). nu_2 is held fixed while nu_1 is swept through the
resonance. The two-photon signal is read out via 4P-3S fluorescence at 330 nm
from 4D decay, displayed directly on an oscilloscope (p. 1-2). Counter-
propagation nearly cancels the Doppler effect. The residual Doppler broadening
is **62 MHz**, cited to the authors' own footnote 7, J. E. Bjorkholm and
P. F. Liao, *to be published* (p. 2). That *to be published* reference is very
likely the fuller Doppler-averaged two-photon lineshape treatment now held in
this record as `bjorkholm1976` (same two authors in reversed order, the
identical Na 3S-4D system, published the following year in Phys. Rev. A) --
plausible from the bibliographic match, but not independently confirmed here
by cross-reading the `bjorkholm1976` PDF for this specific citation.

## The shift formula, Eq. (1), page 1

Page 1 states that such optically induced level shifts are known as "light
shifts" or as the "ac Stark effect" (footnote 3: A. M. Bonch-Bruevich and
V. A. Khodovoi, Usp. Fiz. Nauk **93**, 71 (1967) [Sov. Phys. Usp. **10**, 637
(1968): the translated-journal volume number is reconstructed from a
garbled `1£` in the scan. The Usp. Fiz. Nauk volume 93 and year 1967 are
clearly legible], cited by the paper for a comprehensive review of ac Stark
effect theory and experiments). The shift formula itself, quoted and checked
against the rendered page (symbol-heavy):

"The energy shift of an atomic level n that is induced by the optical field
$\tilde{\mathcal{E}} = \mathrm{Re} \tilde{\mathcal{E}}_0 e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$
is given by perturbation theory as

$$\Delta E_n = h \delta\nu = \frac14 \sum_m \left\lbrace
\frac{|\vec{P}_{mn}\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_0|^2}{E_n - E_m - \hbar\omega} +
\frac{|\vec{P}_{mn}\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_0|^2}{E_n - E_m + \hbar\omega}
\right\rbrace.$$ (1)

The summation is taken over all unperturbed atomic states $|m\rangle$ having
energy $E_m$, and $\vec{P}_{mn}$ is the electric dipole matrix element between
states $m$ and $n$. The energy-level shifts are due to virtual transitions
between atomic levels caused by nonresonant light. Because two-photon
transitions generally involve virtual transitions to an intermediate atomic
state, such level shifts are intrinsic to these two-photon processes."
(p. 1, Eq. 1 and surrounding text)

This is the standard second-order-perturbation-theory light shift: a sum over
intermediate states $m$ with two resonance denominators $E_n - E_m \mp \hbar\omega$,
one per photon-absorption/emission sense. It is the same
physical content, a dynamic polarizability built from off-resonant virtual
transitions to a sum of intermediate states, as the sum-over-states
construction `rb5s6s/polarizability.py` uses for this record's own
$\Delta\alpha_{6S}$ (`docs/methods/03_the_ac_stark_ramp.md`, section *Matrix
elements of the sum and their divergence*), though this record's object is
the frequency-dependent *difference* between two levels' polarizabilities
($\Delta\alpha = \alpha_{6S}-\alpha_{5S}$) built from published matrix
elements, not a single level's shift evaluated from Eq. (1) with matrix
elements computed in-paper.

## What was measured, with page references

- Shifts of **>1 GHz** for both the 3S ground state and the 4D excited state
  of sodium (p. 1).
- Beam waists: the 589-nm ($\nu_2$) beam focused to **18 µm**, the 569-nm
  ($\nu_1$) beam to **4.5 µm**. Up to **40 mW** available in either beam
  (p. 2).
- Fig. 1(a): a **~745 MHz** shift of the 3S level from **26 mW** of 589-nm
  light, with the 4D shift made negligible by attenuating the 569-nm beam
  (p. 2).
- Fig. 1(b): the shift of the 4D level from **~30 mW** of 569-nm light,
  appearing not as a clean shift but as severe line broadening and distortion
  (p. 2).
- The 589-nm laser ran two axial modes **1.77 GHz** apart, assumed equal in
  amplitude and independently contributing to the shift. The 569-nm laser ran
  single-mode (p. 2).
- Fig. 2: the 3S(F=2) shift measured against both laser intensity (solid
  circles at a 589-nm intensity of **2.15 kW/cm²**) and detuning
  $\Delta\nu$ from the 3P_3/2 resonance (the two 589-nm modes tuned **4.0 and
  5.77 GHz** off that resonance). The theory curves (Eq. 1, no adjustable
  parameters, oscillator strength of the 3P_3/2-3S transition taken as
  **0.65** from J. K. Link, J. Opt. Soc. Amer. **56**, 1195 (1966), footnote
  8) agree with experiment except within $|\Delta\nu| \lt 4$ GHz, where "the
  shifts were no longer linearly dependent on light intensity" (p. 3).
- Matrix-element ratio test: the 3S shift via 3P_1/2 near-resonance measured
  at half the shift via 3P_3/2 near-resonance, "experimentally confirmed"
  (p. 3). Zero shift on 3S->4D_5/2 via the 569-nm/3P_1/2 path, because the
  3P_1/2-4D_5/2 dipole matrix element is zero, while 3S->4D_3/2 showed strong
  shifts under the same tuning (p. 3).
- Saturation ruled out directly: with the 589-nm beam already attenuated into
  the regime where the two-photon signal is linear in the product of the two
  intensities, a further 10x attenuation of the 589-nm beam left the shift
  unchanged. A 10x attenuation of the 569-nm beam "essentially eliminated the
  shifts" (p. 3-4).
- Proposed applications: an intensity-dependence method for measuring
  transition dipole moments, and a "kind of Stark spectroscopy" from the
  differential hyperfine shifts of the 4D level, masked in vapor by the
  broadening mechanisms discussed but proposed as observable in an atomic-beam
  experiment (p. 4).

## The Doppler/lineshape treatment, and what it does not cover

Page 2-3 gives a Voigt-profile, single-valued-shift treatment, valid for
$\Delta\nu \equiv (E_n-E_m+\hbar\omega)/h$ large compared with the Doppler
linewidth and with $\delta\nu$: the paper states that the two-photon line has
a Voigt profile, shifted by the amount $\delta\nu_0$ (the level shift of the
zero-velocity group), with a linewidth proportional to
$(\nu_1-\nu_2) + (\nu_2/\Delta\nu)\delta\nu_0$ (shift caused by light at $\nu_2$) (p. 2-3) --
one number, $\delta\nu_0$, per velocity-averaged line, not a distribution of
shifts. This holds only while
$\Delta\nu$ exceeds "twice the Doppler width of the 3S-3P transition (~1.7
GHz)" (p. 3). Below that the paper states, without working the case out, that
"the line shape becomes intensity dependent" and that "we have not... carried
out detailed experiments in this regime."

## Use in this record

This is the earliest item in what the task names the two-photon light-shift
lineage: predating `delone1980` (1980, the general theory of a lineshape as
a map of a shift or field-amplitude distribution) and `camparo1992` (1992,
the ac-Stark shift of a two-photon transition in a stochastic field) by five
and seventeen years respectively, and its own companion `bjorkholm1976`
(1976, the Doppler-averaged two-photon lineshape theory, which explicitly
excludes ac-Stark effects "by construction," per that note's own summary) by
one year. It is the first direct experimental demonstration that a light
shift is intrinsic to a resonantly enhanced two-photon process specifically,
distinct from the microwave/rf double-resonance shifts already known from
Happer's 1971 review (footnote 4) and from the one-photon optical shifts that
had until then required high-power sources (footnote 5).

This paper is not cited anywhere in this record's own derivation of the ramp
law, which instead anchors to `delone1980`'s general theory (per that note's
own *Use in this record*). Nothing here is a claim about a citation chain.
The connection substantiated below is phenomenological, per the task's own
framing of this paper as *ancestral to this record's own AC-Stark ramp
model*: not the paper's quantitative Doppler/Voigt analysis, which carries a
single velocity-averaged shift $\delta\nu_0$ instead of a distribution, but
its qualitative, explicitly reported *spatial* effect, the same physical
mechanism, reported here for the first time in this record's held
literature. Quoted in full because it is the load-bearing passage (p. 2,
checked against the rendered page):

"Since the level shift is proportional to the light intensity, spatial
nonuniformities of the intensity within the region of observation cause the
two-photon absorption lines to be broadened as well as shifted, and the
broadening can obscure the shift. This problem was overcome by focusing one
laser beam ($\nu_1$) to a spot diameter much smaller than the other
($\nu_2$). Then by appropriately imaging only the confocal region of the
tightly focused beam ($\nu_1$) on the detector, only transitions which occur
in a region over which the $\nu_2$ light intensity is nearly constant were
monitored. ... In this way shifts of the 3S ground state (caused by the
weakly focused beam) were clearly observed whereas shifts of the 4D levels
(caused by the strongly focused beam) appeared as a severe asymmetric
broadening of the two-photon lines."

Liao and Bjorkholm treat that spatial effect as a nuisance to be engineered
away: they deliberately image only a nearly-uniform-intensity confocal
region of the other beam so as to isolate a single, clean shift value, and
the "severe asymmetric broadening" of Fig. 1(b) is exactly what they get when
they do not apply that restriction to the tightly focused 569-nm beam. That
reported qualitative effect, an ensemble of atoms at different points in a
focused beam sampling different ac-Stark shifts, producing an asymmetric line,
is the same physical mechanism this record derives in closed form as the
triangular *ramp law*, $f(s) \propto s^{n-1}$ on $[0, S_0]$ for a Gaussian
beam (`docs/methods/03_the_ac_stark_ramp.md`, section *2.6 AC-Stark shift:
derivation of the triangular 'ramp law'*), with analytic cumulants (mean
$+\tfrac23 S_0$, standardized skew $-0.566$ at $n=2$) in place of the
qualitative "severe asymmetric broadening" that this 1975 paper reports and
routes around with an aperture. Where Liao and Bjorkholm's confocal imaging
suppresses the spatial-inhomogeneity-driven asymmetry so it can be ignored,
this record's ramp law instead treats the identical phenomenon as the primary
signal, closes its distribution for the Gaussian-beam case, and reads its
cumulants as an observable channel.

No number from this paper is carried numerically into this record's code. It
is cited for priority and for its qualitative anticipation of the
spatial-shift-distribution mechanism, not for a reusable quantity.
