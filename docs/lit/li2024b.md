# li2024b

VERIFIED, 2026-08-02. Full text read.

Not to be confused with [li2024](li2024.md), a different paper on the same 778 nm line and light-shift problem, by Li Dou and colleagues (Wuhan), which compensates light shift and cell-temperature shift together through a resonance offset lock. This paper, by Li Ye and colleagues, cancels the light shift alone by interrogating two cells at two powers.

## The method

Two Rb vapor cells are fed from the same 1556 nm laser doubled to 778 nm and run at two different fixed powers, each generating its own error signal from lock-in demodulated 420 nm fluorescence. The two signals are combined in real time as S = S1 + beta S2, with beta computed from four measured per-system constants, so that the combined zero crossing sits where the primary system's light shift vanishes. The authors describe this as spatial interrogation, in contrast with the earlier power-modulation (temporal) approach.

## The numbers

The absolute light shift and the light-power sensitivity each fall by a factor of ten. The residual central offset is about 411 Hz, and a static test gives 235 Hz with a standard deviation of 55 Hz. The pre-correction light-shift coefficients are -331 ± 9 and -291 ± 8 Hz/mW.

The Allan deviation in Fig. 5(c) runs from about 1e-12 to 1e-14 over 1 to 100 s, characterized only qualitatively as an order-of-magnitude improvement, not given in tau form. The tau-scaled numbers quoted in the introduction, 3e-13 and 1.8e-13 per root tau, belong to other groups' systems, not this one.

## The model

The light shift is modeled as a scalar proportional to interrogation power, with power read by a single photodiode per beam. Neither the model nor the text refers to a beam profile, an intensity distribution, a waist, a Gaussian, or an inhomogeneity. What the two-cell combination nulls is the mean shift and its first-order sensitivity to changes in that scalar power.
