# saha2010

**Read 2026-07-29** (arXiv v1, held). Cornell, Gaeta group — the same lineage
as [Slepkov 2010](slepkov2010.md), one step further: not one-photon saturated
absorption in the hollow core but **degenerate Doppler-free two-photon
absorption**, on 5S₁/₂ → 5D₅/₂ at 778.1 nm, with the 420 nm 6P₃/₂ cascade as
the monitor.

**Why it matters here.** Two guided geometries have been discussed for this
programme: the optical-nanofibre case, and a hollow-core 778 nm case.
**Neither is scheduled, and the hollow-core one is speculative**: the Bologna
fibre is headed for 780 nm EIT cooling, and a
two-photon line in it is an idea for a possible separate paper. This paper is
the existence proof for the hollow-core half: 1% absorption of the pump at
**1 mW total power** in-fibre, i.e. the tight guided mode delivers a
two-photon signal at powers three orders below free-space practice. The
feasibility argument does not have to be made from scratch, it has been done
on the sister line, in the sister geometry.

**Which fibre the 18 µm belongs to.** Not this one. Neither "Bologna" nor any
18 µm figure appears in this paper, and **Saha's own fibre is a
9-cm-long, 6 µm-diameter hollow-core PBGF, Crystal Fiber AIR-6-800**, guiding
750–810 nm. The 18 µm is instead the mode field of the
**CRYST³** hollow-core fibre (EU H2020 FET-Open GA 964531), an apparatus
operating at Bologna since February 2023. Its source is a held
master's thesis, [nasoni2026](nasoni2026.md), which gives it as an
**injection beam waist**, i.e. a **RADIUS**, so 3.3 MHz and not 6.6. And it is
**measured**, not merely a target: the ideal thin-lens value is
$w_{\rm inj} \simeq 13.6 \pm 0.1$ µm, the text says "the real waist at the
injection is expected to be closer to the target value of approximately
~18 µm", and the characterised profile is
$w_{\rm inj\text{-}x} = 17.1 \pm 0.7$ µm, $w_{\rm inj\text{-}y} = 19.3 \pm 0.4$ µm,
slightly elliptical, from AOM distortion, and "sufficiently close to the
target waist".

**But it is the wrong beam, and that is the caveat that matters.** This is the
**1064 nm optical-dipole-trap** beam — the thesis builds an ODT by coupling
1064 nm into the HCPCF — *not* a two-photon probe. A hollow-core guided mode is
set by the fibre at the wavelength in question, so the 778 nm probe mode is a
different number, and treating 18 µm as the transit waist for a two-photon
line is an **assumption, flagged here, not a measurement of the relevant mode**.
Taken at face value the measured pair gives **3.1–3.4 MHz** transit at 100 °C
(CALCULATED), against 4.3 for the 13.6 µm ideal and 0.93 for the free-space
adopted $w_0 = 64$ µm. The source is an unpublished thesis, so the number is
not independently published and **no published claim should rest on it as it
stands**.

**What it does not do.** No lineshape decomposition, no light-shift treatment
— absorption depth is the observable, not the line shape. The guided-mode
intensity distribution that [Slepkov 2010](slepkov2010.md) shows broadening
and steepening the one-photon line is present here too and unmodelled, which
is precisely the gap the ramp formalism would fill in this geometry: for I²
signals the mode non-uniformity is not a nuisance but a closed-form weight.

Same group and era as Slepkov; cite the pair together when delineating the
guided-mode prior art — Slepkov for keeping the distribution (one-photon),
Saha for the two-photon feasibility (distribution collapsed).
