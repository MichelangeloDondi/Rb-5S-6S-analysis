# martin2019

The 5S-5D differential-polarizability method Orson followed for our 5S-6S Delta\_alpha.

**Two sign anomalies inside the paper itself, verified from the held PDF
2026-07-29** (flagged by the external audit from snippets; both confirmed):
the abstract and Table II quote the ac-Stark coefficient as +2.30(4)e-13 per
mW/mm^2, while Fig. 5's caption reports the measured value as **-2.5(2)e-13**
-- same quantity, opposite printed sign. A paper central to the 778 nm shift
budget carries an internal sign inconsistency, which is worth remembering when
its conventions are borrowed: this is the same literature in which the
Delta-alpha(993) sign dispute lives, and printed signs in it are demonstrably
not reliable enough to settle that dispute by citation alone.

**Eq. (2) verified from the typeset PDF, 2026-07-29.** It is printed as
alpha(omega,J) = **-** (2/3(2J+1)) sum_J' omega_J'J |<J|d|J'>|^2 / (omega^2_J'J
- omega^2), and Eq. (21) repeats the leading minus. As printed, a ground state
below resonance would be NEGATIVELY polarizable, which is wrong -- alpha_5S(DC)
is measured at +318.79(1.42). Whether the paper's tabulated values follow the
printed equation or the standard one is not determinable from the PDF alone.
Recorded because Orson 2021 calculated "in a manner similar to" this work.
