## What it says, in its own terms

The paper evaluates rubidium vapour density, [Rb], inside a high-throughput continuous-flow
129Xe-Rb spin-exchange optical pumping (SEOP) polarizer, the kind of system used to hyperpolarize
129Xe gas for lung and other organ MRI. Density is measured two ways: near-infrared absorption on
the D1 (5^2S1/2 to 5^2P1/2) and D2 (5^2S1/2 to 5^2P3/2) lines, and violet absorption on the
5^2S1/2 to 6^2P1/2/6^2P3/2 lines. Comparing the two lets the paper quantify where each
transition's absorption departs from the Beer-Lambert law at high optical depth: D2 absorption
deviates above [Rb]_D2 = 4x10^17 m^-3, while 6P3/2 (violet) absorption holds to the law up to a
much higher density, (4.16 +- 0.01)x10^19 m^-3, before it too departs.

The measured [Rb] is used to estimate a 129Xe-Rb spin-exchange cross section of
gamma' = (1.2 +- 0.1) x 10^-21 m^3 s^-1, stated to be consistent with literature values for this
cross section.

The paper reports significant spatial heterogeneity in [Rb] for a cell holding 1 g of rubidium
metal localized at the back of the cell, improved homogeneity when the source rubidium is spread
over a larger surface area or a rubidium presaturator is used, and a measured [Rb] that is
consistently lower than the value predicted by saturated rubidium vapour density curves at the
same cell temperature.

## What it is worth here

The transition (violet 5S-6P and the D lines) and the physical process (spin-exchange optical
pumping of 129Xe for MRI) are both different from a rubidium 5S-6S two-photon vapour-cell
programme, so this paper is not a source of numbers such work would import. Its value is narrow: a
worked example of using absorption spectroscopy (including a transition out of 5S1/2) to bound the
Beer-Lambert-law regime as a function of density, and a cautionary data point that a real heated
cell's rubidium density can run measurably below what a saturated-vapour-pressure curve alone
would predict -- worth keeping in mind wherever a saturation-curve density is used in place of a
measured one.
