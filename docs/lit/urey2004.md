## What it computes

A unit-power converging Gaussian beam of 1/e^2 radius w_m meets a circular aperture of radius a at the exit pupil, and the focal field follows from the Fresnel form of the Huygens-Fresnel integral (Eqs. 3 to 5). The transmitted power is 1 - exp(-2/T^2) (Eq. 2). The peak focal irradiance, normalised to the transmitted power, is Eq. 6. It rises with T towards the uniform-illumination value, and normalised to the incident power it peaks near T = 0.9, a result the paper takes from Tanaka et al. (its ref. 11) and sets aside for imaging, verbatim: "This result is not really significant in an imaging system, since the width of the focused spot and the encircled energy is far more important than the peak axial irradiance." The focal profile is Gaussian below T = 0.5 and tends to the Airy pattern as T grows.

The spot diameter is written s = K lambda f#, with f# = R/2a and R the aperture-to-focus distance, and the paper states, verbatim, "where K is the spot size constant that is a function of beam truncation only and is independent of other system parameters." Table 2 fits K to the numerical integrals within 1 per cent for T above 0.4: 1.654 - 0.105/T + 0.28/T^2 for the full width at 1/e^2 and 1.036 - 0.058/T + 0.156/T^2 for the FWHM, against the Gaussian forms 1.27/T and 0.75/T below T = 0.5. The depth of focus is Delta z = K2 lambda f#^2, with K2 = 3.5 + 0.33/T - 0.73/T^2 + 0.52/T^3 at a Strehl ratio of 0.5 and 2.05 + 0.12/T - 0.28/T^2 + 0.22/T^3 at 0.8 (Gaussian forms 1.27/T^2 and 0.635/T^2). A Strehl ratio of 0.5 is the Rayleigh range for a Gaussian beam. Drege's closed form for the 1/e^2 spot constant (Eq. 10) agrees with the numerical integration to within 3 per cent.

## Validity and the rings

The formulas hold for Fresnel number N = a^2/(lambda R) above 5 and f# above 2 (Section 5). Below N = 5 the axial profile loses its symmetry about the geometric focus. Citing Mahajan, the paper says the peak axial irradiance, verbatim, "shifts toward the clipping aperture, and the amount of shift is a function of the Fresnel number", by more than 5 per cent. For large focal radius the ring irradiance is approximated in closed form (Eq. 13), with a phase factor of 1.04, 0.93, 0.84 and 0.77 at T = 0.5, 0.66, 1 and the uniform limit, and the mean ring irradiance is half the peak (Eq. 15). On the underfilled side, verbatim: "Clipping at the aperture is negligible, and the beam profile remains Gaussian as it propagates; however, the system resolution is poor." Going from T = 2/3 to T = 1 raises the power lost at the aperture from 1.1 to 13.5 per cent, improves the resolution by 13 per cent and lowers the diffraction-limited contrast by about 7.5 times.

## Use in this record

The 2025 beam passes the electro-optic modulator's 3 mm bore before it is focused, and chapter 7 puts the focus the bore allows at 40 to 45 um. This paper gives, in closed form, the two quantities that clipping sets first: the transmitted power and the on-axis focal irradiance as functions of T, and the growth of the spot with T at a fixed f-number, which is the mechanism behind the bore's floor on the focus. Computed here from Eqs. 2 and 6, not reported by the paper: 97 per cent transmission gives T = 0.755, and the on-axis focal irradiance relative to the unclipped beam of the same input radius is then (1 - exp(-1/T^2))^2 = 0.68, beside the 97 and 69 per cent that chapter 8 quotes for the input whose unclipped focus is 42 um.

Three limits apply. The aperture here is the focusing system's pupil, while the bench's bore sits upstream of the lens, so the field that reaches the lens has propagated freely after the clipping, and the agreement above is a check of order, not a measurement of that propagation. The validity condition N > 5 holds for a 3 mm bore at 993 nm only for an aperture-to-focus distance below about 0.45 m (computed here). And the paper reports widths, peaks and ring levels, not the distribution of intensity over the atoms' positions that the light-shift density needs. That distribution is the analysis side's clipped-focus profile, and no number here stands in for it.
