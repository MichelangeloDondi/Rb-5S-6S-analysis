## What it says, in its own terms

Verbatim, the paper's own summary (p. 1 = S85, "SUMMARY"): "In this paper we present inference
methods which are based on an 'incorrect' criterion, in the sense that the optimization of this
criterion does not directly provide a consistent estimator of the parameter of interest. Moreover,
the argument of the criterion, called the auxiliary parameter, may have a larger dimension than
that of the parameter of interest. A second step, based on simulations, provides a consistent and
asymptotically normal estimator of the parameter of interest. Various testing procedures are also
proposed. The methods described in this paper only require that the model can be simulated,
therefore they should be useful for models whose complexity rules out a direct approach. Various
fields of applications are suggested (microeconometrics, finance, macroeconometrics)."

From the introduction (p. 2 = S86), on what the method generalizes: "This kind of method was first
proposed by Smith (1990) and the extensions described here are threefold. First, we take
explicitly into account the presence of exogenous variables and we derive the modified
variance-covariance matrix of the estimator. Second, we consider more general criteria for the
first-step estimator of the auxiliary parameter. Third, we introduce several specification tests
of the initial model and a general theory of hypotheses testing based on indirect estimation."

And, directly relevant to `hansen1982` and to `smith1993` in this record's own cluster (same page,
p. 2 = S86), now confirmed complete and unhedged: "Moreover, we show that the indirect method
contains, as a special case, the simulated method of moments (SMM) (McFadden, 1989; Pakes and
Pollard, 1989; Duffie and Singleton, 1989; Smith, 1993;" and Gouriéroux and Monfort, 1993). (checked
against the rendered page directly. The quotation stops short of the final citation because this

From the conclusions (p. 24 = S108, section '11. CONCLUSIONS'), checked against the rendered page and

Moreover, the choice of the criterion function is natural and is likely to capture most features

## The formal construction (Sections 2-3, Appendices 1-2, newly read for this upgrade)

**The model and the two-step estimator (Section 2, pp. 3-7 = S87-S91).** The structural model is
a dynamic system y_t = r(y_{t-1}, x_t, u_t, θ), u_t = φ(u_{t-1}, ε_t, θ), θ ∈ Θ ⊂ R^p, equations
(1)-(2), p. 3 (S87), simulable given θ and a known noise law G0 for ε_t. Section 2.2 defines the
auxiliary parameter β ∈ B ⊂ R^q via a criterion Q_T maximized over β, equations (3)-(6), p. 4
(S88), and Assumption (A2) its non-stochastic limit Q∞(F0,G0,θ0,β). The **binding function**
b(F,G,θ) = Argmax_β Q∞(F,G,θ,β), equation (7), p. 4 (S88) (citing Gouriéroux and Monfort, 1992),
links the true θ0 to the pseudo-true β0 = b(F0,G0,θ0). Assumption (A4), p. 5 (S89), requires b to
be one-to-one with ∂b/∂θ' of full column rank. Section 2.3 (pp. 5-6 = S89-S90) constructs H
simulated paths at a trial θ, fits β̃^h_T(θ,z0^h) on each, and defines (**Proposition 1**, p. 5 =
S89) the indirect estimator as the θ minimizing a quadratic form in [β̂_T − (1/H)Σ_h β̃^h_T(θ,z0^h)]
against a weighting matrix Ω̂_T. **Proposition 2** (p. 6 = S90) gives an equivalent estimator based
on one long simulated path of length TH instead of H separate paths. Section 2.4 (p. 6 = S90)
notes the special, computationally simpler case where the auxiliary criterion is an approximate
log-likelihood of the same dimension as θ. Section 2.5 (p. 7 = S91) gives a third, gradient-based
version (following Gallant and Tauchen, 1992) requiring only one optimization in θ, connects it to
Smith's (1993) Simulated Quasi Maximum Likelihood estimator, and shows it is asymptotically
equivalent to the Proposition-1/2 family when its own metric Σ satisfies Ω = J0ΣJ0.

**The asymptotic distribution and the optimal metric (Section 3, pp. 7-10 = S91-S94).**
Assumptions (A5)-(A8), p. 7 (S91), define the three matrices the whole theory turns on: I0 (the
asymptotic variance of the simulated score), K0 (the covariance between the observed-data and
simulated-data score contributions, conditional on the exogenous path), and J0 (minus the
probability limit of the criterion's own Hessian). **Proposition 3** (p. 8 = S92) gives the general
result: √T(θ̃_T^H(Ω) − θ0) is asymptotically normal with the "modified variance-covariance matrix"
W(H,Ω) = (1+1/H) [∂b'/∂θ Ω ∂b/∂θ']^-1 [∂b'/∂θ Ω J0^-1(I0−K0)J0^-1 Ω ∂b/∂θ'] [∂b'/∂θ Ω ∂b/∂θ']^-1
(a sandwich form: read from the rendered page, p. 8 = S92, since this equation-heavy page's OCR
text layer is unreliable for the inline notation). **Proposition 4**, same page, gives the
**optimal choice of the metric**: Ω\* = J0(I0−K0)^-1 J0 (assuming I0−K0 invertible), collapsing the
sandwich to W_H\* = (1+1/H) [∂b'/∂θ J0(I0−K0)^-1J0 ∂b/∂θ']^-1. **Proposition 5** (p. 9 = S93)
rewrites this directly in terms of the criterion's own second derivatives (no reference to b's
derivative needed when dim β = dim θ) and isolates the finite-H inefficiency as a clean
multiplicative factor, W_H\* = (1+1/H)·W∞\*, equation (20): H replications cost only a
(1+1/H) efficiency loss relative to H → ∞, never a bias. Page 9-10 (S93-S94) works the moments
special case explicitly: with Q_T built from empirical moments k̄_T and Q_T = −½(k̄_T−β)'(k̄_T−β),
the method generalizes the SMM estimator proposed by Duffie and Singleton (1993), becoming
identical to it when there are no exogenous variables (p. 10 = S94, the rendered page reads this
as one uninterrupted sentence, but the PDF's text layer extracts it with the following display
equation's block spliced in between and the two halves swapped, so it is paraphrased here instead
of quoted).

**Estimating Ω\* and W_H\* in practice (Appendix 2, pp. 28-29 = S112-S113).** J0 is estimated from
minus the criterion's own numerically-differentiated Hessian at β̂_T (from equation 17, p. 7 =
S91). I0−K0, the harder piece, is estimated two ways: in the no-exogenous-variable case, by a
Newey-West-type long-run-variance (HAC) estimator of the criterion's own score sequence (citing
Newey and West, 1987), p. 28 (S112), and in the general case, by the sample covariance of S independent
simulation replicates' own score contributions, (T/S)Σ_s(W_s−W̄)(W_s−W̄)', p. 28 (S112).
That is, Monte Carlo replication stands in for the analytic long-run variance exactly where the model
is only known through simulation. **Appendix 1** (pp. 24-27 = S108-S111) carries the full proof of
Proposition 3 for all three estimator versions (Propositions 1, 2, and the Section 2.5
gradient-based one), the derivation running through the same first-order expansion and delta-method
argument each time.

## Specification tests and hypothesis testing (Sections 4-5, Appendices 3-4)

**Global specification test (Section 4.1, p. 10-11 = S94-S95).** **Proposition 6** (p. 11 = S95):
the optimal value of the objective function, TH/(1+H) times the minimized quadratic form, is
asymptotically χ²(q−p) under correct specification (q = dim β, p = dim θ), proved in Appendix 3
(pp. 29-30 = S113-S114) via a first-order expansion showing the statistic reduces to a quadratic
form in the projection of √T(β̂_T − β̃_HT(θ0)) orthogonal to the columns of ∂b/∂θ' (for the Ω\*
inner product). **Consistency test for the proxy model (Section 4.2, p. 11 = S95)**: compares
β̂_T (fit to the real data) against β̂_HT(β̂_T, z0) (fit to data simulated at the fitted θ),
needing no further estimation beyond what indirect estimation already computed: a built-in
posterior check that the auxiliary ("proxy") model is a good enough approximation.

**Hypothesis tests on θ itself (Section 5, pp. 11-12 = S95-S96, Appendix 4, pp. 30-33 =
S114-S117).** Partitioning θ = (θ1, θ2) and testing H0: θ1 = 0, the paper defines three test
statistics on the indirect estimator: a Wald statistic ξ_T^W (equation 21, p. 12 = S96), a score
statistic ξ_T^S built from the constrained estimator's gradient (equations 22-23), and a
criterion-difference statistic ξ_T^C comparing the constrained and unconstrained optimal objective
values (equation 24). **Proposition 7** (p. 12 = S96): all three are asymptotically equivalent
under H0 with the common distribution χ²(p1), "despite the use of simulated values": the
simulation noise washes out of the test's own asymptotic law even though it enters the estimator.
Appendix 4 carries the full algebra for all three, including the constrained estimator's own
first-order expansion and the equivalence proof.

## The worked examples (Sections 6-10, pp. 12-24 = S96-S108)

Six increasingly applied illustrations, each grounding the abstract machinery in an explicit,
numerically evaluated Monte Carlo:

- **Section 6 (pp. 12-14 = S96-S98), a Gaussian MA(1)** with true θ0 = 0.5: the auxiliary
  criterion is an AR(r) truncation for r = 1, 2, 3 (equation, p. 12 = S96), T = 250, H = 1, 200
  replications. **Table I** (p. 14 = S98) gives Mean/SD/RMSE for the indirect estimator at each r
  against the exact ML (Kalman-filter) estimator: the bias is small at every r, the efficiency
  loss is "relatively important" at r = 1, "almost reached" at r = 2, and "completely reached and
  even overshot" at r = 3 (p. 14 = S98, verbatim), while the r = 3 indirect estimator is measured
  as "about eighteen times faster" to compute than the ML estimator (same page).
- **Section 7 (pp. 14-19 = S98-S103), stochastic differential equations.** A geometric Brownian
  motion (equations 28-30, true μ = 0.2, σ = 0.5, T = 150, p. 16 = S100) and an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
  process (equations 31-33, true k = 0.8, a = 0.1, σ = 0.06, T = 250, p. 18 = S102) are each
  estimated three ways: exact ML on the model's own closed-form exact discretization, a "naive"
  estimator on a first-order (Euler) discretization, and the indirect estimator built from that
  same naive discretization as its auxiliary criterion. **Table II** (GBM, p. 17 = S101) and
  **Table III** (OU, p. 19 = S103) are the sharpest illustration in the paper of the point this
  record wants from it: the naive estimator is badly biased (σ bias +0.124 against a true 0.5 for
  GBM, k bias −0.226 against a true 0.8 for OU), while the indirect estimator built from that same
  biased proxy is nearly unbiased (σ bias −0.001, k bias +0.011) and close to the infeasible exact
  ML in RMSE. The correction is bought entirely by simulating the naive model at trial parameters
  and calibrating against the gap, with no change to the (cheap, tractable, biased) criterion itself.
- **Section 8 (pp. 19-21 = S103-S105), stochastic volatility and factor ARCH models**: builds
  tractable auxiliary criteria via a Kalman-filter pseudo-likelihood on a modified proxy state-space
  form (equations 34-37) and, for factor ARCH, via either the extended Kalman filter or a
  discretized-factor auxiliary model with a recursive likelihood (equations 38-41, p. 21 = S105).
- **Section 9 (pp. 21-23 = S105-S107), microeconometrics**: discrete-choice models whose
  likelihood needs high-dimensional normal-CDF integrals are handled by using a logit
  approximation of that CDF (equations 42-43, p. 22 = S106) as the tractable auxiliary criterion.
  Because the resulting simulated paths are not continuous in θ, the paper notes (p. 23 = S107)
  that the classical asymptotic theory of Section 3 does not directly apply and points to Pakes
  and Pollard (1989) for the needed generalized theory.
- **Section 10 (pp. 23-24 = S107-S108), macroeconometrics**: indirect estimation as an automatic
  correction for the bias of linearizing a nonlinear model around zero or around a long-run
  equilibrium (equations 44-48), and for approximating a latent-variable state-space model with
  an inconsistent extended-Kalman-filter first step (equations 49-50, p. 24 = S108).

## Use in this record

This is arguably the single most directly relevant paper in this cluster to this record's own
stated method. This record's own words (using the twin to compute and factor out biases)
describe an indirect-inference construction almost exactly, and the fuller read (Sections 2-3,
confirmed above) does not weaken that reading: the auxiliary parameter here is this record's
windowed-moment (or moment-ratio) vector, computed both on real data and on twin-simulated data at
a trial physical parameter. The "calibration" step is this record's own bias subtraction. Three
things follow from the parts read for this upgrade that the prior note could only flag as owed:

1. **The Section 7 Monte Carlo tables (II and III above) are a direct structural analogue of this
   record's own twin-correction logic**, worked out in a domain with an available closed-form
   truth to check against: a cheap, known-biased proxy (there, an Euler discretization, here, the
   ramp/lineshape statistics computed under whatever approximation is cheapest to simulate) is
   corrected by simulating that same proxy at trial parameters and calibrating against the gap to
   the real-data fit, recovering near-ML accuracy without ever needing the intractable exact
   likelihood. Section 11's own summary of when this works best, "an automatic correction for
   the asymptotic bias implied by the approximation" (quoted above), is close to a formal name
   for what this record calls factoring out a bias through the twin.
2. **Propositions 3-5 are a candidate formal treatment for exactly the degrees-of-freedom question
   the Hartlap/Ledoit-Wolf cluster (`hartlap2007`, `ledoit2004`) approaches from the
   covariance-estimation side.** The optimal metric Ω\* = J0(I0−K0)^-1J0 and the resulting
   modified covariance W_H\* are derived for precisely this record's own
   many-coordinates-versus-few-physical-parameters regime (the auxiliary parameter β "may have a
   larger dimension than that of the parameter of interest", summary, quoted above), and the
   (1+1/H) finite-H inefficiency factor (Proposition 5, equation 20) is a clean, already-derived
   answer to how many twin realizations H are enough, stated as a multiplicative efficiency cost,
   not a rule of thumb. Whether this record's own covariance estimate for its
   moment-ratio vector should be built the Newey-West way (Appendix 2's no-exogenous-variable
   case) or the replicate-variance way (Appendix 2's general case, (T/S)Σ(W_s−W̄)(W_s−W̄)') is a
   concrete, still-open design choice this paper poses but does not answer for this record's own
   case.
3. **Section 5's Wald/score/criterion-difference equivalence (Proposition 7) is a candidate formal
   basis for this record's own admission-test and model-ladder machinery**
   (`docs/methods/06_the_statistics.md`, sections 4.7-4.9), which currently rests on BIC/AIC
   comparisons, not an indirect-inference-specific test. The paper's own Section 4.2
   consistency test for the proxy/auxiliary model (comparing the real-data auxiliary fit against
   the auxiliary fit on simulated data at the fitted structural parameter, with no extra
   estimation needed) is close in spirit to this record's own per-session admission test (real
   data vs twin-simulated data at a trial parameter), though built from a chi-square on the
   optimal objective value, not a per-session z-score.

None of this is asserted as already adopted by this record. It is read here as the closest
published formal scaffolding for a method this record already practises informally, with specific,
page-grounded pointers (above) for whoever next writes the formal covariance/testing treatment
this record's own moment-ratio programme is owed.
