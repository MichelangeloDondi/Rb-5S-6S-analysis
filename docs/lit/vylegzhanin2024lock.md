# vylegzhanin2024lock

## The system

Locks the 480 nm pump of a two-photon Rydberg EIT ladder (780 nm probe on
5S1/2 to 5P3/2, 480 nm pump on 5P3/2 to 32D5/2) to Zeeman-split EIT peaks in
a 87Rb vapor cell. The applied magnetic field is transverse to the beam
propagation axis, along z, generated by a pair of rectangular coils, with
the light linearly polarized perpendicular to it and therefore seen by the
atoms as a combination of sigma-plus and sigma-minus components. The field
splits the EIT feature by sublevel. Stepping the field moves the chosen
peak, giving a lock point tunable over 0.6 GHz without adjusting the laser.

## The numbers

* Short-term stability: 0.15 MHz. Long-term stability: within 0.5 MHz,
  obtained by taking the separation between the most distant mean frequency
  values and fitting a Gaussian to a histogram of the drift over a
  ten-minute recording. The abstract's 0.5 MHz figure is not itself
  presented as a ten-minute value.
* For comparison, the paper cites Rajasree et al., where the long-term
  drift was about 0.5 MHz and the short-term stability about 0.4 MHz.
* Lock linewidth: about 0.8 MHz with the external field applied, about
  1.8 MHz without.
* Unshielded, zero-field lock point moves 1.6 MHz peak-to-peak with pump
  polarization. Locking to a Zeeman sublevel reduces this to 0.6 MHz.

## Use in this record

This paper reports three distinct quantities: a short-term frequency
stability of 0.15 MHz (averaging time unstated), a long-term stability
within 0.5 MHz from a ten-minute drift histogram, and lock linewidths of
0.8 MHz with the applied field against 1.8 MHz without. None of these is
directly comparable to the convolution-kernel linewidth extracted in this
analysis, which is inferred over a full interrogation and reported against
its own observation time. A comparison would need a shared observation time
and a stated transfer function between the two. The lock also reduces its
zero-field polarization sensitivity from 1.6 MHz peak-to-peak to 0.6 MHz by
choosing a Zeeman-sublevel line, the same discipline of measuring rather
than assuming away a systematic.
