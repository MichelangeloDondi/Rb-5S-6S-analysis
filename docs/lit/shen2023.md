## What it says, in its own terms

**The set-up.** Laser-cooled atoms in a trap of known, tunable depth lose population to
collisions with room-temperature background-gas particles. The paper's own prior work (refs
[1, 2]) showed that both the total velocity-averaged collision rate and the probability that a
colliding atom survives a trap of finite depth are universal functions depending only on the
magnitude of the leading dispersion coefficient C6 of the atom-background interaction
potential, V(R) = -C6/R^6. That universality is what lets a cold-atom trap act as a
self-calibrating pressure standard: measure the loss rate as a function of trap depth, fit to
the universal function, and read off the background gas density without an external gauge.

**Where it is expected to fail.** The post-collision energy-transfer distribution that
underlies the universal function is itself only an approximation, expected to deviate from
the universal form "in the limit of small C6 and small reduced mass" (p. 2, abstract). Light
background particles and light trapped species are exactly that limit, so the paper targets
H2 as the background gas and both Rb and Li as trapped species to look for the breakdown
directly.

**The cross-calibration trick.** Rather than trust the universal-function fit for either
species alone, the paper measures a direct ratio of the Li+H2 and Rb+H2 loss rates under
matched conditions. That ratio, R = 0.83(5), combined with an ab initio Li+H2 rate, gives a
Rb+H2 rate that agrees with an independently calculated ab initio Rb+H2 value to within
about 1%. By contrast, the standard procedure of fitting the Rb+H2 loss rate vs. trap depth to
the universal function alone gives a rate roughly 1.5 times larger than either ab initio
number. The paper reads this as the expected small-C6, small-reduced-mass breakdown of the
universal function, caught by comparing two independent routes to the same number rather than
trusting either one alone.

## What it is worth here

Adjacent field, not central. This is cold-atom vacuum metrology, not vapour-cell optical
spectroscopy, and the collision physics it probes (elastic, ground-state, room-temperature
background-gas scattering off an ultracold trapped sample) is a different regime from this
record's own warm-vapour Rb-Rb self-broadening of an optical two-photon line. What is worth
noting is the lineage: this is the same UBC Madison group whose Rb-Rb C6 measurement
([stewart2022](stewart2022.md)) and whose foundational trap-depth method
([vandongen2011](vandongen2011.md)) sit alongside it in this section, and the paper's own
methodological point, that a universal function should be validated by an independent
cross-check rather than trusted from a single fit, is a general good practice, not a
number this record can use directly.
