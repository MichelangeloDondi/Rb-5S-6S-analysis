## What it says, in its own terms

**The sample moment tensor is not the right estimator at high order.** "In contrast to covariance
estimation, the sample moment tensor is generally not rate-optimal for $d \ge 3$", and the paper
constructs an estimator attaining the minimax rate up to logarithmic factors (p. 1).

**Why cumulants and not moments.** "All cumulants of order $d \ge 3$ vanish for Gaussian distributions,
and cumulants are additive under independent summation. These properties make $K_d$ a canonical object
both in statistical theory and in computational pipelines that first whiten second-order structure and
then extract higher-order deviations from Gaussianity" (p. 2). That is, in a different field's words, the
argument this record's own high-order channel rests on.

**The reverse gap.** They identify a regime where an efficiently computable estimator achieves error
smaller than the separation at which low-degree tests can distinguish null from alternative, so efficient
detection is harder than efficient estimation, attributed to the loss itself being NP-hard to compute.

## Why the rate is not carried

This record estimates cumulants of a scalar lineshape, so $p = 1$ and the tensor collapses to a number.
$\sqrt{p/n}$ is then the ordinary $n^{-1/2}$ and says nothing specific. The paper's subject is the
geometry of a $p$-dimensional tensor, which this record does not have. What survives the difference is
the qualitative warning at $d \ge 3$ and the vanishing-for-Gaussian property quoted above.
