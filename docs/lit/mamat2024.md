# mamat2024

VERIFIED against the held preprint (scope in `verify_flags`).

## What it finds

The abstract, verbatim: "For single Rydberg atoms in optical tweezer arrays, we reveal that one of the background electric fields originates from electrons bound to the vacuum cell surface." And, verbatim: "These electrons are generated by the 297-nm light used for single-photon Rydberg excitation." The proof is a detuned 297 nm pulse before the probe: the transition shifts further the longer that pulse lasts, while the tweezer apparatus alone generates no charge on a seconds scale (their Fig. 4). The authors leave the mechanism open between blackbody ionisation of the Rydberg atoms and photoionisation of Rb adsorbates with a lowered threshold.

Without the UV lamps the lines are broadened by drifting fields whose standard deviation the authors put at about 73 mV/cm. With them, a 0.19 +- 0.07 MHz linewidth matches the 0.18 MHz calculated with no field noise, and Rabi oscillations gain coherence (Fig. 2). Less than 14 mW of 365 nm light suffices, and nothing more happens up to 3.43 W (Fig. 3). Verbatim: "The intensity of UV light required to remove these electrons is obviously lower than that needed for light-induced atomic desorption (LIAD) [34–37]." Diodes at 660 nm, which leave the Rb loading rate unchanged and so desorb no Rb, do the same, and so do diodes at 465 nm.

Against an absolute frequency from a vapour cell, the static field is about 255 mV/cm without the lamps and 298 mV/cm with them. Verbatim: "A reduction of the background electric field caused by the binding of electrons to the surface is consistent with the conclusion in Ref. [27]." (reference 27 is `sedlacek2016`). On stability, verbatim from Appendix B: "The spectral shift is less than 2.8 MHz in almost one year even if we try to heat the vacuum cell by hot wind to change the distribution of adsorbates or remove charges from the outer surface of the cell wall." Without the lamps the shift reaches about 6 MHz and changes within a week.

## Use in this record

Read with `sedlacek2016`, this settles the sign of a trade the fibre arm will face: electrons bound to a Rb-covered silica surface lower its static field and add a fluctuating one, and light below the Rb desorption threshold removes them. For a Rydberg measurement at a fibre, whose own excitation light illuminates the surface, the choice between a lower static field and a quieter one is an experimental setting to be made and recorded, not a property of the fibre. The atoms here sit millimetres from the wall and the excitation is single-photon ultraviolet, so the paper gives the mechanism and its controls, not a size for a fibre.

Related on this shelf: `sedlacek2016`, `ocola2024` (UV and the Rydberg excitation rate set the charge on a tapered fibre), `davtyan2018`.
