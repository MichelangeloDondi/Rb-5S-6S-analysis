# lim2021

VERIFIED against the held preprint (scope in `verify_flags`).

## The measurement

A separate optics bench emulates the trap: a 780 nm elliptical Gaussian beam of 1/e^2 diameters 2 and 4 mm, symmetrically truncated by a horizontal slit of opening D and focused by a cylindrical lens of focal length 100 mm, with the focal intensity recorded plane by plane near the focus. The clipping is parameterised by the slit width over the incident 1/e^2 diameter, gamma = D/2w. The profile across the unclipped direction keeps its Gaussian form, so the paper treats the two-dimensional distribution in the clipped plane.

## What it finds

Verbatim: "It is clearly shown that the focal region is elongated along the beam propagation direction as γ decreases, i.e., the laser beam becomes more clipped."

Verbatim: "For γ = 0.5, the high-intensity region, where I/I0 > 0.9, is stretched over 6 mm along the y-axis, which is approximately 4 times longer than that without clipping."

On the radius, verbatim: "When the laser beam is significantly truncated for γ < 1, the beam radius noticeably increases and furthermore, it exhibits peculiar y-dependence such that there appears a central region with a quasi-constant beam radius."

The focus moves too, verbatim: "The focus position is slightly shifted towards the focusing lens with decreasing γ [9], which is attributed to the spherical aberration of the focusing lens."

The simulation is the paraxial Rayleigh-Sommerfeld integral of the slit-truncated Gaussian through the lens. The authors state that they neglect the propagation from the slit to the lens, and report it in good quantitative agreement with the measured maps (Fig. 2). In the hard-clipping limit the input is a flat top of width D. Clipping costs transmitted power, 16 per cent at gamma 0.64 and 25 per cent at 0.55 in the trap experiment.

## What is not understood (p. 4)

Below the paper's own optimal clipping (gamma = 0.64), the condensate's axial extent and atom number both fall markedly, and the paper does not settle on why: "It might be attributed to the low transfer efficiency of atoms to the ODT due to insufficient power of the clipped laser beam or some uncontrolled diffraction effects of the deep clipping, which are not clearly understood at the moment" (p. 4). Grammatically "which are" attaches to the second, plural candidate (the diffraction effects), not to the singular transfer-efficiency explanation before it, but the sentence does not commit to either. The transmitted-power loss over this same range is stated in the paper's own next sentence: 16 per cent at gamma = 0.64 and 25 per cent at gamma = 0.55 (p. 4).

## Use in this record

The analysis side's collection geometry and axial ramp weighting are written with the Gaussian Rayleigh range of the fitted waist, the pi w0^2/lambda of an unclipped beam. This paper measures the opposite of that assumption on a clipped beam: the high-intensity region along the axis is longer than the Gaussian's and its radius flatter, so a collection ratio or an axial window computed from the Gaussian formula at a bore-limited focus carries a model error in the direction of too short a focal region. The size for the 2025 geometry is not in this paper, whose clipping is one-dimensional and whose slit sits at the lens. It belongs to the analysis side's clipped-focus profile, which the owner's ruling of 2026-09-17 puts into the model.
