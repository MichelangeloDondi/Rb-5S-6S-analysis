# Breeden and Litzenberger 1978: a density read off a family of threshold-indexed prices

**Why this record holds it.** It is the priority reference for recovering a distribution from a
family of quantities indexed by a threshold. An option price at a strike is an upper partial
moment of the risk-neutral density, and the density is recovered by differentiating twice in the
strike. That is the same inversion this record performs when it reads a shift distribution off a
line, and it is from 1978.

## What it does

The paper derives the price of an elementary claim on a portfolio from the second partial
derivative of the call pricing function with respect to the exercise price, and from those prices
"the value of any asset whose payoffs are known functions of the portfolio's values at future
dates" follows.

## What this settles for this record's novelty wording

It settles that reading a distribution out of a threshold-indexed family is not ours and is
half a century old, which narrows the claim in the same direction A185 already narrowed it for
indirect inference. What is not in this paper is a bias: the inversion there is exact in the
continuum, and the finite-range problem is Jiang and Tian's subject, not this one's. So the
citation to make is about the inversion, never about the truncation, and a sentence that cites
this paper for the window would be citing it for something it does not contain.
