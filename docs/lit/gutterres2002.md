## What it says, in its own terms

Photoassociation spectroscopy of ultracold 87Rb2 molecules populates the
long-range, double-well 0g-(5S+5P3/2) electronic state. Because the outer
well of this potential sits entirely in the region where the two atoms
interact through the long-range dipole-dipole (R^-3), quadrupole (R^-6) and
R^-8 terms, fitting the observed rovibrational spacings to an analytic
long-range potential (with a generalized simulated annealing search over the
model parameters) determines the same C3 dispersion coefficient that fixes
the atomic 5s-5p transition dipole matrix element and, through it, the
radiative lifetimes of the 5P3/2 and 5P1/2 states.

Applying this to photoassociation data recorded elsewhere (by Gabbanini and
coworkers) for cold 87Rb, the paper reports a 5P3/2 lifetime of 26.25(8) ns
and a 5P1/2 lifetime of 27.75(8) ns, each at a stated precision (0.3%)
comparable to direct atomic-beam lifetime measurements, and states this is
the first such determination from an accurate analysis of this particular
long-range state. The fit also returns the radial spin-orbit coupling
parameters, the asymptotic amplitude product of the 5s and 5p atomic
wavefunctions, and second-order (C6, C8) dispersion coefficients for both
branches of the potential, compared against earlier calculations in Table I.

## What it is worth here

An independent, sub-percent determination of the Rb 5P3/2 and 5P1/2
radiative lifetimes from molecular spectroscopy rather than from a
beam-and-laser lifetime measurement or an atomic-structure calculation,
which makes it a useful cross-check for any 5P branching-ratio or
oscillator-strength input feeding a polarizability sum over states. It is
otherwise a molecular-physics paper with no bearing on lineshapes, ac-Stark
ramps, or vapour-cell collisional broadening, so its use here is confined to
that one atomic constant.
