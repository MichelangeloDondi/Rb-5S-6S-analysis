# ko2026

VERIFIED. Ko, Krokosz, Mazelanik, Wasilewski, Parniak (Faculty of Physics and Centre for Quantum Optical Technologies, University of Warsaw). Opt. Express 34(14), 25823 (2026), also on arXiv as 2604.19311.

## The system and method

Spatially resolved, self-calibrated imaging of a millimeter-wave (mmWave) electric field inside a 50 mm glass cell of warm 87Rb, at 130.72 GHz. Three co-linear optical fields, 780.24 nm (probe), 776.98 nm (coupling) and 1269.03 nm (Rydberg), drive a ladder from the ground state 5S1/2 through 5P3/2 and 5D5/2 to the Rydberg state 32F7/2. The mmWave field couples 32F7/2 to 34D5/2, a transition with no fluorescence path back down in the absence of the mmWave field, so fluorescence at 480 nm (34D5/2 to 5P3/2) occurs only where the mmWave field is driving that last step, giving the imaged signal an effectively zero background.

Absolute field calibration comes from the Autler-Townes (AT) splitting of the 32F7/2-34D5/2 transition. Rather than fitting two Gaussian peaks, which fails once the standing wave develops local minima below threshold and thousands of spectra must be fit at once, the splitting is extracted by solving a five-level Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) master equation for the steady state of a Hamiltonian carrying all four optical/mmWave Rabi frequencies, a decay rate per level, and an explicit transit-time decay rate added to every diagonal entry to model an atom crossing the interaction region in finite time. Doppler broadening is folded in by adding a velocity-dependent term to the Hamiltonian's diagonal and Maxwell-Boltzmann-averaging the resulting density matrix over velocity class before reading the fluorescence as the expectation value of the top-level population. The fitted Rabi frequency Omega_mm(z) is verified against an independently calibrated attenuator (a half-wave-plate-plus-polarizer chain), reproduces standing-wave interference from the cell's back window, and can be suppressed or enhanced with a 3D-printed high-impact-polystyrene Bragg reflector.

Section 3 bins detuning-scanned fluorescence images along the laser axis into $I(n, \Delta_{1269}) $, a spectrum for every pixel position $n$, and Section 4.2 fits each spectrum against the steady-state GKSL model to return $\hat\Omega_{mm}(z) $, a field amplitude resolved along $z$, the position along the beam.

## Validity

The driven system is a five-level ladder through 5P3/2 and 5D5/2 to Rydberg states 32F7/2 and 34D5/2, and the observable is an mmWave-driven Autler-Townes splitting used for electric-field metrology, not an AC-Stark-shift distribution read from a lineshape asymmetry. There is no self-broadening coefficient, polarizability, magic wavelength, or light-shift number in this paper. Autler-Townes splitting arises from a coherently driven, strongly coupled two-level subsystem (the mmWave field), a different mechanism from a spread of AC-Stark shifts across an inhomogeneous ensemble.

The transit-time and Doppler treatment shares its ingredient list with warm-vapor two-photon spectroscopy generally: a single added decay rate Γ_tr with no stated functional form, and a one-dimensional Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity average along the shared optical axis. This is common to most warm-vapor EIT and Rydberg-electrometry work and is not a transferable result for a natural linewidth or a self-broadening coefficient.
