# ko2026

**Read in full 2026-08-03.** Ko, Krokosz, Mazelanik, Wasilewski, Parniak
(Faculty of Physics and Centre for Quantum Optical Technologies, University
of Warsaw). Optics Express 34 (14), 25823, received 14 Apr 2026, revised 22
May 2026, accepted 1 Jun 2026, published 6 Jul 2026. Also on arXiv as
2604.19311. Identified from the PDF's own header (Vol. 34, No. 14, 13 Jul
2026, Optics Express 25823) and its DOI printed on page 1,
https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.601412.

## What the paper does

The goal is spatially resolved, self-calibrated imaging of a millimetre-wave
(mmWave) electric field inside a 50 mm glass cell of warm 87Rb, at 130.72 GHz.
Three co-linear optical fields, 780.24 nm (probe), 776.98 nm (coupling) and
1269.03 nm (Rydberg), drive a ladder from the ground state 5S1/2 through
5P3/2 and 5D5/2 up to the Rydberg state 32F7/2. The mmWave field then couples
32F7/2 to 34D5/2, a transition with "no fluorescence path back down" in the
absence of the mmWave field, so fluorescence at 480 nm (34D5/2 to 5P3/2)
occurs only where the mmWave field is actually driving that last step. That
is the "background-free" part of the title: the imaged fluorescence has
"effectively zero background" because the decay channel it reports on is
dark unless the field is present.

Absolute field calibration comes from the Autler-Townes (AT) splitting of
that top Rydberg transition, extracted not by fitting two Gaussian peaks
(which fails once "the standing wave [has] local minima below threshold" and
thousands of spectra must be fit at once) but by solving a five-level
Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) master equation for the steady
state, rho-dot = 0, of a Hamiltonian carrying all four optical/mmWave Rabi
frequencies, a decay rate per level, and an explicit transit-time decay
Gamma_tr added to every diagonal entry to model "the effect of an atom
crossing the interaction region in a finite time." Doppler broadening is
folded in by adding a velocity-dependent term to the Hamiltonian's diagonal
(their Eq. 6) and then Maxwell-Boltzmann-averaging the resulting density
matrix over velocity class, rho = Integral w(v) rho_v dv (Eq. 8), before
reading off the fluorescence as an expectation value of the top-level
population. The fitted Rabi frequency Omega_mm(z) is then verified against an
independently calibrated attenuator (a half-wave-plate-plus-polarizer chain),
and shown to reproduce standing-wave interference from the cell's back
window, and to be suppressible or enhanceable with a 3D-printed High Impact
Polystyrene Bragg reflector.

## Bridge to this programme

No physics bridge. The driven system is a five-level ladder through 5P3/2 and
5D5/2 to Rydberg 32F7/2 and 34D5/2, a completely different atomic pathway from
this programme's degenerate two-photon 5S1/2-to-6S1/2 line at 993 nm, and the
observable is an mmWave-driven Autler-Townes splitting used for absolute
electric-field metrology, not an AC-Stark-shift distribution read off a
lineshape's asymmetry. There is no self-broadening coefficient, no
polarizability, no magic wavelength and no light-shift number here that
speaks to Delta_alpha, beta_self(6S) or any magic-lambda claim this programme
makes. The physical mechanism producing structure in the line is also
different in kind: AT splitting comes from a coherently driven, strongly
coupled two-level subsystem (the mmWave field itself), not from a spread of
AC-Stark shifts across an inhomogeneous ensemble, so the paper's splitting is
not an instance of the shift-distribution asymmetry this repository's method
targets, even loosely.

The methodological point is closer, and worth naming precisely rather than
folding into "no bridge." Their treatment of a warm vapour cell folds in
transit-time broadening as an added decay rate on the diagonal of the
Hamiltonian and Doppler broadening as a Maxwell-Boltzmann-weighted average
over velocity classes of the resulting steady-state density matrix, which is
the same two ingredients, transit decay and velocity-class-weighted Doppler
averaging, that this programme's own transit and Doppler treatment carries
(compare `docs/methods/02_the_lineshape.md`, biraben1979, borde1976). But the
resemblance stops at the ingredient list: their transit decay is a single
added rate Gamma_tr with no stated functional form, their velocity averaging
is 1D along the shared optical axis of a five-field ladder with no analogue
of a finite-transit Doppler-free two-photon cusp, and the whole apparatus is
built to extract an RF Rabi frequency, not a natural linewidth or a
self-broadening coefficient. That is a shared toolkit, common across most
warm-vapour EIT and Rydberg-electrometry work, not a transferable result or
technique this programme could cite for a specific claim. Filed as a method
anchor for that reason, in the same sense as banerjee2003: read in full,
correctly identified, and honestly carrying nothing this programme's argument
can lean on.

**Lineage placement beside patrick2025: no physics bridge, but a real
position-resolved-reconstruction bridge.** The verdict above concerns physics
content (no shared coefficient, no shared shift mechanism), not method
lineage, and the two should not be conflated. Read for the latter, this paper
is a camera-based, position-resolved distribution-from-spectrum
reconstruction, confirmed against the held PDF rather than inferred from the
title. The abstract states the method plainly: "a spatially resolved method
for imaging millimeter-wave (mmWave) electric fields... Absolute calibration
of the local electric field is achieved by reconstructing the
Autler-Townes splitting of the Rydberg resonance across the imaging volume."
Section 3 bins a stack of detuning-scanned fluorescence images along the
laser axis into $I(n, \Delta_{1269})$, a spectrum for every pixel position
$n$, and Section 4.2 fits each such spectrum against the steady-state GKSL
model to return $\hat\Omega_{mm}(z)$, an mmWave-field amplitude explicitly
resolved along $z$, the position along the beam. That is the same operation
[patrick2025](patrick2025.md) performs with a different observable: a camera
images fluorescence along a cell axis, and a per-position spectral fit
(there Stark-shifted EIT peaks, here AT-split fluorescence) turns one
path-integrated photodetector-style measurement into a profile resolved along
the beam. Placed in this repository's own three-way distinction between
answering shape, where, and where-along-the-beam: `patrick2025` answers WHERE
a perturbation is (localizing an E-field to a wall-adjacent patch via a 2D
frequency-vs-position map), this repository's own fringe-averaged $f(s)$
answers SHAPE (a distribution's functional form, from one spatially-integrated
trace with no camera at all), and `ko2026` answers WHERE-ALONG-THE-BEAM
specifically (a field amplitude $\Omega_{mm}(z)$ reconstructed as a 1D profile
along the shared optical axis, via AT splitting rather than a Stark-shifted
EIT lineshape). The methodological parallel is real: this paper reconstructs a
spatial field amplitude along the beam from position-resolved AT splitting, and
that is conceptually parallel to this programme's own
spatial-shift-from-shape reading. It does not license
any physics-content bridge, which the assessment above still rules out.
