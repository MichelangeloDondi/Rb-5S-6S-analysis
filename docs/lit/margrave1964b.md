## What it says, in its own terms

This is a short, signed book review rather than a research article: it describes and evaluates Nesmeyanov's English-language compilation of vapour-pressure data and measurement methods for the elements, without presenting new measurements of its own. The reviewer summarizes the book's structure -- a critical evaluation of available vapour-pressure data plotted on a log P versus 1/T basis, fitted to the four-parameter form log P = A - B/T + CT + D log T, with the fitted coefficients tabulated and reference tables giving pressures at 50-degree intervals (and the temperatures at which selected pressures are reached). Heats of sublimation at 0 K and 298 K are given, but the review notes that no fuller thermodynamic functions are tabulated.

Its most substantive content is a critical comparison: the reviewer points out that three contemporary authorities on the thermodynamic properties of the elements disagreed on selected heats of sublimation by amounts he considers scientifically significant -- from a few kcal/mol for several elements up to 17-22 kcal/mol for niobium -- and attributes at least part of the spread to low evaporation or condensation coefficients that were only being resolved by work contemporaneous with the review (1961-1963). It also singles out the book's roughly 26-page treatment of the isotope-exchange method, developed by Nesmeyanov's own group, as unusually thorough for the literature of the time, while noting that some modern techniques, such as high-temperature mass spectrometry, receive only a single page.

The review's verdict is mixed: useful mainly as a reference to the pre-1960 literature, already three years out of date by publication, with several elements lacking 1960 data (rhodium, palladium, osmium, iridium, hafnium) already well studied by the time of the review, and, in the reviewer's judgment, superseded for everyday use by more recent English-language compilations that also tabulate full thermodynamic functions.

## What it is worth here

This is provenance, not data: the review contains no numerical vapour-pressure entries for any element and does not mention rubidium at all. Its value to a record that builds a vapour-density model N(T) from literature vapour-pressure fits is that it independently corroborates two things already visible elsewhere in this record's own literature (Nesmeyanov's compilation is already cited in other held notes as a vapour-pressure source, and its fitting form is the same family this record's own density model compares against): that Nesmeyanov's book was a serious, widely used, if already dated, compilation, and that even reputable vapour-pressure and heat-of-sublimation compilations of the same era disagreed with each other by several kcal/mol for some elements -- a useful qualitative reminder of the systematic spread that can exist between literature vapour-pressure sources, though this specific review supplies no quantitative bound relevant to Rb.
