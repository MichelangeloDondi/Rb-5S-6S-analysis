# jayjong2026

Held. Verified against the PDF, nine pages, on 2026-09-10.

## What they measured

The differential scalar polarizability of the Ba+ S1/2 to D5/2 clock
transition has a zero crossing near 481 nm. They locate it at
**623.603 13(17) THz**, which is 2.7 parts in ten million.

From it they infer a ratio of reduced matrix elements,
`<P3/2||r||S1/2> / <P1/2||r||S1/2> = 1.411 81(13)`, described in the abstract
as a stringent test of atomic structure calculations. The measurement then
supports an approximation to the whole `Delta_alpha_0(omega)` curve valid to
450 THz carrying only ONE reduced matrix element in its parameterisation, and
they apply it to blackbody radiation shifts.

## Why it matters to this record, in two directions

**It validates the strategy.** A zero crossing is located without any absolute
intensity, so the waist that makes every absolute result here a bound does not
enter. This record reached the same argument independently on 2026-09-10 and
this paper had already made it, better, with a measurement behind it.

**And its key trick is unavailable to a J = 1/2 to J = 1/2 transition.** Their
intensity independence comes from a ratio of the scalar shift to the TENSOR
shift, which shares the same zero crossing while cancelling the intensity. A
tensor polarizability requires an angular momentum of at least one. Both states
of the 5S to 6S transition have J = 1/2 and therefore no tensor term at all, so
that ratio cannot be formed here. **The absence of tensor structure, which this
record had been reading as a simplification, removes the observable that makes
their measurement immune to intensity drift.** A zero crossing on this
transition would still need no intensity CALIBRATION. It would need intensity
STABILITY, which is a weaker claim and has to be said as such.

## What it opens

Their closing point is that the approach applies to other alkali-like systems.
The equivalent question here is which ratio, on a transition with no tensor
term, could play the part their `delta_0 / delta_2` plays. The two isotopes and
the four hyperfine components share one beam, so a ratio among them is the
place to look, and this record has not looked.
