# liu2001

VERIFIED, 2026-08-03. Full text read (5 pages).

## The system

Two Doppler-free two-photon transitions in natural-abundance potassium (93.26% 39K, 6.73% 41K, 40K neglected at 0.01%): 4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 at 728.4 nm/2 (2 x 411.4 THz) and 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 at 729.8 nm/2 (2 x 410.6 THz), studied at the Clarendon Laboratory, University of Oxford. The excitation frequencies sit near 1/6 of the 1S-2S transition interval of hydrogen, deuterium, and muonium, motivating the transitions as a possible frequency-transfer standard linking those systems.

## The method

A cw Ti:Sapphire laser (Microlase MBR-110, argon-ion pumped) is focused through a heated potassium cell and retro-reflected by a plane mirror, giving two counter-propagating beams with w0 about 70 um. Both lines are read out through 405 nm cascade fluorescence (6S/4D -> 4P -> 4S, filtered with a 405 nm bandpass interference filter in front of a PMT), because the alternative decay routes near 730 nm cannot be optically discriminated from the excitation light.

A transition-rate calculation (eq. 2, using tabulated Einstein A-values) predicts the 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 line at about 6.8 x 10^-3 of the 4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 rate, meaning a signal-to-noise ratio of at least 150 on the 6S1/2 line is needed to see it at all. The observed 4D5/2 signal came out at about 1/200 of the 6S1/2 signal, in agreement with the estimate. The observed signal-to-noise ratios were >200 for the S-S line and ~3 for the S-D line. The 4S1/2 -> 4D3/2 branch, predicted at only 2.8 x 10^-4, was not observed.

The 4S-6S line was recorded at a cell temperature of 150 C (density 5 x 10^19 atoms/m^3, i.e. 5 x 10^13 cm^-3) with 200 mW of laser power. The much weaker 4S-4D5/2 line required raising the cell to 250 C (10^21 atoms/m^3, i.e. 10^15 cm^-3) and the power to 300 mW, plus an added quarter-wave-plate-and-polarizing-beam-splitter optical isolator to suppress optical feedback into the laser, which increased the S/N by more than a factor of 100.

## The numbers

- Natural linewidth of the K 6S1/2 state (Table 1): 2.04 MHz. The 4D3/2 and 4D5/2 natural widths are 0.55 and 0.56 MHz.
- Measured 4S-6S two-photon linewidth: about 17 MHz FWHM at 150 C, one density, one laser power. About 1 MHz of this is ascribed to transit-time broadening. The rest is attributed, undivided, to laser jitter and collisional broadening.
- Light shift: estimated to be less than 20 kHz under the conditions employed, a calculated ceiling at one fixed power, not a measured slope against intensity.
- Hyperfine splitting (F=1 -> F'=1 to F=2 -> F'=2 separation), this work against a prior optical-double-resonance value: 39K 420(3) MHz against 418.08(20) MHz, 41K 232(8) MHz against 229.94(80) MHz.
- Isotope shift (41K minus 39K), 4S-6S transition, centers of gravity: 520(3) MHz.
- Absolute laser excitation frequencies (wavemeter, half the two-photon transition frequency): 4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 411475 +/- 2 GHz, 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 410672 +/- 2 GHz.

## Validity

No density-dependent or intensity-dependent environmental coefficient is reported. Both lines are recorded at one fixed cell temperature and one fixed laser power each, so no self-broadening rate or light-shift slope is separated from the other contributions to the linewidth. The collisional contribution to the 17 MHz FWHM is not isolated. About 1 MHz is ascribed to transit-time broadening and the remainder jointly to laser jitter and collisional broadening, with no density scan and no coefficient reported in any unit. The light shift is given only as an upper bound at one fixed power density, not as a slope measured against varying intensity. Temperature and pressure dependence are not scanned as independent variables. The 6S1/2 line is taken at 150 C and the 4D5/2 line at 250 C, each chosen only for adequate signal.

## Use in this record

K 4S -> 6S is the potassium analog of the Rb 5S -> 6S two-photon transition: both are the lowest-lying alkali nS -> (n+1)S two-photon transition off the ground state, excited by a single-colour retro-reflected beam and read out by cascade fluorescence through an intervening P state. This paper is the earlier and cruder of two potassium measurements of the same line, using a single density and a wavemeter frequency axis good to +/-2 GHz with no EOM-sideband calibration, against `kirankumar2011`'s decade-later +/-10 MHz absolute frequency and sub-MHz hyperfine splittings. Neither paper reports a measured self-broadening rate, light-shift slope, or other density- or intensity-dependent coefficient for the potassium S-S two-photon line. Across the alkali nS two-photon literature, `zameroski2014` (Rb 5S->7S) remains the one directly measured alkali nS self-broadening rate, and `lee2010`/`lee2012` (Cs 6S->8S) remain the one line with a measured light shift and a power/pressure Voigt decomposition.
