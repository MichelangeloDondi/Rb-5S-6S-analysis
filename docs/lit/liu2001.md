## Abstract, verbatim

**Verbatim, split at the source's own semicolon** (house style bars
semicolons here, no word added or removed): "The two-photon transitions
4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 and 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 in atomic potassium have been observed
free of the first-order Doppler effect. The rate for the S-D transition,
which has been detected for the first time, is two orders of magnitude
lower than that for the S-S transition." Continuing straight on from that
semicolon in the source: "The observed signal to noise ratios for the
S-S and S-D transitions were respectively >200 and ~3. The laser
excitation frequencies are located in the near vicinity of 1/6 of the
1S-2S transition interval of hydrogen, deuterium and the hydrogen-like
atom, muonium. These potassium
transitions may therefore provide a suitable frequency transfer standard
for all these systems and a useful link between them."

## What it does

Two Doppler-free two-photon transitions in natural-abundance potassium
(93.26% 39K, 6.73% 41K, 40K neglected at 0.01%): 4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 at 728.4
nm/2 (2 x 411.4 THz) and 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 at 729.8 nm/2 (2 x 410.6 THz). A
cw Ti:Sapphire laser (Microlase MBR-110, argon-ion pumped) is focused
through a heated potassium cell and retro-reflected by a plane mirror to
give two counter-propagating beams with w0 about 70 um. Both lines are
read out through 405 nm cascade fluorescence (6S/4D -> 4P -> 4S, filtered
with a 405 nm bandpass interference filter in front of a PMT), because
the alternative decay routes near 730 nm cannot be optically discriminated
from the excitation light.

A transition-rate calculation (their eq 2, using tabulated Einstein
A-values) predicts the 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 line at about 6.8 x 10^-3 of the
4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 rate, meaning a S/N of at least 150 on the 6S1/2 line is
needed to see it at all. The observed 4D5/2 signal came out at about
1/200 of the 6S1/2 signal, which the paper calls agreement with the
estimate. The 4S1/2 -> 4D3/2 branch, predicted at only 2.8 x 10^-4, was
not observed.

The 4S-6S line was recorded at a cell temperature of 150 C (density 5 x
10^19 atoms/m^3, i.e. 5 x 10^13 cm^-3) with 200 mW of laser power. The
much weaker 4S-4D5/2 line required raising the cell to 250 C (10^21
atoms/m^3, i.e. 10^15 cm^-3) and the power to 300 mW, plus an added
quarter-wave-plate-and-polarizing-beam-splitter optical isolator to
suppress optical feedback into the laser, which increased the S/N by
more than a factor of 100.

## Key numbers

- Natural linewidth of the K 6S1/2 state (Table 1): **2.04 MHz**. The
  4D3/2 and 4D5/2 natural widths are 0.55 and 0.56 MHz.
- Measured 4S-6S two-photon linewidth: **about 17 MHz FWHM** at 150 C,
  one density, one laser power. About 1 MHz of this is ascribed to
  transit-time broadening. The rest is attributed, undivided, to "laser
  jitter and collisional broadening."
- Light shift: "estimated to be less than 20 kHz under the conditions
  employed," a calculated ceiling at one fixed power, not a measured
  slope against intensity.
- Hyperfine splitting (F=1 -> F'=1 to F=2 -> F'=2 separation), this work
  against a prior optical-double-resonance value: 39K **420(3) MHz**
  against 418.08(20) MHz, 41K **232(8) MHz** against 229.94(80) MHz.
- Isotope shift (41K minus 39K), 4S-6S transition, centres of gravity:
  **520(3) MHz**.
- Absolute laser excitation frequencies (wavemeter, half the two-photon
  transition frequency): 4S1/2 -> 6S1/2 **411475 +/- 2 GHz**, 4S1/2 ->
  4D5/2 **410672 +/- 2 GHz**.

## BRIDGES: the earlier half of the same-family K S-S two-photon pair, and what it still does not carry

K 4S -> 6S is the direct potassium sibling of this programme's Rb 5S ->
6S line: both are the lowest-lying alkali nS -> (n+1)S two-photon
transition off the ground state, excited the same way (a single-colour,
retro-reflected beam, Doppler-free by construction) and read out the same
way (cascade fluorescence through an intervening P state). This paper is
also the earlier and cruder of the two K papers now held here: a single
density, a wavemeter frequency axis good to +/-2 GHz, and no EOM-sideband
calibration, against `kirankumar2011`'s decade-later +/-10 MHz absolute
frequency and sub-MHz hyperfine splittings.

**What it measures.** The natural 6S1/2 linewidth, the 4S-6S hyperfine
splitting for both stable isotopes, the 41K-39K isotope shift, absolute
transition frequencies, and, as its main methodological contribution, the
first-ever detection of the far weaker 4S1/2 -> 4D5/2 two-photon line,
with a transition-rate calculation that correctly predicts the observed
line-strength ratio.

**What it does NOT measure.** No density-dependent or intensity-dependent
environmental coefficient appears anywhere as a fitted or scanned result.
Both lines are recorded at one fixed cell temperature and one fixed laser
power each, so nothing is varied to separate a self-broadening rate or a
light-shift slope from the other contributions to the linewidth:
- **Collisional broadening**: not isolated at all. Of the 17 MHz FWHM,
  "approximately 1 MHz of this can be ascribed to transit time
  broadening and the rest to laser jitter and collisional broadening,"
  with no attempt to separate the two, no density scan, and no
  coefficient reported in any unit.
- **Light shift (AC Stark)**: given only as an upper bound at one fixed
  power density, not as a slope measured against varying intensity.
- **Temperature/pressure dependence generally**: the 6S1/2 line is taken
  at 150 C and the 4D5/2 line at 250 C, each chosen for adequate signal,
  not scanned as an independent variable.

**Verdict on the deep-research claim.** liu2001 is the earlier half of
the same-family K S-S two-photon pair with `kirankumar2011`, and it
independently supports the same scoping conclusion: no measured S-S
environmental coefficient of any kind, self-broadening, light-shift
slope, or any other density- or intensity-dependent number, exists in
either K paper. The picture is consistent across a decade: liu2001
(2001) leaves the 17 MHz width undecomposed beyond a fixed transit-time
estimate, and `kirankumar2011` (2011) explicitly calculates or
extrapolates every candidate environmental shift from outside sources for
the specific purpose of cancelling it in a differential measurement,
rather than reporting it. Across K, Rb (this programme) and the wider
alkali S-S two-photon literature surveyed here, `zameroski2014` (Rb
5S->7S) remains the one directly measured alkali nS self-broadening
rate, and `lee2010`/`lee2012` (Cs 6S->8S) remain the one line that
carries a measured light shift and a power/pressure Voigt decomposition.

**Where this slots into LITERATURE.md.** Beside `kirankumar2011` in the
"Cross-alkali check" paragraph of section 8: the two K papers together,
not `kirankumar2011` alone, are what establishes that no measured S-S
environmental coefficient exists across the same-family alkali
literature surveyed here, leaving `lee2010`/`lee2012` as the sole
exception.
