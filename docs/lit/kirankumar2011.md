# kirankumar2011

**Read here 2026-08-03.** Verbatim abstract: "Hyperfine splittings for the
39K and 41K isotopes and the isotope shift for the 4s 2S1/2 → 6s 2S1/2
two-photon transition have been measured using sub-Doppler fluorescence
spectroscopy. We have accurately measured the transition isotope shift, and
a discrepancy has been found in the earlier reported transition isotope
shift value, which has been corrected. The resulting hyperfine splitting
values of 418.2 (5) and 230.4 (1.3) MHz for the 39K and 41K isotopes
respectively are in agreement with previous measurements. The precision of
the absolute frequency values for the two-photon transition has been
improved by about two orders. Our measured magnetic dipole constants for
the upper 6 2S1/2 state for the 39K and the 41K isotopes are consistent
with the earlier measured values."

## What it does

Natural-abundance potassium vapour (93.26% ³⁹K, 6.73% ⁴¹K) in a sealed
pyrex cell heated to about 250 °C (350 °C for the weaker ⁴¹K lines), excited
on the forbidden 4s ²S₁/₂ → 6s ²S₁/₂ two-photon line at 728.58 nm per photon
with a cw Ti:Sapphire laser, retro-reflected for Doppler-free excitation,
detected via cascade fluorescence at 405 nm. The laser scan is calibrated
by passing the beam through a broadband electro-optic modulator (EOM) and
reading the equally spaced sideband peaks, which removes the nonlinearity
of a swept reference cavity. The wavemeter used for the absolute-frequency
axis is itself calibrated against the Rb 5S₁/₂ → 7S₁/₂ two-photon transition
at 394 397 906.983 kHz (a frequency-comb value from a separate group).
Laser power density at the cell was about 636 W/cm² (200 mW over a ~200 µm
beam diameter).

## Key numbers

- ³⁹K hyperfine splitting (F=2→F′=2 to F=1→F′=1): **418.2 (5) MHz**, against
  a prior 420(3) MHz.
- ⁴¹K hyperfine splitting: **230.4 (1.3) MHz**, against a prior 232(8) MHz.
- Transition isotope shift (⁴¹K − ³⁹K): **500.8 (6) MHz**, about 19 MHz
  lower than the previously reported 520(3) MHz, which the paper states is
  corrected here.
- Absolute frequency (centre of gravity): ³⁹K 411 475 860 ± 10 MHz, ⁴¹K
  411 476 106 ± 10 MHz, a precision improvement of about two orders over
  the prior ±2 GHz wavemeter measurement.
- Magnetic dipole constant A(6²S₁/₂): ³⁹K 21.8(5) MHz against an atomic-beam
  magnetic-resonance value of 21.81(18) MHz, ⁴¹K 11.8(1.3) MHz against
  12.03(40) MHz.
- Observed two-photon linewidths span 3.5 to 5 MHz FWHM (average 4.7 MHz),
  with a natural-broadening contribution estimated at about 2.04 MHz.
- Total systematic uncertainty 212 kHz against a statistical uncertainty of
  480 kHz (³⁹K hyperfine splitting), overall uncertainty 525 kHz.

## BRIDGES: the nearest same-family alkali S-S two-photon measurement, and what it does not carry

This is the closest same-family alkali reference this repository holds for
an nS two-photon transition to a higher S state measured by isotope shift
and hyperfine structure rather than by lineshape systematics. One precise
correction to the framing: potassium's ground state is 4s, so 4s→6s skips
5s and is a Δn=2 transition, the same class as Zameroski's Rb 5S→7S
(`zameroski2014`), not a Δn=1 analogue of this programme's Rb 5S→6S line.
It is same-family by transition TYPE (a forbidden, parity-allowed two-photon
S→S line in an alkali vapour cell), not by principal-quantum-number spacing.

**What it measures.** Hyperfine splitting, isotope shift, absolute
transition frequency and the excited-state magnetic dipole constant A, all
to kHz-to-sub-MHz precision, using a differential method (line-centre
separations) that cancels common-mode systematics by construction.

**What it does NOT measure.** No density-dependent or intensity-dependent
environmental coefficient appears anywhere in the paper as a measured
result. The three candidate mechanisms Section 3.5 considers are all
handled as corrections to be bounded and subtracted, never as a
reported physics number:
- **AC Stark shift**: CALCULATED from Eq. (2) using known Rabi frequencies
  at the one fixed power density used (636 W/cm²), giving −212 kHz. Not
  measured as a slope against laser power or intensity, and never scanned.
- **Pressure (collisional) broadening**: not measured in this experiment at
  all. It is an ESTIMATE, "about 60 kHz", obtained "by extrapolation from
  the experimental study by Hughes and Lloyd on pressure effects of
  homogeneous K vapour" (their ref [41], a 1937 absorption study unrelated
  to the 6S state or to two-photon spectroscopy). No self-broadening rate
  or pressure-shift coefficient is fit from this paper's own data, and
  vapour density is not varied as an independent variable.
- **Blackbody-radiation-induced dynamic Stark shift**: CALCULATED at 250 °C
  from published dynamic polarizabilities (their ref [42]), −1.89 kHz.

Section 3.5 states the reason directly: "Since in the present study, only
the difference in frequencies is being measured, the systematic errors get
cancelled." The apparatus and analysis are built specifically so that
density- and intensity-dependent shifts do not need to be characterised.

**Where this leaves the scoping claim.** The claim that S-S environmental
coefficients are largely unmeasured across the alkalis holds up against
this paper's actual content. Even the nearest same-family alkali S-S
two-photon measurement, whose only job is isotope shift and hyperfine
structure, treats every density- and intensity-dependent shift as a
subtracted nuisance rather than a result, and borrows its only
pressure-broadening number from an eighty-year-old absorption study on a
different transition. This strengthens rather than merely repeats the
scoping already built on `zameroski2014` (the one directly measured
alkali nS self-broadening rate, on Rb 7S) and `lee2010`/`lee2012` (the
Cs 6S→8S line, which does measure a light shift and a power/pressure
Voigt decomposition and is the outlier, not the rule).

**Where this slots into LITERATURE.md.** It belongs beside `lee2010` and
`lee2012` in the "Positioning this analysis" paragraph of §8: those two
are the closest analogue that DOES carry environmental coefficients
(light shift, Voigt widths against power and pressure), and
`kirankumar2011` is the companion data point showing that outcome is not
the norm for same-family alkali S-S two-photon work. It also belongs
beside `zameroski2014` in §2 as a second confirmation that a directly
measured self-broadening or self-shift coefficient for an alkali nS state
is rare enough that Zameroski's Rb 7S number stands almost alone.
