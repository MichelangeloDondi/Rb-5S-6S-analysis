## What page 1 gives, verbatim

"Trapping a Rydberg atom close to a surface is an important step towards the
realisation of many proposals of quantum information or hybrid quantum
systems. One of the challenges in these experiments is to overcome the
electric field emanating from contaminations on the surface. Here we report
on measurements of an electric field created by 87Rb atoms absorbed on a
25 nm thick layer of SiO2, covering a 90 nm layer of Au. The electric field is
measured using a two-photon transition to the 23D5/2 and 25S1/2 state. The
electric field value that we measure is higher than typical values measured
above metal surfaces, but is consistent with other measurements above SiO2
surfaces. In addition, we measure the temporal behaviour of the field and
observe that we can reduce it in a single experimental cycle, using UV light
or by mildly heating the surface, whereas the buildup of the field takes
thousands of cycles. We explain these results by a change in the ad-atoms
distribution on the surface. These results indicate that the stray electric
field can be reduced, opening new possibilities for experiments with trapped
Rydberg atoms near surfaces."

## Use in this record

Directly on point for the search priority's item 5 (surface electric fields /
adsorbate fields for Rydberg atoms near a dielectric surface) and for the
strategic document's own list of what the OIST nanofibre programme keeps from
a collaboration: a per-run calibration of the surface-charge term at 6S
sensitivity, an atom-based monitor of adsorbate dynamics on the fibre
(this record's own aim statement, corrected 2026-09-22 from
`private/STRATEGY_DISCUSSION_2026-09-21.md`, which does not contain this
phrasing or that section heading).
This paper is a silica-on-metal geometry, not a bare silica nanofibre,
but it is a clean, quantitative precedent for exactly that atom-based
monitoring idea: a two-photon Rydberg transition used as a field probe, with
a measured asymmetry between fast (UV/heat) discharge and slow (thousands of
cycles) rebuild. It sits beside the already-held patrick2025 (surface-charge
imaging in Rydberg vapor cells) and raj2026 (surface charges on a
nanowaveguide) as an older, non-OIST-group anchor for the same phenomenon,
and beside the newly-held hattermann2012 as the pair documenting both the
buildup-rate side and the active-mitigation side of the same physics.
