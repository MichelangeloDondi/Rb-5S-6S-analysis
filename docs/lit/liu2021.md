## Abstract, verbatim

"Many applications of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond crucially rely
on a spectrally narrow and stable optical zero-phonon line transition.
Though many impressive proof-of-principle experiments have been
demonstrated, much work remains in engineering NV centers with spectral
properties that are sufficiently robust for practical implementation. To
elucidate the mechanisms underlying their interactions with the
environment, we apply multi-dimensional coherent spectroscopy to an NV
center ensemble in bulk diamond at cryogenic temperatures. Our spectra
reveal thermal dephasing due to quasi-localized vibrational modes as well as
ultrafast spectral diffusion on the picosecond timescale. The intrinsic,
ensemble-averaged homogeneous linewidth is found to be in the tens of GHz
range by extrapolating to zero temperature. We also observe a
temperature-dependent Stark splitting of the excited state manifold,
relevant to NV sensing protocols."

## What it does

Type Ib bulk monocrystalline diamond, NV- density 1-2 ppm, mounted on a
cold-finger cryostat. Three approximately 90 fs pulses from an optical
parametric amplifier (250 kHz repetition rate) are focused in a box geometry
onto the 637 nm zero-phonon line to generate a photon-echo four-wave-mixing
(FWM) signal, heterodyne-detected against a local-oscillator pulse routed
around the sample. Fourier-transforming the echo along two of the three
delays gives a one-quantum multi-dimensional coherent spectroscopy (MDCS)
spectrum, in which the diagonal direction carries the inhomogeneous
strain-broadened linewidth and the orthogonal cross-diagonal direction
carries the homogeneous dephasing linewidth. Because NV centers sit in a
regime of moderate rather than dominant inhomogeneous broadening, the two
directions are fit jointly, not read off independently.

The homogeneous dephasing rate gamma, tracked from 6 K to 140 K, rises
exponentially with temperature. That rise is fit to a single localized
phonon-mode occupation model (their eq 1), which separates an intrinsic
zero-temperature rate gamma0 from a thermally activated amplitude gamma*
with characteristic phonon energy Eph. A second measurement holds
temperature fixed and low while varying the waiting time T between 1 ps and
2 ns, and finds gamma growing linearly with T at two sample locations, an
ultrafast spectral-diffusion channel distinct from the thermal one. A third
measurement integrates the photon-echo field over the delay tau instead of
resolving it in two dimensions, and finds two dephasing time constants
rather than one, a signature of non-Markovian dephasing at short delay. Last,
diagonal slices of the 2D spectrum develop two resolved lobes as temperature
rises, modeled as two fixed-width Gaussian orbital sub-distributions with
temperature-dependent centers, attributed to an internal electric field
that Stark-splits the excited-state orbital doublet.

## Key numbers

- Homogeneous dephasing fit (their eq 1, resonance energy 1945 meV):
  gamma0 = 37.31 GHz (T2 = 1/gamma0 = 26.8 ps), gamma* = 7890 GHz,
  Eph = 34.41 meV, the last matching an independently reported 35 meV
  vibronic splitting.
- Ultrafast spectral diffusion: gamma grows at 1.98 MHz per ps and 1.59 MHz
  per ps (two sample spots) as the waiting time T runs from 1 ps to 2 ns.
- Integrated FWM: two dephasing times differing by more than 10 ps at the
  lowest temperature measured, converging as temperature rises.
- Bimodal inhomogeneous splitting: two Gaussians of fixed widths
  sigma1 = 2.6 meV (627.8 GHz) and sigma2 = 2.3 meV (555.4 GHz), fitted
  center separation about 5 meV (40 per cm), read as an internal electric
  field of 0.29 MV per cm at 50 K rising to 0.43 MV per cm at 140 K via a
  reported transverse susceptibility of 1.4 MHz per V per cm.
- ZPL center energy 1946 meV (637 nm). The excitation-pulse bandwidth
  (about 15 meV half-width) sits below the lowest reported vibronic mode
  energy (22.6 meV), so the ZPL is addressed without exciting phonon
  sidebands.

## LINEAGE BRIDGES: an independent, solid-state route to the same distribution-from-lineshape idea

This programme reads a perturbation distribution off a lineshape in the
Delone lineage (`delone1980.md`). The AC-Stark shift distribution set by a
beam's known transverse intensity profile is convolved with a homogeneous
Lorentzian linewidth to produce a single, linearly recorded lineshape, and
the distribution's cumulants are then recovered by fitting that forward
model. The distribution's functional form is assumed from geometry, not
measured, and the homogeneous and inhomogeneous contributions are separated
only afterward, inside the fit.

Liu 2021 resolves the same underlying problem, a homogeneous dynamical rate
sitting inside an inhomogeneous strain-and-field distribution, by a
different route. Their four-wave-mixing MDCS technique places the two
contributions on orthogonal axes of a two-dimensional spectrum (diagonal for
inhomogeneous, cross-diagonal for homogeneous), a direct consequence of the
third-order nonlinear response and the photon-echo phase-rewinding it
exploits. Nothing about the shape of the inhomogeneous distribution needs
to be assumed beforehand. The separation is a property of the nonlinear
measurement itself, not of a fitted forward model, and it holds for an
arbitrary strain distribution rather than only for one whose form is
already known.

That makes Liu 2021 an independent, solid-state invention of the same idea
Delone stated for atoms in a fluctuating field: a measured lineshape carries
a perturbation distribution, and the two can be pulled apart. NV centers
replace atoms, strain and internal electric field replace laser intensity,
and nonlinear multidimensional spectroscopy replaces the forward-model
inversion of a linear profile this programme uses. The bimodal Stark
splitting resolved in their section 2.4 is the closest structural analogue
here: an inhomogeneous distribution that turns out to be two displaced
sub-populations rather than one smooth profile, a possibility a single
closed-form P(s) of the kind this programme fits does not itself entertain.

**Where this does not transfer.** Liu 2021's method needs a coherent
third-order nonlinear signal and femtosecond pulses, tools this programme's
continuous-wave two-photon spectroscopy does not have and does not need,
because the geometric distribution here is known a priori and does not have
to be measured independently. The two are not competing solutions to the
same experimental problem. They are two different proofs that the same
inverse-problem structure recurs whenever an ensemble lineshape mixes a
homogeneous rate with an inhomogeneous perturbation distribution, once in
cold atomic vapor and once in a solid-state defect ensemble.
