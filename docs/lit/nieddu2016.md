## What it says, in its own terms

This is a topical review, not a new measurement: it surveys the state of optical-nanofibre (ONF) work with neutral atoms as of early 2016. Its opening argument for why an ONF is useful at all is the standard one -- a tightly focused free-space beam is limited to an interaction length of order the Rayleigh length, $L_R = \pi w_0^2/\lambda$, so a 780 nm beam focused to a 1 um waist interacts with atoms over only about 4 um. The review then develops the mode-propagation theory of a step-index optical fibre from Maxwell's equations down to the eigenvalue conditions for the four guided-mode families (HE, EH, TE, TM), as background for readers coming from atomic physics rather than fibre optics.

The survey portion (not read in the pages covered here) is organized around the historical progression the abstract states explicitly: early work using ONFs to probe atomic vapours and cold atoms, the achievement of ONF-based cold-atom traps, the demonstration of strong coupling between a single trapped atom and an ONF-based fibre cavity (built by splicing an ONF between two fibre-Bragg-grating mirrors), and more recent nonlinear-optics results such as electromagnetically-induced transparency and slow light in atomic media coupled to ONFs. The review closes by pointing toward higher-order-mode ONFs, which can carry orbital angular momentum, as a direction for future atom-photon hybrid quantum systems.

## What it is worth here

This is background literature for the optical-nanofibre addendum rather than a source of numbers for the vapour-cell programme: it is a general-purpose survey of the ONF-atom field from the same OIST unit that later built the 993 nm two-photon apparatus this record's own ONF sections describe, predating the group's own 5S-6S nanofibre work. Its worked Rayleigh-range argument is a clean, quotable motivation for why a guided-mode geometry helps in the higher-intensity, longer-interaction-length regime this record's fibre arm considers, but the review carries no measurement, apparatus parameter or number specific to the transition or apparatus this record analyses.
