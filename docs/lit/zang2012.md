# zang2012

**Read 2026-07-29** (arXiv v2, held). Peking University: magic wavelengths for
trapping in a four-level Rb *active* optical clock.

**The near-coincidence, stated plainly because a referee will find it.** Zang
scans the differential shift between **6s₁/₂ and 5p₁/₂** over 550–650 nm and
1200–1600 nm and finds two magic wavelengths in the infrared band, **1342 nm
and 1421 nm**. This programme's computed 5S–6S magic wavelengths are 1203.9 /
1287.9 / **1339.6** nm. The third sits **2.4 nm** from Zang's 1342.

They are different magic conditions. Zang cancels the differential
polarizability of the **6s–5p** pair, dominated in that region by the
5p₁/₂–8s₁/₂ and 5p₁/₂–6d₃/₂ couplings; this work cancels it for the **5s–6s**
pair. Both involve 6S, which is why the roots fall near one another — the same
6S polarizability structure drives both — but neither number is the other, and
a trap at 1342 nm would not hold 5S and 6S equally.

**Consequence for the claim.** "First 5S–6S magic wavelengths" is correct and
survives, but it must be written with the state pair explicit and this paper
cited as the nearest prior work in the band. An unqualified "first magic
wavelengths in Rb near 1340 nm" would be wrong. The proximity is worth one
sentence rather than silence: it is the kind of thing that looks like an
oversight if the reader finds it first.
