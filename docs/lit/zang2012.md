# zang2012

**VERIFIED, 2026-07-29**, arXiv v2 (no published version located).

**The system.** A four-level Rb active-optical-clock scheme, computing
magic trapping wavelengths for the fine-structure pair 6s₁/₂-5p₁/₂,₃/₂
(m_j-resolved for 5p₃/₂, their Figs. 4-5), scanned over 550-650 nm and
1200-1600 nm.

**The numbers.** The conclusion lists four magic wavelengths for
6s₁/₂-5p₁/₂ (595, 612, 1342, 1421 nm) and seven for 6s₁/₂-5p₃/₂ (575, 610,
624, 1331, 1336, 1453, 1461 nm). Table I gives the 5p₁/₂-6s₁/₂ resonance at
1323.88 nm and the 5p₃/₂-6s₁/₂ resonance at 1366.87 nm. The visible roots
near 595 and 612 nm are attributed to the 5p₁/₂-8s₁/₂ and 5p₁/₂-6d₃/₂
couplings, which together contribute less than 0.639% to the 5p₁/₂
polarizability. The near-resonant infrared terms are 5p₁/₂-6s₁/₂
(1323.88 nm) and 5p₁/₂-4d₃/₂ (1475.65 nm).

**Validity.** Any magic wavelength from a 6S-involving differential
polarizability must fall between the 1323.88 and 1366.87 nm poles, so every
candidate in that band, theirs at 1331, 1336, and 1342 nm, is confined to
the same 43 nm window. Proximity between roots there is set by the level
structure, not by two calculations computing the same quantity. The
paper's magic condition is for the 6s-5p differential polarizability, a
different state pair from 5s-6s, so a trap at 1342 nm would not hold 5S and
6S equally.

**Use in this record.** Six of the paper's magic wavelengths fall inside
the 1200-1600 nm band. The computed 5S-6S values are 1203.9, 1287.9, and
1339.6 nm. The 1339.6 nm value falls between the paper's 1336 nm (3.6 nm
below) and 1342 nm (2.4 nm above), rather than near a single isolated root.
This is the nearest prior magic-wavelength work in the band, for a
different state pair, so the claim of the first 5S-6S magic wavelengths
stands.
