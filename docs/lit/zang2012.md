# zang2012

**Read from the held arXiv v2.** Peking University: magic wavelengths for
trapping in a four-level Rb *active* optical clock.

**The near-coincidence, stated plainly.** Zang
scans the differential shift between **6s₁/₂ and 5p₁/₂,₃/₂** — both fine-structure
partners, $m_j$-resolved for 5p₃/₂ (their Figs. 4–5) — over 550–650 nm and
1200–1600 nm. Their conclusion lists **four** magic wavelengths for 6s₁/₂–5p₁/₂
(595, 612, **1342**, **1421** nm) and **seven** for 6s₁/₂–5p₃/₂ (575, 610, 624,
**1331**, **1336**, **1453**, **1461** nm).

*This note first recorded only 1342 and 1421, which understated the problem.*
Six of those roots are in the 1200–1600 nm band, and this programme's computed
5S–6S values are 1203.9 / 1287.9 / **1339.6** nm — so **1339.6 is bracketed**,
3.6 nm above Zang's 1336 and 2.4 nm below their 1342, rather than sitting near a
single isolated root.

**Why that is less alarming than it looks, and the reason is structural.** From
their Table I, the 5p₁/₂–6s₁/₂ resonance is at **1323.88 nm** and the
5p₃/₂–6s₁/₂ resonance at **1366.87 nm**. Any magic wavelength arising from a
6S-involving differential must fall between those poles, so every candidate —
theirs at 1331, 1336, 1342 and ours at 1339.6 — is squeezed into the same 43 nm
window. Few-nm separations there are **forced by the level structure**, not
evidence that two calculations are computing the same thing.

They remain different magic conditions: Zang cancels the differential
polarizability of a **6s–5p** pair, this work of the **5s–6s** pair, and a trap
at 1342 nm would not hold 5S and 6S equally. *A mechanism attribution has been
removed here:* this note previously credited the infrared roots to the
5p₁/₂–8s₁/₂ and 5p₁/₂–6d₃/₂ couplings, but the paper attaches those to its
**visible** roots near 595 and 612 nm and states that 8s₁/₂ and 6d₃/₂ together
contribute "less than 0.639%" to the 5p₁/₂ polarizability. The near-resonant
terms in the infrared are 5p₁/₂–6s₁/₂ (1323.88 nm) and 5p₁/₂–4d₃/₂ (1475.65 nm).

**Consequence for the claim.** "First 5S–6S magic wavelengths" is correct and
survives, but it must be written with the state pair explicit and this paper
cited as the nearest prior work in the band. An unqualified "first magic
wavelengths in Rb near 1340 nm" would be wrong. The proximity is worth one
sentence rather than silence: it is the kind of thing that looks like an
oversight if the reader finds it first.
