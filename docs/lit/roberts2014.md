## What it says, in its own terms

The review surveys the search for parity (P) and time-reversal (T) violation in atomic, molecular and nuclear systems as a low-energy complement to direct high-energy tests of the Standard Model. It opens with atomic parity nonconservation (PNC), caused by the electroweak interaction -- Z-boson exchange between electrons and the nucleus, or P-violating forces internal to the nucleus -- and manifested in P-violating atomic observables. It identifies caesium as the atom that has received the most experimental and theoretical attention, because a precise measurement of the 6S-7S PNC amplitude, combined with sufficiently accurate atomic-structure calculations, yields the caesium nuclear weak charge, an electron-nucleus weak coupling constant that stands as the most precise low-energy Standard Model test of its kind, and it notes that the resulting value differs from the Standard Model prediction by 1.5 standard deviations -- a discrepancy the review describes as reasonable agreement but still worth further investigation.

The introduction frames three further active areas built on the same atomic-PNC toolkit: measuring PNC across a chain of isotopes of one element, where uncertain atomic-structure calculations largely cancel in the ratio because the electronic structure is essentially unchanged between isotopes, nuclear anapole moments, a parity-violating, time-reversal-conserving nuclear moment sourced by parity-violating forces inside the nucleus, measured so far in only one experiment, and PNC in molecules. The review's other major theme, previewed in its table of contents but not reached in the pages read here, is the search for permanent electric dipole moments in atoms, molecules and nuclei as a time-reversal-violation signature, together with a section on using atoms to search for dark-matter axions.

## What it is worth here

This is a different field with no shared measurement, transition or apparatus: it concerns weak-interaction-induced parity violation and time-reversal-violation searches in specially chosen heavy atoms -- caesium's PNC role rests on a relativistic enhancement of the weak-interaction matrix element, unrelated to anything in a two-photon 5S-6S lineshape measurement -- not the AC-Stark, collisional or lineshape physics this record's vapour-cell programme addresses. Its relevance here is essentially none beyond general orientation on why precision atomic spectroscopy commands attention as a fundamental-physics tool elsewhere.
