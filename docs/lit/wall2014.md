# wall2014

**Read here 2026-07-26.** Two corrections to the second-hand note this replaces:
the authors are **Wall, Cassidy and Hogan (UCL)** -- an earlier placeholder
listed a different group, invented rather than checked -- and the system is
**helium Rydberg states in electric fields**, not Rb.

**The precedent, verbatim.** "Atoms that interact with the laser beam at a
position close to the focus can experience strong ac Stark shifts, while atoms
located further from the focus experience weaker shifts. The ensemble of atoms
located further from the focus experiences a smaller range of ac Stark shifts,
causing the overall signal from these atoms to 'bunch up' close to the
unperturbed transition frequency, whereas closer to the focus atoms experience
a wider range of intensities, causing the signal from these atoms to be spread
out in frequency. The cumulative effect ... is a spectral line with a maximum
close to the unperturbed transition frequency, and a long tail towards higher
frequencies."

**This is the density-of-states argument that generates a ramp-shaped weight,
and it is closer to this programme than the external audit realised.** The
audit read it as a generic inhomogeneous-broadening precedent; it is in fact
**single-colour two-photon**, so the I^2 intensity weighting -- which this
programme treats as part of its own novelty -- is already present. That makes
it the strongest precedent found, and it must be cited up front.

**Read in full 2026-07-26 (second pass, external). The scoop is real but
narrower than the first reading suggested — cite and distinguish, do not
concede.** The I^2 weighting IS genuinely present: they multiply each
single-atom convolved spectrum by I_laser(x,y,z)^2, justified by the quadratic
dependence of the integrated signal. Five things nonetheless separate it from
the claim made here:

1. **Purely numerical.** The appendix is a per-atom ensemble sum over a
   *measured* 3D intensity map with two parameters tuned inside their error
   bars. No weight function is ever written down.
2. **The inference runs the other way.** Wall computes alpha(2^3S_1) ab initio
   and uses it to *predict* the lineshape. This programme recovers the shift
   *from* the shape. They never extract a polarizability from an asymmetry.
3. **Different geometry.** Their ramp comes from *longitudinal* convergence --
   peak intensity varying along the propagation axis, focus 20 mm past the
   atomic beam, cloud far larger than the beam. Ours comes from the
   *transverse* profile.
4. **Their per-plane line peaks at the MAXIMUM shift, not the minimum**
   (Fig. 13a); only superposition across planes drags the aggregate peak back
   toward the unperturbed position. That is a superposition artifact, and
   structurally unlike a monotone triangular weight.
5. **Different regime.** 21-133 GHz shifts against a 7 GHz bandwidth, i.e.
   shift >> linewidth. This archive sits at sub-MHz shifts against a ~1.1 MHz
   laser width. And Wall had a working frequency reference throughout, which is
   the condition under which this method is *not* the one to use.

So the honest statement is that neither "keep the distribution" nor the I^2
weighting is new, while the closed form, the inversion direction, the
transverse geometry and the reference-free regime are all unclaimed by this
paper.
