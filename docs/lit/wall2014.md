# wall2014

Held. Verified against the PDF.

## The system

Single-colour two-photon excitation of helium Rydberg states in a converging laser beam, in an electric field. The authors are Wall, Cassidy, and Hogan (UCL).

## The mechanism, in the paper's words

"Atoms that interact with the laser beam at a position close to the focus can experience strong ac Stark shifts, while atoms located further from the focus experience weaker shifts. The ensemble of atoms located further from the focus experiences a smaller range of ac Stark shifts, causing the overall signal from these atoms to 'bunch up' close to the unperturbed transition frequency, whereas closer to the focus atoms experience a wider range of intensities, causing the signal from these atoms to be spread out in frequency. The cumulative effect ... is a spectral line with a maximum close to the unperturbed transition frequency, and a long tail towards higher frequencies."

This is a density-of-states argument that produces a ramp-shaped weight. The excitation is single-colour two-photon, so the I^2 intensity weighting is already present, which places this paper closest among prior art to the construction used in this record.

## Differences from the lineshape-inversion method

1. The treatment is purely numerical: a per-atom ensemble sum over a measured 3D intensity map, with two parameters tuned inside their error bars. No weight function is written down analytically.
2. The inference runs from polarizability to lineshape. The paper computes alpha(2^3S_1) ab initio and uses it to predict the lineshape, rather than recovering a shift from an observed asymmetry.
3. The geometry is longitudinal. The ramp comes from convergence along the propagation axis (peak intensity varying along it, focus 20 mm past the atomic beam, cloud much larger than the beam), not from the transverse profile.
4. The per-plane line peaks at the maximum shift, not the minimum (Fig. 13a). Only superposition across planes drags the aggregate peak back toward the unperturbed position, a superposition artifact structurally different from a monotone triangular weight.
5. The regime is 21-133 GHz shifts against a 7 GHz bandwidth, shift much greater than linewidth, with a working frequency reference available throughout, the condition under which recovering a shift from lineshape asymmetry is unnecessary.

The tail in this paper runs toward higher frequency (blue): "a spectral line with a maximum close to the unperturbed transition frequency, and a long tail towards higher frequency." The shape argument, a distribution of shifts with most of its weight at small |s|, is sign-agnostic, but the two constructions point opposite ways since a positive Delta-alpha under the convention used in this record gives a red shift.

The geometry is a converging beam whose focus lies beyond the atomic beam axis, so the distribution is over the axial gradient as well as the transverse one. This is the closest published statement of the three-dimensional version of the argument.

## Validity

Helium Rydberg states, single-colour two-photon excitation. Together with Cs ([wieman1987](wieman1987.md), [camparo1992](camparo1992.md), [lee2010](lee2010.md)), Yb ([stalnaker2006](stalnaker2006.md), [antypas2018](antypas2018.md)), and Rb ([hamilton2023](hamilton2023.md), this work), the AC-Stark-lineshape literature spans four elements and both one- and two-photon excitation. The distinction from this paper is carried by the closed form and the cumulants, not by species.
