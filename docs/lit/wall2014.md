# wall2014

**Read here 2026-07-26.** Two corrections to the second-hand note this replaces:
the authors are **Wall, Cassidy and Hogan (UCL)** -- an earlier placeholder
listed a different group, invented rather than checked -- and the system is
**helium Rydberg states in electric fields**, not Rb.

**The precedent, verbatim.** "Atoms that interact with the laser beam at a
position close to the focus can experience strong ac Stark shifts, while atoms
located further from the focus experience weaker shifts. The ensemble of atoms
located further from the focus experiences a smaller range of ac Stark shifts,
causing the overall signal from these atoms to 'bunch up' close to the
unperturbed transition frequency, whereas closer to the focus atoms experience
a wider range of intensities, causing the signal from these atoms to be spread
out in frequency. The cumulative effect ... is a spectral line with a maximum
close to the unperturbed transition frequency, and a long tail towards higher
frequencies."

**This is the density-of-states argument that generates a ramp-shaped weight,
and it is closer to this programme than the external audit realised.** The
audit read it as a generic inhomogeneous-broadening precedent; it is in fact
**single-colour two-photon**, so the I^2 intensity weighting -- which this
programme treats as part of its own novelty -- is already present. That makes
it the strongest precedent found, and it must be cited up front.

**What is delineated, checked in the text.** The lineshape is described
qualitatively and reproduced by a numerical ensemble average over the
converging beam; there is no closed-form weight, no analytic asymmetry
coefficient, and no attempt to invert the lineshape for the polarizability.
The shifts are Rydberg-state Stark shifts in a converging beam with the
frequency axis intact, not a coefficient recovered when the reference is
unusable. The surviving claim here is the closed form plus the reference-free
inversion, not "keep the distribution" and not "I^2 weighting".
