# wall2014

**Read from the held source.** Two corrections to the second-hand note this replaces:
the authors are **Wall, Cassidy and Hogan (UCL)** -- an earlier placeholder
listed a different group, invented rather than checked -- and the system is
**helium Rydberg states in electric fields**, not Rb.

**The precedent, verbatim.** "Atoms that interact with the laser beam at a
position close to the focus can experience strong ac Stark shifts, while atoms
located further from the focus experience weaker shifts. The ensemble of atoms
located further from the focus experiences a smaller range of ac Stark shifts,
causing the overall signal from these atoms to 'bunch up' close to the
unperturbed transition frequency, whereas closer to the focus atoms experience
a wider range of intensities, causing the signal from these atoms to be spread
out in frequency. The cumulative effect ... is a spectral line with a maximum
close to the unperturbed transition frequency, and a long tail towards higher
frequencies."

**This is the density-of-states argument that generates a ramp-shaped weight.**
It is not a generic inhomogeneous-broadening precedent, as a second-hand reading
had it: the excitation is
**single-colour two-photon**, so the I^2 intensity weighting is already present.
That makes it the closest precedent in this record, and it must be cited up
front.

**Read in full. The overlap is real but
narrower than the first reading suggested.** The I^2 weighting IS genuinely present: they multiply each
single-atom convolved spectrum by I_laser(x,y,z)^2, justified by the quadratic
dependence of the integrated signal. Five things nonetheless separate it from
the claim made here:

1. **Purely numerical.** The appendix is a per-atom ensemble sum over a
   *measured* 3D intensity map with two parameters tuned inside their error
   bars. No weight function is ever written down.
2. **The inference runs the other way.** Wall computes alpha(2^3S_1) ab initio
   and uses it to *predict* the lineshape. This programme recovers the shift
   *from* the shape. They never extract a polarizability from an asymmetry.
3. **Different geometry.** Their ramp comes from *longitudinal* convergence --
   peak intensity varying along the propagation axis, focus 20 mm past the
   atomic beam, cloud far larger than the beam. Ours comes from the
   *transverse* profile.
4. **Their per-plane line peaks at the MAXIMUM shift, not the minimum**
   (Fig. 13a); only superposition across planes drags the aggregate peak back
   toward the unperturbed position. That is a superposition artifact, and
   structurally unlike a monotone triangular weight.
5. **Different regime.** 21-133 GHz shifts against a 7 GHz bandwidth, i.e.
   shift >> linewidth. This archive sits at sub-MHz shifts against a ~1.1 MHz
   laser width. And Wall had a working frequency reference throughout, which is
   the condition under which this method is *not* the one to use.

Neither "keep the distribution" nor the I^2 weighting is new. The closed form,
the inversion direction, the transverse geometry and the reference-free regime
are all unclaimed by this paper.

**Audited against the PDF 2026-07-30 — clean.** The record (Wall, Cassidy &
Hogan, UCL; *PRA* **90**, 053430 (2014); "Single-color two-photon spectroscopy of
Rydberg states in electric fields") and the quoted passage are both exact.

Two things worth adding that the note did not carry:

- **Their tail runs to HIGHER frequency**, i.e. blue: "a spectral line with a
  maximum close to the unperturbed transition frequency, and a long tail towards
  higher frequency". This programme's ramp runs the other way, because
  a positive $\Delta\alpha$ under the pinned convention gives a red shift. The
  *shape* argument is identical and sign-agnostic — a distribution of shifts with
  most of its weight at small $|s|$ — but any sentence comparing the two must say
  which way each tail points, given how much trouble sign conventions have caused
  here.
- **The geometry is a converging beam whose focus lies beyond the atomic beam
  axis**, so their distribution is over the *axial* gradient as well as the
  transverse one. That is M9's territory rather than the idealised transverse-only
  case, and it makes this the closest published statement of the
  three-dimensional version of the argument.

**Species note.** This is **helium** Rydberg states, single-colour two-photon.
Together with Cs ([wieman1987](wieman1987.md), [camparo1992](camparo1992.md),
[lee2010](lee2010.md)), Yb ([stalnaker2006](stalnaker2006.md),
[antypas2018](antypas2018.md)) and Rb ([hamilton2023](hamilton2023.md), this
work), the AC-Stark-lineshape lineage this programme sits in already spans four
elements and both one- and two-photon excitation. The delineation is therefore
**not** carried by species — it is carried by the closed form and the cumulants,
and no cross-species argument is available to widen it.
