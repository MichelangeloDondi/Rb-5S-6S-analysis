# beterov1973

**Read from the held PDF** (introduction, contents and section scan).
I. M. Beterov and R. I. Sokolovskiĭ, Institute of
Semiconductor Physics, Siberian Division, USSR Academy of Sciences,
Novosibirsk, and the Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys. Sov. Phys. Usp.
**16**, 339 (1973), translated from Usp. Fiz. Nauk **110**, 169–190 (June
1973).

## The sentence worth having

On the regime where the shift is small compared with the inhomogeneous width —
which is this programme's regime too:

> "Gas-laser fields are, as a rule, weaker. The Stark shifts produced by them
> are comparable with the natural line width Γ and are much smaller in the
> optical band than the Doppler width kv... Allowance for the **inhomogeneous
> broadening** of the spectral lines assumes then fundamental importance. It is
> curious that the Stark effect **is not masked by the motion of the atoms** and
> becomes manifest quite uniquely in the emission and absorption spectra."

That is a 1973 statement of the general position this programme takes: a light
shift far below the dominant broadening is still readable from the line, and
the inhomogeneity has to be modelled rather than averaged away.

## What it is not — and the distinction is not a small one

**Their inhomogeneity is velocity; ours is spatial intensity.** Every averaging
in the paper is over the atomic velocity distribution, and the structure they
analyse is the Bennett hole/spike produced by velocity-selective saturation in
a strong field. A scan of the text for spatial field structure — beam profile,
transverse intensity distribution, Gaussian mode — returns nothing; the
standing wave is treated the classical way, decomposed into two travelling
waves with opposite Doppler shifts. So the mechanism that generates the ramp
here is absent.

It is also **one-photon** throughout (a probe transition adjacent to a
strongly-driven one, in He–Ne and Ne discharge lines), so the I² weighting that
makes the ramp asymmetric at all does not arise.

**The verbal echo with M19 is an echo, not a result.** Beterov's "not masked by
the motion of the atoms" concerns Doppler averaging failing to erase a
velocity-selective structure; M19 concerns transit averaging failing to erase a
spatial one. Both are reassurances that motion does not wash out a light-shift
signature, and they are different mechanisms with different proofs. Do not
cite one as support for the other.

## On the Russian-language facet — it does not close it

The audit's addendum B recorded that Cyrillic searches could not reach
untranslated *Optika i Spektroskopiya* research articles: "this facet cannot be
closed with the tools used here." **Holding this paper does not change that**,
and the manuscript should not imply otherwise:

- it is a **translated** review, indexed in the ordinary Western corpus, so it
  is not evidence that the untranslated literature is reachable;
- it is about a **different topic** — nonlinear resonance effects in gas-laser
  spectra — and carries essentially no alkali content (one mention in the whole
  text) and no self-broadening coefficients (collisions get the "brief mention"
  its own abstract promises);
- so it says nothing either way about whether a Soviet Rb 6S self-broadening
  measurement exists.

What it *is* good for on that facet is **a bibliographic pointer**: its
reference list is dense with ZhETF, JETP Letters and Usp. Fiz. Nauk entries
from the Novosibirsk (Chebotaev/Letokhov) school, which is where a reader with
eLIBRARY.RU access would start. Record it as the entry point, not as coverage.
