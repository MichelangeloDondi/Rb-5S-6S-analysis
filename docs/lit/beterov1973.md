# beterov1973

Held. Introduction, contents and relevant sections verified against the PDF.

I. M. Beterov and R. I. Sokolovskii, Institute of Semiconductor Physics, Siberian Division, USSR Academy of Sciences, Novosibirsk, and the Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys. Sov. Phys. Usp. 16, 339 (1973), translated from Usp. Fiz. Nauk 110, 169-190 (June 1973).

## The regime

On the regime where the shift is small compared with the inhomogeneous width:

> "Gas-laser fields are, as a rule, weaker. The Stark shifts produced by them are comparable with the natural line width Γ and are much smaller in the optical band than the Doppler width kv... Allowance for the inhomogeneous broadening of the spectral lines assumes then fundamental importance. It is curious that the Stark effect is not masked by the motion of the atoms and becomes manifest quite uniquely in the emission and absorption spectra."

## The system

The inhomogeneity treated throughout is velocity, not spatial intensity. Every averaging in the paper is over the atomic velocity distribution, and the structure analysed is the Bennett hole/spike produced by velocity-selective saturation in a strong field, in He-Ne and Ne discharge lines. The standing wave is decomposed the classical way, into two travelling waves with opposite Doppler shifts, with no treatment of beam profile or transverse intensity distribution. The transitions treated are one-photon throughout, so the intensity-squared weighting that a two-photon line carries does not arise.

## Coverage of the wider Russian-language literature

The paper is a translated review, indexed in the ordinary Western corpus rather than only in the untranslated Soviet literature. It covers a different topic, nonlinear resonance effects in gas-laser spectra, and carries almost no alkali content (one mention in the whole text) and no self-broadening coefficients. Its reference list is dense with ZhETF, JETP Letters and Usp. Fiz. Nauk entries from the Novosibirsk school (Chebotaev, Letokhov), which is where further Soviet-era literature on this topic would be found.

## Use in this record

The paper's statement that the Stark effect is not masked by atomic motion concerns Doppler averaging over velocity. It is not evidence for the analogous claim about transit-time averaging over beam position (M19): the two are different mechanisms with independent proofs.
