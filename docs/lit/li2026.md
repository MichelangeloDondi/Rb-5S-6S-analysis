# li2026

**Magic-wavelength matter-wave interferometry with optical clock states.**
Li, Das, Ma, Zanon-Willette, Lan, Kwong and Wilkowski, arXiv:2608.17576,
submitted 18 August 2026, 27 pages with supplementary information.

## What it does

A dual matter-wave interferometer operates simultaneously on the two states of
the strontium-88 optical clock transition, singlet-S-zero and triplet-P-zero,
driven by Bragg pulses at the 813 nm magic wavelength. The stated design
premise is verbatim that the beam splitters must manipulate different clock
states in the same way, "so that optical internal energy becomes a controlled
degree of freedom rather than a source of systematic phase shifts". At 30 mrad
the differential phase between the two Mach-Zehnder interferometers is
consistent with zero, which bounds state-dependent acceleration in free fall at
the 1e-5 level. The same interferometer then measures state-dependent optical
dipole forces and returns a tune-out wavelength of the metastable
triplet-P-zero state, 478.95(8) nm.

## Why it is held here

Three contacts with this repository, and none of them is a number this bench
uses.

1. **The premise is this record's subject read from the other side.** The paper
   treats the differential light shift between two internal states as a
   systematic to be engineered away with a magic wavelength. This repository
   measures the differential polarizability that sets such a shift, and the
   thesis chapter measures the distribution it acquires over an inhomogeneous
   beam.
2. **A tune-out wavelength is a zero of that same polarizability.**
   `run_polarizability.py` computes the first magic wavelengths of the
   5S-to-6S pair and `run_polarizability_deep.py` carries the intermediate-state
   sum. This paper measures the analogous quantity on an excited clock state.
3. **The closing sentence names the direction**: the platform is offered for
   differential force sensing, excited-state polarizability metrology and
   quantum-clock tests of gravity.

## What it is not

It is not a Taipei result. The corresponding author is at Nanyang
Technological University and the apparatus is the Singapore strontium machine.
The author in question carries three affiliations on it, Nanyang, the Department of Physics at
National Taiwan University, and the Institute of Atomic and Molecular Sciences
at Academia Sinica in Taipei. It is also a preprint, with no journal reference
at the time of reading.

## Caution on the citation

The arXiv identifier is the reliable handle. The record holds no journal
reference for it, and the tune-out value above is the abstract's own and has
not been checked against the body.
