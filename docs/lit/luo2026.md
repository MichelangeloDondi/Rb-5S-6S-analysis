## What it says, in its own terms

The paper computes radiative transition rates for rubidium Rydberg nF states from both spontaneous emission and blackbody-radiation-induced transitions, spanning principal quantum numbers n = 4 to 65 at four temperatures (0, 77, 300 and 520 K). It uses the standard formalism: an Einstein A coefficient built from a reduced electric dipole matrix element for spontaneous decay, and a blackbody-induced rate obtained by weighting the same A coefficients with the Planck thermal photon occupation number, summed separately over downward and upward transitions so that both stimulated emission and thermally driven absorption into higher levels are counted. Energy levels for the lowest n come from the NIST Atomic Spectra Database. Everything else comes from a quantum-defect model.

Its physical motivation is that the nF series, despite an almost hydrogen-like quantum defect (delta_F about 0.02, far smaller than delta_S, delta_P or delta_D), decays predominantly into the distinctly non-hydrogenic nD series, so its radiative lifetime cannot be read off a simple hydrogenic formula. The introduction traces the experimental history of Rydberg-lifetime measurements in Rb -- selective-field-ionization studies of the nS, nP and nD series by several groups, and one all-optical method -- and notes that the nF series in particular has had only one dedicated experimental lifetime study to date, whose original analysis did not include blackbody-induced transitions at all: those were discovered separately the same year and only folded in afterward as a correction that the original study's own authors described as small for F levels between n = 10 and 21.

The headline quantitative result -- 24.6% of the total 21F7/2 decay rate at 520 K coming from blackbody-induced transitions -- is presented as evidence that an earlier, 1980, theoretical estimate of the blackbody correction for F states was too low. The calculation is checked for internal consistency against available theoretical and experimental data for the better-studied nS, nP and nD series before being applied to the F states themselves.

## What it is worth here

The methodology -- an Einstein-A spontaneous-emission rate combined with a Planck-weighted blackbody-induced rate, summed over upward and downward channels -- is the same general apparatus that any careful treatment of blackbody-radiation depopulation in an alkali excited state would use, and this record's own twin already carries a blackbody-radiation term. But the paper's own scope is Rydberg nF states (n up to 65, orbital angular momentum l=3), which is a different regime from the low-lying 6S1/2 state this programme actually measures: neither the n-range nor the angular-momentum channel transfers directly, and no number here is reusable without a fresh calculation for 6S. Its value is as a landscape reference for how blackbody-correction sizes are estimated and checked in current Rb Rydberg work, not as a source of a number this record can cite for its own transition.
