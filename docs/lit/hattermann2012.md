## What page 1 gives, verbatim

"We observe the shift of Rydberg levels of rubidium close to a copper surface
when atomic clouds are repeatedly deposited on it. We measure transition
frequencies of rubidium to S and D Rydberg states with principal quantum
numbers n between 31 and 48 using the technique of electromagnetically
induced transparency. The spectroscopic measurement shows a strong increase
of electric fields towards the surface that evolves with the deposition of
atoms. Starting with a clean surface, we measure the evolution of
electrostatic fields in the range between 30 and 300 μm from the surface. We
find that after the deposition of a few hundred atomic clouds, each
containing ∼10⁶ atoms, the field of adsorbates reaches 1 V/cm for a distance
of 30 μm from the surface. This evolution of the electrostatic field sets
serious limitations on cavity QED experiments proposed for Rydberg atoms on
atom chips."

Its own framing of what it adds beyond two named prior studies, verbatim:
"First, McGuirk et al. and Obrecht et al. studied the electrostatic field of
adsorbed atoms on both conducting and insulating surfaces [9, 10]. Second,
Tauschinsky et al. measured electrostatic fields of adsorbates using
electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) on Rydberg states [11]. The
results presented in this article complement the data published by these two
groups."

## Use in this record

The quantitative anchor beneath naber2016 (held above): where Naber measures
the fast-discharge/slow-rebuild asymmetry on a silica-on-gold surface, this
paper is the earlier, metal-surface measurement that puts a number and a rate
on the buildup itself (~10⁶ atoms per cycle, a few hundred cycles to 1 V/cm at
30 μm) and states plainly why it matters: "serious limitations on cavity QED
experiments proposed for Rydberg atoms on atom chips," the same class of
limitation the strategy document names for Rydberg atoms near an ONF
(`private/STRATEGY_DISCUSSION_2026-09-21.md`: surface potentials near a
nanofibre for Rydberg atoms, a priority for the OIST nanofibre programme).
Independent of the OIST/ONF lineage
already held in depth (rajasree2020, vylegzhanin, patrick2025, raj2026), this
is a different apparatus family (atom chip, not nanofibre) converging on the
same physics, which is exactly the kind of cross-platform, cross-group
confirmation the search was asked to find.
