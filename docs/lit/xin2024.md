# xin2024

Held. Verified against the PDF on 2026-09-11.

## The result

The abstract reports the creation of a quantum gas by cooling individual
rubidium atoms pinned in a three-dimensional optical lattice, verbatim, "using
electromagnetically induced transparency and adiabatic expansion", and that,
verbatim, "After just 10 ms of cooling" the phase transition from a thermal to
a quantum gas was verified by transfer into optical dipole traps, with the
collapse of the sample in three dimensions as the signature.

## What this record takes from it

Not degeneracy. A quantum gas is far colder than a two-photon linewidth
measurement needs, and its atom number is far smaller than a warm cell's.

The transferable number is the preparation time. The whole cost of a cold
platform here is atoms per shot, four to six orders below the cell, and that
cost is only repaid by repeating. Ten milliseconds of cooling on top of a trap
load is a duty cycle this record's forecast can carry, and it bounds the
argument that cold preparation is too slow to make up the atom deficit. The
argument that survives is about the trap *loading* time and the atom number, not
about the cooling.
