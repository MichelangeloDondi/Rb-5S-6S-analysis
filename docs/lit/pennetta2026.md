# pennetta2026

Held. Verified against the published PDF.

## The system

Hybrid nanofibre trap for cold cesium on a 450 nm tapered fibre (Rauschenbeutel group): attraction from Casimir-Polder and surface-charge electrostatic forces, repulsion from a blue-detuned evanescent field. Loaded 96(5)% from a 935/685 nm two-colour magic trap into a trap of depth about 1 µK, with storage time 140(9) ms, Ramsey coherence T2* = 17.8(7) ms and spin-echo coherence T2' = 44.7(2.5) ms.

## Findings

Quantifies the atom-surface forces, including Casimir-Polder content absent from gokhroo2022, and identifies the trapping-light differential light shift as the coherence-limiting mechanism, addressed by low-light trapping. Cesium, D2 qubit, 450 nm fibre. The physics (force balance, coherence limits) transfers to other platforms. The numbers do not.

## Read at the PDF (2026-09-22): the two traps, side by side

The journal version was received on 5 December 2025 and accepted on 9 June 2026. Its arXiv v1, 2509.17767v1, lists five
authors and a Ramsey time of 16.8(2) ms, and the numbers below are the journal's. The paper has two traps, and their numbers are not to
be mixed:

| trap | depth | trap minimum from the surface | frequencies | T2* | T'2 (spin echo) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| standard two-colour (P_blue 8.1 mW, P_red 55 uW) | about 22 uK | about 280 nm | 53, 30 and 78 kHz (radial, azimuthal, axial, calculated) | 1.6(1) ms | 12.3(1.0) ms |
| hybrid (red switched off) | about 1 uK (model) | about 650 nm (model) | about 7 and 1 kHz (radial, azimuthal, model) | 17.8(7) ms | 44.7(2.5) ms |

In the standard trap the reversible coherence is limited by motional-state-dependent differential light shifts, proportional to
the trapping intensity (p. 4). In the hybrid trap T'2 is no longer limited by differential light shifts and is attributed to
magnetic-field fluctuations of 3(1) mG (pp. 4 and 8). The attractive potential is fitted as Casimir-Polder plus an electrostatic
term from 19(3) surface charges per micrometre (p. 3), and the introduction cites surface-charge work, including two Rydberg papers,
for the electrostatic part only.
