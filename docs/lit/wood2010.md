## The method

The paper's stated problem: for a chaotic or near-chaotic dynamic model, minute changes in the noise
realization or in the parameters cause drastic changes in the system trajectory, and this sensitivity
to history is inherited and amplified by the joint probability density of the data and the process
noise, so that density, though a valid likelihood in principle, is useless as a basis for measuring
fit (p. 1102), and is shown empirically rough as a function of a single noise deviate or of a model
parameter (Fig. 1b, c, p. 1102, checked against the rendered page). Wood's fix is to reduce the raw
data to a vector of summary statistics, "phase-insensitive summary statistics, quantifying local
dynamic structure and the distribution of observations" (abstract, p. 1102), and build the
likelihood on that vector instead of on the raw data directly.

The construction (p. 1103, checked against the rendered page):

1. Assume the statistics vector s is multivariate normal, `s ~ N(mu_theta, Sigma_theta)` (equation
   2). mu_theta and Sigma_theta are themselves intractable functions of the parameter vector theta.
2. At any trial theta, simulate N_r replicate data sets from the model, convert each to a replicate
   statistics vector by the same transform used on the real data, and take the sample mean and sample
   covariance of the replicates as the estimates mu-hat_theta and Sigma-hat_theta = SS^T/(N_r - 1).
3. The log synthetic likelihood (the paper's own un-numbered display equation immediately after
   equation 2) is then exactly the log of that Gaussian, evaluated at the *observed* statistics vector
   s and with the additive normalizing constant dropped:
   `l_s(theta) = -1/2 (s - mu-hat_theta)^T Sigma-hat_theta^-1 (s - mu-hat_theta) - 1/2 log|Sigma-hat_theta|`.

So the likelihood is Gaussian in the summary statistics by explicit construction, not as an
after-the-fact approximation bolted on. Wood's Theoretical properties of the method subsection
(Methods, PDF p. 4, right column) justifies the assumed normal form as the Laplace approximation to
the true, unknown density of s about its mode (equations 5 and 6, checked against the rendered page),
and argues l_s is then a valid likelihood for theta: the maximizer theta-hat is a consistent
estimator, with asymptotic covariance equal to the inverse Hessian of l_s (equation 7, same page,
checked against the rendered page), as the raw sample size n tends to infinity, but
without a guarantee of full asymptotic efficiency: Wood's own stated qualifier is that theta-hat is
guaranteed asymptotically normal only if Sigma_theta0 is itself normal, or if the number of statistics
used is large (Methods, PDF p. 5, left column, the paragraph immediately following equation 7).

How many simulations per trial point (N_r)? The paper gives exactly one explicit figure: N_r = 500,
for the illustrative Ricker-map panel (Fig. 1d caption, p. 1103). No N_r is stated in the main text
for the blowfly application: only that the MCMC chains ran for 50,000 *iterations* per replicate
(p. 1104), which is the chain length (the number of proposed theta values), a different count from
N_r (the number of simulated data sets used to estimate mu-hat_theta and Sigma-hat_theta at each
proposed theta). The Methods text points more than once to a separate Supplementary Information file for MCMC and
example-specific detail (Methods intro, PDF p. 4) and for code (Methods Summary, p. 1104). That file
is not part of this held PDF.

Methods also describes two refinements, both on PDF p. 5 and both about Sigma-hat_theta, not
about the definition of l_s: a Campbell-style robust covariance estimator that downweights simulated
replicates whose Mahalanobis distance from mu-hat_theta is large, via an iterative QR-based recipe.
And, separately, a tempered Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability (scaling the log-likelihood
difference by a factor gamma < 1 before exponentiating, equivalently raising the ordinary acceptance
ratio to the power gamma) offered as an alternative way to stop the MCMC chain sticking where the
tails of the statistics' distribution make l_s locally irregular.

## Applications

Two examples, both worked on PDF pp. 1-3 (journal pp. 1102-1104, equations 1, 3 and 4 in the table
below checked against the rendered page):

| example | data | model | statistics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ricker map (illustrative) | simulated, Poisson-sampled (log(r)=3.8, sigma=0.3, phi=10) | equation (1), N_(t+1) = r N_t exp(-N_t + e_t) | autocovariances to lag 5, cubic regression of the ordered first differences y_t - y_(t-1) on the observed values, two coefficients of a power-transformed autoregression, the mean population, and the number of zeros observed |
| Nicholson's blowflies (main result) | real, 4 replicate cultures under adult or juvenile food limitation | equation (3), a delay differential equation, discretized with demographic and environmental noise to equation (4) | autocovariances to lag 11, the same first-difference regression summary as the Ricker example, mean{N_t}, mean{N_t} minus median{N_t}, the number of turning points, and a 5-coefficient autoregression on N_(t-12) (up to cubic) and N_(t-2) (up to quadratic) |

For the Ricker illustration, the resulting 95% intervals (3.6 < log(r) < 4.2, 0.10 < sigma_e < 0.55,
9.1 < phi < 11.3) contained the values used to generate the data, but Wood reports his own coverage
simulation found actual coverage of only 0.84-0.87 against a nominal 0.95 at sample size 50, closing
to nominal only for sample sizes of 100-200 (p. 1103): a small-sample calibration caveat stated by
the method's own author, not found later by a critic.

For the blowflies, the full stochastic model (equation 4) fit all four replicates well by Wood's own
chi-squared-based model check (P>0.2 in every case), while the demographic-noise-only simplification
(the same equation with the two environmental noise terms fixed at 1) fit badly in all four (P << 0.002).
The Akaike information criterion favoured the full model by more than 1,800 points in every replicate
(p. 1104). Wood reads this as evidence that Nicholson's blowfly cycles are limit cycles perturbed by
noise, not noise-driven quasicycles, using a further diagnostic (Fig. 4, p. 1104) that overlays 1,500
stability-parameter pairs sampled from the second half of each replicate's MCMC chain on the model's
own stability diagram.

## Exploring l_s: MCMC and the point estimate

The MCMC is a plain random-walk Metropolis sampler on l_s alone (mentioned p. 1103, spelled out in
the Methods Summary's Exploring-l_s-by-MCMC paragraph, p. 1104, checked against the rendered page):
propose a new parameter vector as the current one plus a draw from a symmetric proposal distribution,
and accept it with probability
`min[1, exp(l_s(proposed) - l_s(current))]`. No separate prior-ratio term appears in the acceptance
probability as stated. The chain is used to explore where l_s is large, which is a likelihood-ratio
random walk, not necessarily a Bayesian posterior sampler (it coincides with one under a flat
prior).

The abstract's promised further post-processing step that turns this into pure likelihood-based
inference (p. 1102) is made concrete in the Methods' Maximum-likelihood estimation subsection (PDF
p. 6): near the converged chain's maximum, regress the sampled l_s values quadratically on the chain's
theta values (and their cross-terms and squares), maximize the resulting quadratic to obtain
theta-hat, and read the Hessian of l_s directly off the regression coefficients to obtain an
asymptotic covariance matrix, turning MCMC output into a conventional point estimate plus
covariance, usable for Akaike-information-criterion or generalized-likelihood-ratio comparisons
between models (Methods Summary, Further-information paragraph, p. 1104).

A goodness-of-fit diagnostic recurs through the paper (Methods Summary, p. 1104, Methods, PDF
pp. 5-6, checked against the rendered page): the whitened quadratic form
`(s - mu-hat_theta)^T Sigma-hat_theta^-1 (s - mu-hat_theta)` is compared against a chi-squared
distribution with dim(s) degrees of freedom, both as a single number at
theta-hat and, more informatively, by plotting the simulated replicates' own values of that same
quadratic form against chi-squared quantiles to see where the observed statistics vector falls.

## What changed from the 2026-09-21 stub

The old stub's core method summary (reduce noisy simulator output to phase-insensitive summary
statistics, use repeated simulation at a trial parameter point to get those statistics' mean and
covariance, and build a Gaussian likelihood on the summary statistics from that mean and covariance,
explored by MCMC) is confirmed accurate in every clause against the primary text, including its use
of Wood's own word phase-insensitive, quoted in full above. Nothing in it needed correcting. What this
reading adds, which a secondary description could not supply: the exact equations (2 for the assumed normal form,
the unnumbered log-l_s formula, 5-7 for the asymptotic justification, 1/3/4 for the two ecological
models). That the Gaussian form is asserted by construction and then justified theoretically, not
fitted. The concrete statistics lists for both examples. N_r = 500 for the one case where the paper
states it, and the honest gap that it is not stated for the blowfly fits (the 50,000 figure there is
the chain length, not N_r). That the abstract's further post-processing step is the quadratic
regression of PDF p. 6, not a further MCMC step. Wood's own small-sample coverage caveat. And the
goodness-of-fit chi-squared diagnostic, which the abstract does not mention at all.

## Use in this record

This is the paper the task set out to check: the name and the formal definition of the synthetic
likelihood, one Gaussian likelihood built on a chosen vector of summary statistics, with its mean and
covariance supplied at each trial parameter point by repeated simulation from the model under test,
evaluated at the real data's own statistics vector. That is the same shape as this record's stated aim
of a joint fit across observables with the twin used to compute and factor out biases: the twin plays
the role of Wood's simulator, supplying mu-hat_theta and Sigma-hat_theta at each trial point in the
joint parameter vector, while this record's own statistics vector is built from windowed moments and
their ratios and combinations instead of from autocovariances and autoregression coefficients, the
same construction (equation 2, p. 1103) applied to a different choice of s, which is exactly the
question `identifiability_degeneracies_knobs_uncertainties.md` governs.

Two of Wood's points carry over directly, not by loose analogy. First, his goodness-of-fit
statistic above is the same construction, one level up, as this record's own per-session and
per-window admission tests: a whitened quadratic form of an observed vector against a simulated null,
read against a chi-squared reference. Second, Wood's own coverage caveat (0.84-0.87 achieved against a
nominal 0.95 at n=50, p. 1103) is this record's own closure discipline (inject, fit, recover, and
never trust a quoted interval's nominal coverage without checking it by repetition), stated
independently, by the method's own inventor, in 2010.

What needs more work before it applies here: Wood's theta-hat and its Hessian-based covariance come
from a *local* quadratic regression of l_s near the MCMC chain's maximum, which presumes the chain has
found and mixed around that maximum. This record's stated approach is MLE and profile likelihood
directly, so it needs its own route from l_s (or the equivalent windowed-moment likelihood) to
theta-hat and its covariance, whether by direct optimization or by the same MCMC-plus-quadratic-
regression device if the likelihood surface turns out to have the local, small-scale irregularity
(p. 1103) that made Wood himself reach for MCMC instead of a smooth-function optimizer.
