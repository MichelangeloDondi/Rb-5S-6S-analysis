# mcguirk2004

VERIFIED against the held preprint (scope in `verify_flags`).

## What it measures

Condensates of about 10^5 87Rb atoms in a magnetic trap are brought 5 to 30 um from a substrate and their transverse dipole mode is excited. A surface force shifts the trap frequency through the curvature of its potential. Rb is deposited by pushing condensates into the surface, and a uniform field from external plates, up to 150 V on a capacitor plate, amplifies the adsorbate field so its sign and size can be read from the slope of frequency against applied voltage (Figs. 1 and 3). Even the largest depositions cover at most a few per cent of a monolayer.

## What it finds

On Si and Ti, whose work functions exceed or match Rb's ionization energy, the adatom gives up part of its electron and forms a dipole with its image, about 1 x 10^-29 C m or 3 debye each. The field grows roughly linearly with the number deposited, and the distance dependence, 1/r^2.3 and 1/r^2.0, points to an elongated distribution of dipoles. Verbatim: "We have observed an electric field, produced by a distribution of a large number of adatoms, as large as 10 V/cm at a distance of 10 µm."

On glass the abstract reports, verbatim, a surface "which displays a smaller transitory electrical effect". In the text, verbatim: "The best measurements we make place the dipole moment of Rb on glass to be five to ten times less than Rb on Ti or Si." And on its lifetime, verbatim: "We have observed that, contrary to its behavior on Ti and Si substrates, Rb desorbs from glass with a time constant of ∼ 12 − 24 hours at room temperature".

On cleaning, verbatim: "In fact, applying a halogen UV light source (100 mW/cm2 peaked at 365 nm) to remove adatoms from glass appears to have made the electrical forces much worse, either by ionizing the adsorbates or ionizing other surface contaminants in the process of desorbing atoms."

## Use in this record

The nanofibre extension puts Rydberg atoms within a micrometre of fused silica exposed to rubidium, cold or warm, and one of the things the owner lists as what Síle's programme keeps is an atom-based monitor of adsorbate dynamics on the fibre. This paper is the anchor for the sign of the problem on glass: Rb adsorbates on an insulator polarise weakly and leave within a day at room temperature, so the field is set by a balance of arrival and departure, not by a fixed coverage, and treatments that ionise the adsorbates make it worse. It measured ground-state atoms 5 to 30 um away. A Rydberg atom's polarizability is many orders larger and the fibre's curvature and temperature differ, so the paper gives the mechanism and the time scale, not a field for the fibre. The same group's later measurement on fused silica (`obrecht2007`) puts the dipole per adatom near 3.2 debye, about the size this paper finds on the conductors and an order of magnitude above its bound for BK7. The two differ in glass and in method, and the spread is itself the reading for the fibre: the adsorbate dipole on silica is not known to better than a factor of ten from these two papers.

Related on this shelf: `obrecht2007` (the same group's field mapping over yttrium and fused silica), `sedlacek2016` (Rb on quartz and electron binding), the Hattermann 2012 and Naber 2016 PDFs on the shelf (adsorbate fields on Rydberg atoms, notes pending), `abel2011` (Rydberg electrometry near glass).
