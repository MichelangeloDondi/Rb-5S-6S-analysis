# xin2018

Held. Verified against the PDF, seven pages, on 2026-09-11.

## What the experiment is

Cold 85Rb is loaded from free space into a hollow-core photonic crystal fibre
by an optical dipole trap, and the fibre's guided fields split, reflect and
recombine the atoms' superposition. The trap is 808 nm light at 280 mW, and
the paper states "the calculated trap depth is 0.53 mK, assuming 22-mm dipole
beam waist" (the micron sign is lost in the extraction, and the beam waist is
22 microns).

## The observable is transmission, which is what this record assumed

The paper measures, verbatim, "the transmission Tr of the probe beam" and takes the
column density from it: the optical depth is minus the logarithm of the
transmission, equal to the density times the cross-section times the fibre
length. This record's guided platform rows carry `detection = "absorption"`
and compute an absorbed fraction for exactly this reason, and until now that
choice rested on the general argument that a fibre is read in transmission.
It now rests on the host group's own practice.

## The atom number, and it is the load-bearing number

The paper states, verbatim, "OD = 1 is approximately equivalent to 1.5 x 10^4
atoms for resonant linearly polarized probe beam", and, of its own
performance, that "our fiber atom interferometer only uses about 10^4 atoms". Over the
four-centimetre fibre that is about 250 atoms per millimetre.

`results/platform_twins.csv`'s `hcpcf_cold` row carries 1.13e5 atoms over ten
millimetres against this paper's 1e4 over forty, so the row puts **eleven times
as many atoms in the mode**. That row is tagged ENVELOPE and its density was a
design figure, so this is not a defect in the file. It is the first measurement
the file can be held against.

**The comparison is the atom number and not the linear density**, because
`absorbed_fraction` is twice the excitation rate over the probe flux and the
rate counts the atoms the mode actually drives. A per-millimetre reading of the
same two numbers returns forty-five, which is this ratio times the fibres' own
length ratio of four, and it answers a question the transmission dip does not
ask. Two comparisons are defensible and the paper supports both: against the
1e4 its interferometer uses, the ratio is 11.3. Against the 2.2e4 its own
"OD = 1 is approximately equivalent to 1.5 x 10^4 atoms" implies at the OD it
reports, 5.2.

Scaled to those, the row's absorbed fraction falls from 2.06e-7 to 1.8e-8 or
4.0e-8, and its signal-to-noise per second from 2.52 to 0.222 or 0.488. **That
is a slow measurement, not the absence of one**: a signal-to-noise of ten
arrives in 34 minutes on the first reading and 7 on the second, against 16
seconds at the row's own assumed number.

## What it does not say

Nothing about two-photon spectroscopy, and nothing about a warm fibre. The
warm guided row's atom number comes from a vapour filling the mode and is
untouched by this paper.
