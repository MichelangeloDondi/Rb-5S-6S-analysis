# seo2025

**Read in full 2026-08-03.** Meung Ho Seo, Jongchul Mun, Sang-Bum Lee, Jae
Hoon Lee (Korea Research Institute of Standards and Science, Daejeon),
"Matter-wave lensing of ultracold atomic gases by interaction quenching via
two-photon scattering," *EPJ Quantum Technology* **12**, 13 (2025), DOI
10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00315-8. Received 1 August 2024, accepted 14
January 2025, published online 27 January 2025. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND
4.0). No arXiv preprint is cited in the paper.

**Verbatim abstract:** "Precision quantum sensors using cold atom
interferometers with long interrogation times are often limited by the
ballistic expansion of atomic samples after release from traps, manifesting
by means of laser beam wavefront uncertainties. In this study we utilize
near-resonant light interacting with an ultracold atomic sample for
collective-mode excitation of a 87Rb Bose Einstein condensate (BEC) in a
magnetic trap. The collective motion is initiated after abruptly modifying
the atom-atom interaction energy by reduction of the BEC atom number density
via photon scattering using the two-photon transition from 5S1/2 to 5D5/2.
We show that the two-photon transition can induce matter-wave lensing of the
atomic cloud with minimal center-of-mass perturbation providing an optimal
ultra-cold atomic sample for atom-based quantum sensors such as quantum
gravimeters and accelerometers."

**What it does.** A 87Rb BEC (N = 4e5 atoms, QUIC magnetic trap, 14 Hz
axial / 230 Hz radial) is hit with a short (10 microsecond) pulse of 778 nm
light resonant with the 5S1/2, F=2 -> 5D5/2, F'=4 two-photon transition,
scattering out a controlled fraction of the atoms. Because the two-photon
cross-section is tiny (absorption cross-section 4e-20 cm^4/W, giving an
on-resonance optical depth of ~100 km, versus 330 micrometres for the 780 nm
D2 single-photon line), the beam passes through the whole cloud without
attenuation and removes atoms spatially uniformly. This abruptly lowers the
chemical potential everywhere at once, which projects the BEC onto an
excited collective breathing/quadrupole mode instead of exciting unwanted
center-of-mass (dipole) motion. Timing the release from the trap (t_hold) to
a quarter or an integer multiple of the collective-mode period lets the
cloud's radial or axial width be minimised or "collimated" during
time-of-flight, i.e. matter-wave lensing, without any dynamical control of
the trap fields. The paper contrasts this with a single-photon D2 pulse,
which has a huge cross-section and instead removes atoms asymmetrically
(shading), inducing a dipole center-of-mass motion 8.2-8.6x larger than the
two-photon case. Everything is cross-checked against 3D Gross-Pitaevskii
simulations (Pytalises, Split-Step Fourier) that reproduce the measured
cloud shapes, widths, and oscillation frequencies (22 Hz axial / 420 Hz
radial collective modes) without free parameters beyond a 149 microsecond
release-time offset.

**Key numbers.** Two-photon spectroscopy of the 5D5/2, F=4 Zeeman manifold
(three resolved sigma+/sigma- peaks, 4.45 MHz split by a 4.23 G field) gives
a fitted excited-state linewidth of 550 kHz against the known Lorentzian
linewidth of 667 kHz, a discrepancy attributed to non-linear driving effects
not in their fit model. AC Stark shift from the excitation beam is estimated
below 2*pi*38 kHz, dominated by the far-off-resonant D2 intermediate state
(11 THz detuning). Best measured collimation: expansion rate 1.14
micrometres/ms with the atom number reduced to 1.5e4 (limited by absorption-
imaging sensitivity, not by the method). A simulation at 1/10 the trap
frequency (1.4 Hz / 23 Hz) with 90% atom-number reduction (N = 4e4) predicts
0.245 mm/s expansion, i.e. ~90 pK. Applied to a mobile gravimeter, they
estimate this reduces the Coriolis-force systematic below 2.8 nm/s^2 at
36.7 degrees latitude and could improve state-of-the-art mobile absolute
gravimeter accuracy (currently 30-50 nm/s^2) by an order of magnitude,
alongside the dominant wavefront-distortion term.

**Bridge to this repo: essentially none, checked honestly.** The surface
overlaps are real but do not transfer:

- **Same atom, neighbouring two-photon family, wrong state.** Their
  two-photon transition is 5S1/2 -> 5D5/2 at 778 nm; this programme's is
  5S1/2 -> 6S1/2 at 993.4 nm per photon (half the 6S1/2-5S1/2 gap of
  20132.5 cm^-1), not 778 nm. They are sibling Rb two-photon lines in the
  same family Biraben/Bjorkholm-style Doppler-free spectroscopy has used
  for decades (compare `cao2025`, `hassanin2023`, `amy2017` on the same
  5S-5D line), but not the same transition, and this paper never touches
  6S1/2.
- **Wrong physical regime for self-broadening.** This is a nanokelvin
  quantum-degenerate BEC whose "interaction energy" is the mean-field
  s-wave contact term (chemical potential, Gross-Pitaevskii), not binary
  collisional dephasing. Their peak density (~9 atoms/micrometre^3 =
  9e12 cm^-3) happens to sit in the same numerical range this programme's
  vapour-cell self-broadening bound (`beta_self` ~ 3.5 kHz per 1e12 cm^-3)
  is anchored on, but the mechanism is unrelated -- there is no
  van-der-Waals dipole-dipole dephasing argument to be made from a
  condensate's mean field, and nothing here constrains or corroborates
  beta_self.
- **The 550 kHz vs 667 kHz linewidth fit is a loose methodological
  echo, not usable data.** It is a lineshape-fit-extracts-a-width exercise
  like this programme's, but the fitted "linewidth" is an excited-state
  decay/driving parameter in a three-peak Zeeman-split BEC spectrum with an
  admittedly incomplete non-linear-driving model, not a Doppler- or
  collision-broadened lineshape of the kind THEORY_NOTE or the self-
  broadening series analyse. Nothing here is citable as a linewidth
  measurement in our sense.
- **No magic wavelengths, no light-shift-distribution method, no
  self-broadening coefficient, no compact-clock framing.** The application
  target is atom-interferometric inertial sensing (gravimeters,
  accelerometers), not frequency standards -- adjacent to but distinct from
  the compact-clock literature this repo tracks (`callejo2025`,
  `bandi2025`, `ahern2025`).
