## The system

Seo, Mun, Lee, and Lee (Korea Research Institute of Standards and Science, Daejeon), "Matter-wave lensing of ultracold atomic gases by interaction quenching via two-photon scattering," *EPJ Quantum Technology* **12**, 13 (2025), DOI 10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00315-8. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0).

Abstract: "Precision quantum sensors using cold atom interferometers with long interrogation times are often limited by the ballistic expansion of atomic samples after release from traps, manifesting by means of laser beam wavefront uncertainties. In this study we utilize near-resonant light interacting with an ultracold atomic sample for collective-mode excitation of a 87Rb Bose Einstein condensate (BEC) in a magnetic trap. The collective motion is initiated after abruptly modifying the atom-atom interaction energy by reduction of the BEC atom number density via photon scattering using the two-photon transition from 5S1/2 to 5D5/2. We show that the two-photon transition can induce matter-wave lensing of the atomic cloud with minimal center-of-mass perturbation providing an optimal ultra-cold atomic sample for atom-based quantum sensors such as quantum gravimeters and accelerometers."

## The method

A 87Rb BEC (N = 4e5 atoms, QUIC magnetic trap, 14 Hz axial / 230 Hz radial) is hit with a 10 microsecond pulse of 778 nm light resonant with the 5S1/2, F=2 -> 5D5/2, F'=4 two-photon transition, removing a controlled fraction of atoms. The two-photon absorption cross-section (4e-20 cm^4/W, an on-resonance optical depth of about 100 km, against 330 micrometres for the 780 nm D2 line) is small enough that the beam passes through the cloud without attenuation, removing atoms uniformly rather than asymmetrically as a single-photon D2 pulse does. The uniform density reduction lowers the chemical potential everywhere at once, projecting the BEC onto a collective breathing/quadrupole mode rather than a center-of-mass dipole mode. Released at a quarter or an integer multiple of the collective-mode period, the cloud's width during time-of-flight is minimized, giving matter-wave lensing without dynamical control of the trap fields. A single-photon D2 pulse instead induces dipole motion 8.2 to 8.6 times larger. Three-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii simulations (Pytalises, split-step Fourier) reproduce the measured cloud shapes, widths, and oscillation frequencies (22 Hz axial / 420 Hz radial) with no free parameter beyond a 149 microsecond release-time offset.

## The numbers

Two-photon spectroscopy of the 5D5/2, F=4 Zeeman manifold (three resolved sigma+/sigma- peaks, 4.45 MHz split by a 4.23 G field) gives a fitted excited-state linewidth of 550 kHz against the known Lorentzian linewidth of 667 kHz, a discrepancy attributed to non-linear driving effects not in the fit model. The AC Stark shift from the excitation beam is below 2*pi*38 kHz, dominated by the far-off-resonant D2 intermediate state (11 THz detuning). The best measured collimation gives an expansion rate of 1.14 micrometres/ms with the atom number reduced to 1.5e4, limited by absorption-imaging sensitivity. A simulation at one-tenth the trap frequency (1.4 Hz / 23 Hz) with 90% atom-number reduction (N = 4e4) predicts 0.245 mm/s expansion, about 90 pK. Applied to a mobile gravimeter, this is estimated to reduce the Coriolis-force systematic below 2.8 nm/s^2 at 36.7 degrees latitude, potentially improving mobile absolute gravimeter accuracy (currently 30-50 nm/s^2) by an order of magnitude alongside the dominant wavefront-distortion term.

## Use in this record

No quantity in this record rests on this paper. Its two-photon transition is 5S1/2 -> 5D5/2 at 778 nm in a nanokelvin BEC governed by mean-field (Gross-Pitaevskii) dynamics, distinct from the 5S1/2 -> 6S1/2 transition at 993.4 nm per photon in a thermal vapor cell governed by binary collisional dephasing. The peak BEC density (about 9e12 cm^-3) falls in the same numerical range as this record's self-broadening bound, but a condensate's mean-field interaction energy is not a van der Waals dephasing mechanism, so the coincidence constrains nothing. The fitted 550 kHz and 667 kHz linewidths describe an excited-state decay parameter in a Zeeman-split spectrum, not a collision-broadened lineshape, and are not comparable in the sense used in this record.
