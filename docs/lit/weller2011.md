# weller2011

Held. Verified against the PDF.

## The system

Absolute absorption spectroscopy on the rubidium D1 line, to 170 °C and
3×10¹⁴ cm⁻³, including resonant dipole-dipole interactions.

## The numbers

The self-broadening coefficient for the rubidium D1 line is
β/2π = (0.69 ± 0.04) × 10⁻⁷ Hz cm³, in agreement with the theoretical
prediction of 0.73 × 10⁻⁷ Hz cm³. Spectra match theory to 0.1% up to
3×10¹⁴ cm⁻³.

Their table gives theory alongside measurement for three alkalis, all
values ×10⁻⁷ Hz cm³:

| species/line | theory | measured |
|---|---|---|
| Rb D1 | 0.73 | 0.69 ± 0.04 |
| Rb D2 | 1.03 | 1.10 ± 0.17 |
| Na D1 | 0.51 | 0.49 ± 0.07 |
| Cs D1 | 0.83 | 0.75 ± 0.11 |

Theory agrees with measurement to a few percent across the series.

The same fit returns the natural linewidth as its intercept:
Γ₀/2π = (5.7 ± 0.7) MHz, against the accepted 5.75 MHz for the Rb D1 line.

## Validity

The measured coefficient is resonant dipole-dipole on an allowed
transition. The ground state is one of the two coupled levels, so every
ground-state atom acts as a resonant perturber, giving the largest possible
broadening of this kind.

## Use in this record

The rubidium 5S-6S transition studied in this programme has no resonant
dipole-dipole channel, since both states are S states, so its
self-broadening coefficient should sit below Weller et al.'s value and
follow van der Waals scaling instead. On that basis, 0.69 × 10⁻⁷ Hz cm³
(69 kHz per 10¹² cm⁻³) is an upper bound rather than a direct estimate for
the 5S-6S coefficient, placing the archival bound of 0.2-0.4 MHz per
10¹² cm⁻³ roughly 15 to 30 times looser than the kHz-per-10¹² cm⁻³ scale
otherwise expected. The same construction, recovering the natural linewidth
from the intercept of a linewidth-vs-density fit, is used in this programme
for the self-broadening coefficient.
