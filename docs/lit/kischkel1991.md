## Identification, corrected against the working hypothesis

This is not a cesium paper and not a pressure-broadening or pressure-shift
study, though the literature-intake note that flagged it guessed both.
Kischkel, Baumann and Kümmel, at the Physikalisches Institut in Tübingen, investigate
neutral YTTERBIUM (Z=70), a lanthanide, not an alkali. The apparatus is a
retroreflected single-mode cw ring dye laser (Coherent CR 699-21, Stilbene
3) driving Doppler-free two-photon absorption inside a heated thermionic
diode (about 1000 K, a few grams of metallic Yb plus 8 hPa neon buffer gas),
with a Faraday rotator to block optical feedback and lock-in detection at
78.5 Hz. The observable throughout is atomic structure: magnetic dipole (A)
and electric quadrupole (B) hyperfine constants and isotope shifts across
the five even and two odd stable Yb isotopes, extracted from Doppler-free
line positions and cross-checked with King plots. The companion paper
[kischkel1992](kischkel1992.md) is the direct sequel, extending the same
apparatus and method from the 6s7d and 6s8d states studied here to the 6s9d
and 6s10d states.

## What it does

Two-photon excitation from the 4f¹⁴6s² ¹S₀ ground state reaches the J=2
members of the 4f¹⁴6s7d, 4f¹⁴6s8d, 4f¹⁴6p², 4f¹³6s²6p and 4f¹³5d6s6p
configurations, between 44760 and 47342 cm⁻¹ (wavelengths 421 to 451 nm,
their Table 1). Peak positions, obtained by fitting Lorentzian curves of
equal width to the recorded spectra (a Doppler-broadened Gaussian
background could be omitted without loss of accuracy), give the A and B
hyperfine constants of the two odd isotopes ¹⁷¹Yb and ¹⁷³Yb for each
transition (Table 2) and the isotope shifts among all five even isotopes
plus the two odd ones (Table 3). Second-order hyperfine corrections, needed
because several of these J=2 levels sit close in energy to other J states of
the same configuration, are read from King-plot deviations of the odd
isotopes and cross-checked against an ab initio effective-tensor-operator
calculation (Sandars and Beck 1965) built on MCDF radial hyperfine
integrals. Section 6 finds an approximately 19 percent admixture of
4f¹⁴6p² ¹D₂ in the 4f¹⁴6s7d ¹D₂ state, and the 4f¹³6s²6p (5/2,3/2)₂ level
close to pure jj coupling.

## Measured coefficients

There is no pressure-broadening or pressure-shift coefficient of any kind
in this paper. Every number in Tables 2 and 3 is a hyperfine splitting
constant or an isotope shift, both quoted in MHz, and neither is a
collisional rate. Two representative entries, from Table 2: for
4f¹⁴6s7d ³D₂, A(¹⁷¹Yb) = 2622(7) MHz and A(¹⁷³Yb) = -821(2) MHz. From Table
3: the isotope shift δν(172-176) for that same state is -736(15) MHz. The
one place the word "broadening" appears at all is a passing remark in
Section 2, that the roughly 50 MHz observed linewidth (larger than the
natural width) "might be due to pressure broadening in the interaction
region of the thermionic diode (Grynberg and Cagnac 1977)". No broadening
rate, no density dependence and no coefficient of any kind is attached to
that remark. It names a plausible cause for an instrumental linewidth
floor, it does not measure anything.

## BRIDGES

None of the physics this repository needs is here. Kischkel 1991 and 1992
were checked for a measured alkali excited-S-state collisional coefficient,
something like a Cs 6S-8S self-broadening or self-shift rate, that could sit
beside [zameroski2014](zameroski2014.md) in the beta_self calibration
comparison docs/LITERATURE.md section 2 already builds. Neither paper
carries one. Ytterbium is not an alkali, the states studied are even-parity
6s(n)d and 6p² levels reached by two-photon excitation from an S ground
state, not an nS to n'S pair, and the entire content of both papers is
hyperfine structure and isotope shift, not collisional broadening or shift
at any pressure. The calibration comparison this repository already has
stands unchanged: [zameroski2014](zameroski2014.md) remains the only
measured self-broadening rate for an nS state in an alkali (Rb 5S to 7S,
129 ± 11 kHz/mTorr, 5.39 ± 0.46 kHz per 10¹² cm⁻³), and
[lewis1980](lewis1980.md) remains the confirmation, read from a 71-page
review, that no van der Waals-type nS-n'S alkali self-broadening
coefficient appears anywhere in that survey either.

A second comparison paper, "true2021", was named to sit beside zameroski2014
in that same list. No file, citation or bibliographic record for a
"true2021" exists anywhere in this repository: not in docs/lit, not in
docs/LITERATURE.md, not in docs/references.bib, and not anywhere else a
full-repository search reaches. Nothing in this note should be read as
confirming that citation. If it names a real, relevant paper, supplying and
verifying it is a separate literature-intake task, one these two Kischkel
notes cannot do in its place.

One thread is worth keeping, and it is thin. Kischkel's Doppler-free
two-photon apparatus (retroreflected single-mode dye laser, Faraday
rotator against optical feedback, a Doppler-free Lorentzian fit against a
Doppler-broadened Gaussian background, with Grynberg and Cagnac 1977 cited
for that lineshape decomposition) belongs to the same experimental
tradition that [biraben1979](biraben1979.md) and
[borde1976](borde1976.md) already anchor in this repository's
transit-time section, just run on Yb hyperfine structure instead of a
transit-time lineshape. That is a family resemblance in apparatus, not a
physics bridge, and it adds nothing this repository does not already have
directly from Biraben and Bordé.
