## What the first page says

Wiley StatsRef: Statistics Reference Online, copyright 2014 to 2015 John Wiley & Sons, DOI
10.1002/9781118445112.stat00570.pub2, an update of the 2014 entry by the same author. The abstract defines L-moments
as the expected values of certain linear combinations of the order statistics of a random sample, multiplied by
scalar constants, and lists the advantages it claims over ordinary moments: they exist when the distribution has a
finite mean, they determine the distribution uniquely, their sample ratios are bounded as the population ones are,
their sampling distributions are better approximated asymptotically, and they are less sensitive to outlying values.
