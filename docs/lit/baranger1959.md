# baranger1959

REPORTED. Not held: behind the American Physical Society's Physical Review Archive paywall, no
open-access or preprint copy located by search on 2026-09-21. Everything below is second-hand.

## What is reported about it

Phys. Rev. **115**, 521-525 (1959), DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.115.521, "Electric Field Distributions in
an Ionized Gas" (a Part I: a sequel, "Electric Field Distributions in an Ionized Gas. II", Phys.
Rev. 118, 626, followed in 1960 and is not covered here). A search-engine summary describes
the paper as presenting "a method for improving systematically the Holtsmark distribution based on
a cluster-type expansion that takes into account increasing orders of correlation, and applies it
to the calculation of the distribution of the high-frequency component of the electric field in an
ionized gas in thermal equilibrium," and states it "was the first theory to go beyond the Holtsmark
limit and was based on a cluster expansion similar to that of Ursell and Mayer" (paraphrased from
search results, not a verbatim quotation of the paper).

The physical setting this paper addresses, Stark broadening of a spectral line by the fluctuating
electric microfield each emitting/absorbing ion or atom experiences from its neighbours in a
plasma, is a textbook case in which a line's broadening and asymmetry are a direct map of a
field-strength probability distribution, exactly the structure delone1980 (already held on this
record's shelf, and read in full: see `docs/lit/delone1980.md`) generalizes to an arbitrary field
distribution P(F) and an F^k multiphoton weight.

## Use in this record

Named in the strategy document as the paper where "Baranger and Mozer (1959) read a field
distribution off a line," and it is the specific, named origin of the electric-microfield-
distribution problem in plasma spectroscopy that delone1980's later review (already fully read and
held) generalizes and that this record's own light-shift-distribution problem is a different
physical instance of: there, a fluctuating interparticle electric field broadens a line through the
(linear or quadratic) Stark effect. Here, a spatially varying laser intensity broadens a line
through the AC-Stark effect. Both are cases where "the lineshape is a probability map of a field
distribution," and this paper is the earliest calculation, in this literature search, of such a
distribution from first principles (a cluster expansion of interparticle correlations), not
assumed or fitted. Worth holding in full mainly for completeness of the citation chain
(Baranger & Mozer 1959 to Delone 1980's generalization to this record's own light-shift case), not
because its own plasma-physics cluster-expansion machinery is directly reusable here.
