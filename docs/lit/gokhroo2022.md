# gokhroo2022

Held. Read in full. DOI verified against Crossref.

## The system

Cold 87Rb in a magneto-optical trap positioned around an optical nanofibre, driving 5S1/2 (F=2) -> 6S1/2 (F''=2) at 993 nm, the same transition and wavelength used in this record. An M Squared SolsTis Ti:Sapphire laser, quoted at sub-MHz linewidth, provides the drive, referenced against a counter-propagating 993 nm vapour cell for a Doppler-free frequency reference.

## The measurement

The transmission profile shows a two-peak structure with a central dip, attributed at the hypothesis level to resonance-scattering-induced pushing of atoms out of the high-intensity region near the fibre ("We speculate that ... resonance scattering induced pushing ... becomes the dominant effect"). Dip positions are compared with dressed-state resonances, but no fitted lineshape model is given, and Casimir-Polder, van der Waals, or surface-potential terms do not appear in the paper. The published title reads "light fields." The arXiv preprint title reads "electric fields."

## Use in this record

No subsequent publication models the proposed pushing-dip mechanism, including by the paper's own theory co-authors, whose nanofibre force-theory work predates it. Citing works address unrelated or different nanofibre phenomena: an ONF state-insensitive trap for strontium, Rydberg excitation near a nanofibre ([Vylegzhanin et al. 2023](vylegzhanin2023.md)), and a fictitious-field trap ([Vylegzhanin et al. 2025](vylegzhanin2025.md)). A later review article by the group (`li2024perspective`) cites this paper once, for the capability of observing the transition, not the dip. The dip is a density-depletion effect, atoms pushed out of the bright region of the field, distinct from a distribution of light shifts across an inhomogeneous field. A quantitative model of it needs the force and density dynamics in addition to any shift-distribution treatment.
