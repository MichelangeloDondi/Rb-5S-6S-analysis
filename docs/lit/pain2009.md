## What it does

A transition array is a strength-weighted distribution of line energies (Eqs. 1-2), convolved with a broadening profile. Its moments are written as traces over the configuration (Eq. 4), for which Bauche-Arnoult and co-workers derived analytic formulae up to the third order. The paper works with the reduced centred moments alpha_n (Eq. 5): the skewness alpha_3 and the kurtosis alpha_4, the latter compared with 3 for a Gaussian. It tests three families that carry them. Gram-Charlier (Eqs. 6-9) can go negative. The normal inverse Gaussian (Eq. 10) takes its four parameters directly from the mean, width, skewness and kurtosis. The generalized Gaussian's exponent is fixed by the kurtosis (Eq. 14 is a fit for the root). The skewness grows about as Z^6 along an isoelectronic sequence, following Bauche et al.

Section 2.4 makes the point that matters here. Two profiles sharing the first four moments still give different spectra (Fig. 6), which is the signature of the moments above the fourth, because fixing the low moments fixes the higher ones for each chosen family. Table 2 lists alpha_k up to order 14 for one array (Br XII 3d6 to 3d5 4p) from Cowan's code against the families. Section 4 explains why the analytic route stops: the number of radial-integral products grows combinatorially with the order (Eqs. 15-16), and the fourth moment already needs one- to ten-electron operators (Eq. 17).

## Use in this record

- A precedent inside atomic spectroscopy for treating a feature's shape through its moments, with the moments computed from theory and compared with the data through a model profile. This record's closed-form ramp moments play the same role for one term of the line.
- A caution on "to mu_12": in this field the first four moments are usually enough for the global shape, and the higher ones are either fixed by the chosen family or too costly to compute. They are measurable in principle and show up as family-to-family differences. That is the question this record's sensitivity matrix answers numerically: which orders add a direction.

## Limits

- The direction of inference is the forward one: moments from structure calculations make a line shape. Moments are not fitted to data to extract parameters.
